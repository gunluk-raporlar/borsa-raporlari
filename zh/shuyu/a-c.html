Output Gap, defined as the difference between actual output and potential output in an economy, is an important monetary policy variable for evaluating price pressures. Inflation targeting regimes, central banks shape policies based on future inflation expectations rather than current inflation. Accordingly, where the output gap is positive, it is estimated that the economy is operating above capacity due to excess demand, which could cause demand-pull inflationary pressures. Conversely, where the output gap is negative, it is estimated that there is excess supply or idle capacity due to weak demand in the economy, which could lead to a decrease in inflation.
