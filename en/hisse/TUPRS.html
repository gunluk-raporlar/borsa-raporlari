🌍 Macroeconomic Evaluation

Refinery margins are the primary derivative of global product supply-demand balance: capacity exits in Europe and the low trajectory of intermediate distillate stocks support crack spreads; Tüpraş's high complexity coefficient maximizes benefit from this environment. The decline in oil prices reduces raw material costs while product prices remain resilient, working in favor of margins; the energy sector's intraday rise approaching 4.9% prices in this expectation. USD/TL's level at 48.83 provides strong natural protection for the company, whose revenues are effectively dollar-based. Although the decline in inflation to 31.5% and interest rate cut expectations change the financial income composition of the company with net cash position, operating margins continue to be the main determinant. The stability of domestic fuel demand and growth in aviation fuel maintain high capacity utilization. Main risks; sharp normalization in global crack spreads, oil price shock and broker sell recommendations creating short-term price pressure.

🧾 Balance Sheet Evaluation

Tüpraş increased its sales by 42.8% and net profit by 319.6% in the first half of 2026; in the second quarter, it recorded 404.5 billion TL in sales (+57.7%) and 46.5 billion TL in net profit (+292.9), fully reflecting the strong trend in refinery margins. Quarterly net margin was 11.5%, far above the 2.6% in the first half of last year; stock gains and expanding crack spreads are driving profitability. The company, which generated 1.18 trillion TL in sales and 73.5 billion TL in net profit over the last four quarters, has a market value of ~792 billion TL; with P/E ~10.8x, P/B ~1.7x and EV/EBITDA ~5.9x, it trades below global refinery thresholds. Balance sheet structure is strong: financial debt/equity is low at 14.7% and the company has ~131 billion TL in net cash position; this maintains high dividend capacity even in scenarios where margins normalize. ROE is 15.9% with a recovery trend; 3.4% dividend yield is supported by a regular distribution policy. The main debate is whether we are at the peak of the refinery cycle; as part of the Q2 profit is from stock gains, P/E should be read somewhat higher on a normalized profit basis. In summary, Tüpraş is the clearest example of a value+growth profile with its strong cash position, reasonable multiples and high dividend capacity; the main risk is the cyclical normalization of margins.
