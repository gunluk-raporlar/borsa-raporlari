Stock Exchange Dictionary

Basic concepts covered in Borsa Okulu lessons — you can filter by searching. For the complete dictionary of economy and finance, see Terms & Definitions page.

A

Overbought / oversold

Regions where oscillators (RSI, stochastic, Wave Trend) have risen too much (overbought) or fallen too much (oversold) by decoupling from the price's momentum. It can be a reversal signal but may stay in the trend for a long time.

B

Base price

It is the reference price used to calculate a stock's daily percentage change; it is determined based on the previous day's closing price. Daily upper and lower price limits are also derived from the base price.

Rights issue (paid-in capital increase)

It is a capital increase in which existing shareholders can buy new shares by paying for them in proportion to their shareholding; unlike a bonus issue, the investor pays for the new shares.

Bonus issue (free capital increase)

It is when a company adds its internal resources to capital and gives its existing shareholders new shares free of charge. In a stock that receives bonus shares, the price adjusts proportionally to the share increase; the total value does not change.

Benchmark

The index against which portfolio performance is compared (e.g., XU100). A portfolio is considered weak if it's new and even when profitable, it underperforms the index.

BETA (coefficient)

It measures the sensitivity of a stock's return to the index. If beta is 1, the stock moves with the index; above 1 means sharper movement than the index, below 1 means calmer movement. It is one of the fundamental measures of portfolio risk.

Earnings season

The periods of January-April-July-October when companies release their quarterly financial reports. Major stock movements occur on balance sheet days.

BIST 30 and BIST 100

Indexes consisting of the top 30/100 stocks by market value and volume on Borsa Istanbul. When news says 'the index rose', it generally refers to XU100.

Bull and bear market

A long-term rising period is a 'bull' market, while a falling period is a 'bear' market. The names come from the bull's upward thrust and the bear's downward strike.

D

Support and Resistance

The area where buying pressure emerges as prices fall is 'support', while the area where selling pressure emerges as prices rise is 'resistance'. Broken resistance can become support, and broken support can become resistance.

E

EMA (Exponential Moving Average)

A moving average that gives more weight to recent prices. The technical scan on this site looks at 5-8-13-21 (short), 34-55 (medium), 89-144 (long) EMA sequences; if short averages are above long ones, it's called an 'AL' sequence.

Order types

PI: market order execution, L: limit order, IOC: cancel if not filled, FOK: fill or kill.

Inflation data (CPI)

Consumer inflation announced by TÜİK at the beginning of each month. Higher than expected inflation can increase interest rate expectations and pressure the market.

EURIBOR

Euro Interbank Offered Rate. It is the reference interest rate that banks in the euro zone use when lending short-term to each other; it forms the basis for pricing euro loans and derivative products. It closely tracks the European Central Bank's policy rate.

F

Fibonacci retracement

The 23.6 / 38.2 / 50 / 61.8 pullback levels of a movement. Traders watch these areas as potential support/resistance.

H

Volume

The amount of lots/shares traded in a specific period. Price movement supported by volume is important for signal strength.

Initial public offering (IPO)

It is the first time a company's shares are offered for sale to investors; the first sale is called an 'initial public offering' (IPO). After the offering, the company's shares begin trading on the stock exchange.

Free float ratio

It is the ratio of a company's shares that freely change hands on the stock exchange to its total shares. Thresholds vary by share type; in shares with a low ratio, liquidity is weak and the price moves sharply with few orders.

K

P/E ratio (Price/Earnings)

The ratio of stock price to earnings per share. Roughly indicates how many years it takes for a company to recoup its profits; compared to industry average.

Leverage

Increasing position size with debt (or derivatives). Amplifies both gains and losses proportionally; high risk for beginners.

L

LIBOR

London Interbank Offered Rate. For many years it was the reference rate for global loans; after manipulation scandals, it was gradually retired between 2021 and 2023, and transaction-based rates such as SOFR (US) and €STR (euro zone) took its place.

Liquidity

How easily a stock can be bought and sold without affecting its price. In illiquid stocks, the bid-ask spread is wide.

Lot and share

The trading unit on the stock exchange is 'lot'; 1 lot = 1 share. Orders are placed on a lot basis.

M

MACD

Momentum indicator calculated from the difference between two moving averages. When the MACD line crosses above the signal line, it's interpreted as positive; when below, as negative.

O

Average cost

It is the average of purchases of a share at different prices, weighted by the number of lots. It shows the breakeven price of the position; it is calculated as total amount / total lots.

P

P/B

Price-to-book ratio. Below 1 indicates that market value is below the balance sheet equity; it is used in sector comparisons of cheapness-expensive­ness.

Market depth

It is the distribution of order volumes placed at price levels on the buy and sell sides of a stock. In a stock with good depth, large orders are met without sharply moving the price.

Market value

Number of shares × share price. Shows the total size of the company on the stock exchange; BIST 30 consists of the stocks with the highest market value and trading volume.

R

Regression channel

Bands drawn above and below the price's trend axis over a specific period. The 'channel position %' on the site shows where the price is within these bands: 100% at the upper band, 0% at the lower band.

Repo / Reverse Repo

Repo (repurchase agreement) is the sale of a security with a commitment to repurchase it; reverse repo is the same transaction viewed from the counterparty's side. TCMB manages interbank liquidity through repo auctions; the repo rate is a mirror of short-term interest rate expectations.

RSI (Relative Strength Index)

An oscillator between 0-100; values above 70 are considered 'overbought', below 30 'oversold'. It measures the speed/strength of price movement, not a standalone buy/sell signal.

S

Short (short position)

Selling first then buying back later to profit from falling prices. Done in futures markets; limited in stock markets.

Stop-loss

An automatic sell level set to limit losses. Setting the stop level when opening a position is fundamental risk management.

T

Settlement (T+2)

The process by which a trade is actually finalized for the parties; in Turkey it is carried out by Takasbank. The T+2 rule applies in the Borsa İstanbul equity market: on a sale, the money becomes available two business days after the trade date.

CBRT policy rate

The base interest rate updated by the Central Bank at specific monthly meetings. Interest rate cuts are generally seen as positive for the stock market, while increases are viewed negatively.

Dividend

The distribution of a company's profits to shareholders. When a cash dividend is paid, the stock price opens lower by the amount of the dividend (dividend adjustment).

Trend and channel

A period where both troughs and peaks are rising is an uptrend. Bands drawn parallel to the trend axis form a 'channel'.

V

Virman

It is the transfer of assets from one account to another. The most common case in the stock market is the transfer of shares from the old brokerage firm to the new one when changing brokers.

Volatility

The price fluctuation. High volatility = wide swings = both opportunity and risk. Usually measured by percentage standard deviation.

W

Wave Trend (indicator)

An oscillator showing the short-term wave (cycle) position of the price. It appears on the site with overbought/oversold labels like 'SELL AT TOP' / 'BUY AT BOTTOM'.

Y

YFD

In its most common usage, Investment Fund Auditor (SPK legislation): the institution that audits the financial statements of investment funds and their compliance with legislation. This abbreviation is encountered in fund announcements on KAP and in BES content.

Ç

Diversification

Distributing investment across different stocks/sectors/asset classes. It reduces single-stock risk; the demo portfolio on the site is an example of diversification equally distributed to BIST 30.

İ

Trading hours and session

Borsa Istanbul main market is open on weekdays 10:00-18:00 (excluding lunch break). The technical scan on the site is updated every 30 minutes during these hours.
