1. Global Macroeconomic Outlook

US: Resilient Growth, Sticky Inflation

The US economy appears to have grown 3.4% quarter-on-quarter in the third quarter of 2026, clearly exceeding the 1.5% market expectation. Annual growth stands at 2.1%; this points to momentum above potential growth. However, the composition of this resilience is striking: consumer spending has doubled from the previous 1.6% to 3.2% quarter-on-quarter. This picture implies that the demand side has not yet broken, and therefore the final mile of disinflation will be difficult.

The picture on the inflation front is complex. Annual headline inflation at 3.7% is above the 3.6% expectation and has risen from the previous 3.4%; the monthly increase of 0.4% also exceeded the 0.1% expectation. Annual core inflation at 2.6% is slightly above expectations. On the PCE side, the annual 3.6% and monthly 0.5% increases show that we are still far from the Fed's 2% target. Although annual PPI has declined from the previous 5.6% to 4.9%, it reminds us that pipeline pressures persist.

Labor Market and the Fed's Dilemma

The labor market is giving signals of moderate cooling: unemployment at 4.1%, U-6 broad unemployment has risen to 7.9%, wage growth at 3.1% annually is below expectations. Nevertheless, non-farm payrolls at 165 thousand have more than doubled the 80 thousand expectation; JOLTs job openings at 7,335 million are above expectations. This dual picture — strong employment growth but slowing wages — narrows the Fed's room for maneuver. The policy rate is fixed at 4.0%; the real rate is nearly neutral at approximately +0.30 points. The Fed's rate projections are 3.6% for the first year and 3.4% for the second year — meaning market pricing points to a gradual easing path, but this path may be delayed due to the stubborn course of inflation.

Euro Area: On the Brink of Stagflation

In the Euro Area the picture is much more fragile. Although quarterly growth at 0.4% exceeded expectations, industrial production underlying the 1.3% annual growth is worrying: -0.5% monthly, -1.1% annually. Retail sales at -0.7% monthly are well below expectations. Worst of all, annual inflation has risen to 4.0% and PPI has surged to 11.1% annually — this paints a picture of a cost shock re-triggered by energy costs. The policy rate is fixed at 2.65%; the real rate is deep in negative territory at approximately 4.8 points. Consumer confidence continues to deteriorate at -17.9 points. This combination — negative real rates, rising PPI, weak industry — is a classic stagflation warning.

Global Liquidity and Risk Appetite

Global liquidity conditions are tightening. The US 30-year bond yield has risen to 5.43% and the 5-year to 4.95%; this move at the long end of the yield curve shows that the term premium is increasing and the market is pricing long-term inflation/cost risks. VIX has risen, albeit limited, to 18.70; DXY at 103.19 confirms that the dollar is trading strong. This environment creates an unfavorable ground for capital flows to emerging markets.

2. Turkey's Macroeconomic Outlook

Inflation: The Stage Where Disinflation Slows

In Türkiye, annual CPI at 29.77% is slightly above the expectation (29.56%) and nearly flat from the previous 29.73%. Monthly inflation at 2.58% exceeded the 2.4% expectation; this shows that we are in the "middle bend" stage of the disinflation process, not the "final mile". Annual core inflation at 28.3% is below the headline and has declined from the previous 28.7% — this is a positive signal. Annual PPI at 27.41% is below CPI; the monthly increase of 1.65% has slowed from the previous 2.07%. PPI running below CPI indicates that cost pressures have eased somewhat, but the monthly CPI increase of 2.58% remains high due to rigidity in services inflation.

Monetary Policy: Cautious Easing

The TCMB cut its policy rate from 37% to 36%; the real interest rate is clearly positive at approximately +4.80 points. This shows that Türkiye is in a rare position on a global scale: while the real interest rate is close to neutral in the US and deeply negative in the Euro Area, a strongly positive real interest rate is maintained in Türkiye. This positive real interest rate serves as a critical anchor for exchange rate stability and disinflation; however, it also weighs on domestic demand and credit costs. The TCMB's 100 basis point cut implies that a "cautious easing" cycle has begun despite the flat course of inflation.

Growth and Labor

Growth accelerated to 1.1% quarter-on-quarter from the previous 0.3%; annual growth of 2.3% is below the 2.9% expectation and down from the previous 2.6%. This is a divergence indicating a loss of momentum on an annual basis but a recovery on a quarterly basis. Industrial production showed a strong recovery at 2.1% month-on-month from the previous -1.0%; annual 2.61%. Unemployment was 8.5%, and the participation rate rose slightly to 52.7%. Capacity utilization is on a moderate rise at 74.5%. However, confidence indicators are deteriorating: consumer confidence fell sharply from 91.9 to 84.8, the economic expectation index declined from 101.3 to 97.3, and business confidence from 102 to 99.8. This signals fragility on the demand side of growth.

3. Domestic Demand, Credit and Liquidity

Interest Rate-Credit Channel

Despite the policy rate being cut to 36%, the deposit rate stands at 42.04%, clearly above the policy rate; this shows that banks' funding costs and deposit competition continue. The consumer loan rate, meanwhile, rose to 66.46% from the previous 64.74% — that is, while the policy rate was cut, the loan rate increased. This divergence is critical: the transmission channel from the policy rate to credit costs is clogged. Banks are not passing the policy cut through to loan rates due to risk premiums and funding costs; in fact, they are moving in the opposite direction.

Credit Growth and Money Supply

Although credit growth slowed slightly to 35.29% annually from the previous 36.32%, it is still high. M3 money supply declined to 26.87% annually from the previous 28.85% — monetary expansion is slowing but remains above inflation. This combination — high credit growth, high deposit rates, rising consumer loan rates — shows that domestic demand cannot be fully cooled through the interest rate channel and that the credit channel is partially clogged. Retail sales are nearly stagnant at 0.1% month-on-month and slowed to 7.7% annually from the previous 10.1% — domestic demand is cooling but there is not yet a sharp contraction.

4. Global Risk Appetite and Interest Rate Expectations

Market Indicators

VIX rose 6.92% to 18.70 from the previous 17.49; this shows that risk appetite has deteriorated slightly but remains far from panic territory. DXY is at 103.19, with the dollar strong. US bond yields are rising at the long end: 30-year at 5.43%, 5-year at 4.95%. The shape of the yield curve — short-term at 4.13%, long-term at 5.43% — points to a steepening curve; this generally indicates that fiscal policy risks and inflation expectations are being priced in. Although the Fed's rate projections (3.6% in the first year, 3.4% in the second year) imply gradual easing, the rise in long-term yields shows that the market does not fully trust this path.

Reflection on BIST

The limited deterioration in global risk appetite and the strong course of the dollar are restricting capital flows to emerging markets. However, BIST 100 rising 36% to 11.811.4.8 and delivering a 1.50% return in dollar terms shows that Türkiye-specific factors (positive real interest rate, disinflation expectation) are partially resisting the global headwind. Nevertheless, the upward trend in VIX and the increase in US yields create a ceiling that limits BIST's upside potential.

5. Budget and External Balance

Budget: Marked Deterioration

The budget balance turned to a sharp deficit of -532 billion TL in September 2026 from the previous surplus of +12.9 billion TL. Although this is a single-month deviation, its magnitude is striking; it shows that budget discipline is being strained. The quality of financing becomes critical so that the budget deficit does not conflict with monetary policy.

External Balance: Current Account Surplus, Trade Deficit

The current account balance posted a surplus of 4,053 billion dollars in August 2026, exceeding the 3.6 billion dollar expectation and showing a marked improvement from the previous 0,036 billion dollars. This is a positive picture formed with the contribution of tourism revenues (25.53 billion dollars in Q3) and goods trade dynamics. However, the trade balance deteriorated to -6.7 billion dollars in October from the previous -5,232 billion dollars; exports were 25.9 billion dollars and imports 32.58 billion dollars. The flat course of exports (previous 25,976 billion dollars) and the rise in imports show that the foreign trade deficit is widening. Foreign exchange reserves rose slightly to 57.02 billion dollars (previous 56.09 billion dollars) — this shows that external financing fragility has decreased, albeit limitedly, but the reserve level is still modest for a buffer function.

6. Energy, Commodities, Foreign Exchange and Bond Markets

Energy and Commodities

Brent crude rose 3.23% to 95.76 dollars/barrel; WTI increased 2.51% to 88.70 dollars. This shows that energy costs are rising again and creating imported input cost pressure for net energy importer countries such as Türkiye. Gold, meanwhile, fell sharply 3.03% to 3.879.20 dollars/ounce; gram gold declined 3.28% to 6.246.36 TL. This decline in gold is consistent with the strong dollar and rising real returns; however, it may also indicate that geopolitical risks have decreased.

FX and Bonds

USD/TRY declined slightly to 50.08 (-0.25%), EUR/TRY fell 0.69% to 55.73. The flat-to-declining trend of the exchange rate shows that the positive real interest rate and the reserve increase are supporting the TL. The real effective exchange rate rose to 107.76 from the previous 105.1 — the TL is appreciating in real terms; while this supports disinflation, it limits export competitiveness. The US 30-year bond at 5.43% confirms that global long-term interest rates are rising; this puts upward pressure on Türkiye's external borrowing costs and risk premium.

7. Transmission Mechanisms: From Macro to Market

Interest-Credit-Domestic Demand Chain

Policy rate 36% → deposit rate 42.04% → consumer loan rate 66.46% → domestic demand. The critical break in this chain is in the second link: the deposit rate is above the policy rate, and the loan rate is well above both the policy and deposit rates. This shows that monetary transmission is working with a lag and only partially. The domestic demand channel is weakening (retail sales slowed to 7.7% year-on-year, consumer confidence fell to 84.8) but there is no sharp contraction yet. Corporate sales and margins are under pressure in this environment; valuation multiples are being compressed due to high nominal interest rates.

Exchange Rate-Imported Input-Inflation Chain

USD/TRY 50.08 → imported input cost → PPI → CPI. The sideways course of the exchange rate limits this channel; annual PPI at 27.41% is below CPI. However, Brent crude rising to 95.76 dollars is reviving cost pressure through energy imports. The rise in the real effective exchange rate to 107.76 (real TL appreciation) also supports disinflation but squeezes exporter margins.

Global Interest Rate-Capital Flow-Risk Premium Chain

US 30-year 5.43% → global risk premium → foreign capital flow → Turkey risk premium. Rising long-term US yields are restricting capital flows to emerging markets; Turkey's positive real interest rate partially offsets this negative. A strong dollar with DXY at 103.19 makes this chain work against Turkey.

8. Reflections on BIST 30: Sector Channels

Banking: The Winner of High Interest Rates?

The high deposit rate (42.04%) and funding costs running above the policy rate affect banks' net interest margin in two directions: deposit costs are high but loan rates (66.46%) are much higher. This spread supports bank profitability; however, slowing credit growth (slowed to 35.29%) and rising risk costs limit this support. The key for BIST 30 banking stocks is whether loan rates will converge toward the policy rate.

Industry and Holdings: Margin Pressure

Industrial production is recovering (2.1% monthly) but energy costs (Brent 95.76 dollars) and imported input prices are squeezing margins. Holdings are partially sheltered due to portfolio diversification; however, the high interest rate environment increases borrowing costs and reduces investment appetite. Capacity utilization at 74.5% is moderate; demand uncertainty is delaying investment decisions.

Retail and Consumer: The Most Fragile Link

Consumer confidence falling to 84.8, retail sales slowing to 7.7% year-on-year and the consumer loan rate rising to 66.46% make the retail sector the most fragile link. In a high interest rate environment, spending financed by consumer loans is contracting; retailers with strong cash flow and low debt gain relatively, while leveraged companies dependent on discretionary consumption are under pressure.

9. Scenarios and Monitoring Framework

Base Scenario (55%): Gradual Disinflation, Moderate Growth

Annual CPI moving sideways in the 29.77-30% band, the TCMB continuing cautious easing, growth remaining in the 2-3% band. In this scenario BIST 4.8 is supported by the preservation of the positive real interest rate and exchange rate stability; banking and exporting industry stand out. The development that would invalidate this scenario: monthly CPI rising clearly above 2.58% or the policy rate being raised again.

Optimistic Scenario (25%): Rapid Disinflation

Core inflation (28.3%) falling rapidly, PPI (27.41%) pulling CPI down, the TCMB easing more quickly. In this scenario loan rates fall, domestic demand revives, BIST 30 valuation multiples expand. The development that would invalidate it: energy prices (Brent 95.76 dollars) rising further or the exchange rate (USD/TRY 50.08) depreciating sharply.

Pessimistic Scenario (20%): Stagflation Risk

PPI (11.1%) and inflation (4.0%) continuing to rise in the Euro Area, inflation (3.7%) remaining sticky in the US, global interest rates (US 30-year 5.43%) rising further. In this environment capital flows to Turkey stop, the TL comes under pressure, and the TCMB is forced to halt easing. BIST 30 remains under clear pressure in this scenario. The development that would invalidate it: VIX (18.70) rising above 25 or DXY (103.19) rising sharply.

Indicators to be Monitored

Monthly CPI and core inflation, TCMB policy rate decisions, the deposit-loan rate spread, the USD/TRY course, foreign exchange reserves (57.02 billion dollars), the current account balance, Brent crude (95.76 dollars), the US 30-year bond yield (5.43%), VIX (18.70) and DXY (103.19).

10. Conclusion and Evaluation

The Turkish macroeconomy finds itself at an equilibrium point in autumn 2026 that could be described as "fragile stability." The positive real interest rate (+4.80 points) and the sideways exchange rate (USD/TRY 50.08) serve as an anchor for disinflation; the current account surplus (4,053 billion dollars) and rising reserves (57.02 billion dollars) limit external financing fragility. However, the sideways course of annual inflation at 29.77%, the high monthly increase of 2.58% and the becoming-evident budget deficit (-532 billion TL) show that disinflation has not yet settled onto a permanent path.

The global environment is not supportive: sticky inflation in the US (3.7%) and rising long-term yields (5.43%), stagflation signals in the Euro Area (PPI 11.1%, inflation 4.0%, industry -1.1%) and a strong dollar (DXY 103.19) are restricting capital flows. In this environment, Turkey's positive real interest rate creates a buffer but also imposes a growth cost: annual growth at 2.3% is below expectations, and consumer confidence at 84.8 is weak.

The picture for BIST 30 is two-sided: banking benefits from the high interest rate spread, industry struggles with energy and input costs, and retail is under pressure from consumer loan rates (66.46%). The index rising to 14.815.37 shows that the positive real interest rate and disinflation expectations are partially resisting the global headwind; however, the sustainability of this resilience depends on the monthly path of inflation and global risk appetite.

Methodology and Terms

  • Signal (general): Stock-based general technical evaluation (STRONG BUY / BUY / NEUTRAL / SELL / STRONG SELL); seen on the board and in the technical screening table.
  • Oscillator vote: Individual BUY/SELL votes of indicators such as RSI, StochK, MACD, CCI, WT; given in the format "11 buy/8 sell" and may conflict with the general signal (for example, a stock may diverge positively in oscillator votes while the board signal is SELL).
  • WT (Wave Trend): short-term cycle (peak/trough) oscillator; generates BUY/SELL signals.
  • Channel (%N): position of the price within the 60-day regression channel; 0% near the channel bottom (trough area), 100% near the channel top (peak area).
  • ADX: Average Directional Index; values above 25 indicate a strong trend, values below 20 indicate weak/sideways movement.
  • r: Pearson correlation coefficient calculated over a 60-day window (trend strength indicator); given in parentheses in the model portfolio table.
  • CASH: the portion not allocated to stocks in the model portfolio, held for protection.
