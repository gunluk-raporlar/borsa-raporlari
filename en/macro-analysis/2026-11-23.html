1. Global Macroeconomic Outlook

US: Resilient Growth, Stubborn Inflation

The US economy grew 3.4% quarter-on-quarter in the third quarter of 2026, significantly outperforming the market expectation of 1.5%; annual growth came in at 2.1%. This picture shows that demand has not yet broken despite monetary policy remaining tight for a long time. The labor market confirms this resilience: the unemployment rate at 4.1% points to a limited easing, while the change in non-farm payrolls came in at 165 thousand, well above expectations. However, the rise in the broad unemployment measure U-6 to 7.9% implies that caution is warranted regarding the quality of employment.

The inflation front is problematic. Annual CPI at 3.7% is above expectations; core PCE is 3.1%, while headline PCE is 3.6%. The climb in annual PPI to 5.5% shows that pipeline pressures have not yet faded. The Fed policy rate is steady at 4.0%; the real rate is only around +0.29 points, meaning the monetary stance is not tight enough to support disinflation. The Fed's rate projections pointing to 3.6% for the first year and 3.4% for the second year suggest that an easing path as fast as the market expects is not on the table.

Euro Area: Recovery Fragile, Inflation Reheating

In the Euro Area, quarterly growth at 0.4% points to a moderate recovery, while annual growth rose to 1.3%. However, industrial production continues to contract at -1.5% year-on-year; the -0.7% monthly decline in retail sales shows that domestic demand is weak. The trade balance posted a deficit of 10.3 billion euros, while the current account surplus of 27.5 billion euros offers relative relief in external balance.

On the inflation side, the picture is inverted: annual CPI rose to 4.0%, while core inflation at 2.6% has regained momentum. The jump in annual PPI to 11.1% shows that energy-driven cost pressures are back on the agenda. The ECB policy rate is steady at 2.65%; the real rate is around -1.30 points, in deeply negative territory. This means the ECB is effectively running a loose stance and implies that its room for maneuver in fighting inflation has narrowed. Unemployment at 6.5% is historically low, while the decline in wage growth to 3.0% offers a partial disinflation contribution.

Global Liquidity and Risk Appetite

This picture shows that global liquidity is squeezed within the triangle of "high rates, resilient demand, stubborn inflation." The near-zero real rate in the US suggests that financial conditions are not as tight as believed; in the Euro Area, the negative real rate appears to be artificially supporting risk appetite. In this environment, risk appetite is bound to remain volatile.

2. Turkey's Macroeconomic Outlook

What Stage of Disinflation Are We In?

In Türkiye, annual CPI at 29.77% is very close to the previous period's 29.73%; monthly inflation at 2.58% is above expectations. The decline in core inflation to 28.3% (previous 28.7%) and annual PPI staying flat at 27.41% show that disinflation has entered a "slowdown phase." In other words, the rapid decline period is behind us, and progress has slowed at the final milestone. This is the most challenging stage: services inflation and inertia resist at this point.

Monetary Policy and Real Interest Rate

The TCMB policy rate has been cut to 36.0% (previous 37.0%). Compared with inflation, the real policy rate is clearly positive at around +4.80 points. This shows that Türkiye exhibits a stance rarely seen on a global scale: "tight but on an easing trend." However, the decline in the deposit rate from 41.86% to 41.86% (previous 42,752) implies that the market has begun to price in the policy rate cut. The rise in the consumer loan rate to 66.46% (previous 64.74) shows that the credit channel is still very expensive, meaning monetary transmission works with lags and selectively.

Growth, Employment and Confidence

Growth recovered in the second quarter with a quarterly 1.1%, while annual 2.3% remained below the expectation (2.9%). Industrial production made a strong jump with a monthly 2.1%; annual 2.61% was moderate. Unemployment at 8.5% and participation rate at 52.7% leave the employment outlook relatively stable. However, confidence indicators are mixed: while consumer confidence rose to 86.8, the economic expectation index fell from 101.3 to 97.3 and business confidence dropped to 99.2. This divergence reflects the gap between households' short-term optimism and businesses' medium-term caution.

3. Domestic Demand, Credit and Liquidity

Interest-to-Credit Transmission Channel

Although the policy rate fell to 36.0%, the rise in consumer loan rates to 66.46% shows that the transmission mechanism is not working fully. The widening of this spread stems from the combined effect of banks' funding costs, risk premiums and macroprudential regulations. While credit growth remains high at 35.29% annually, it is slowing compared to the previous 36.32%; that is, credit expansion is losing momentum.

Money Supply and Liquidity Conditions

M3 money supply at 28.17% annually is running close to nominal inflation; this shows that real money supply is neither contracting nor expanding. Deposit rates at 41.86%, above the policy rate, imply that banks are continuing deposit competition and trying to attract liquidity. In this environment, reviving domestic demand through the credit channel is difficult; whether the rise in consumer confidence turns into spending depends on income expectations.

Outlook for Domestic Demand

Retail sales are nearly flat at 0.1% monthly; annual 7.7% is below inflation, that is, a sign of contraction in real terms. This confirms that high credit costs are restraining domestic demand. The recovery in consumer confidence has not yet been reflected in spending behavior.

4. Global Risk Appetite and Interest Rate Expectations

Market Volatility and the Dollar

The jump in VIX from 28.44 to 35.89 (+26.20%) points to a sharp deterioration in global risk appetite. This level is historically the "high fear" zone and adversely affects capital flows to emerging markets. DXY is relatively flat at 103.81; the dollar's global value is stable but risk appetite is deteriorating. This combination shows that capital is in search of a "safe haven".

Bond Yields and Yield Curve

The US 30-year bond yield fell to 5.30% (previous 5.40%), while the 5-year fell to 4.70% (previous 4.87%) and the 3-month to 4.12%. The decline in long-term yields implies that the market expects rate cuts in the medium term; however, the yield curve still being positively sloped shows that recession pricing is not yet strong. The combination of high VIX and falling bond yields is a typical "risk-off" picture.

Reflection on BIST

The deterioration in global risk appetite was directly reflected in BIST 30 falling 2.40% on November 20 (to 13.957.13 points). Although Turkey's real interest rate advantage is attractive for foreign investors, when global volatility rises the risk premium widens and can overshadow this advantage.

5. Budget and External Balance

Budget Performance

In October 2026 the budget balance posted a deficit of -481.0 billion TL; the previous period was -532.0 billion TL. Although the slight narrowing of the deficit is positive, the level is still high. The budget deficit-to-GDP ratio is not included in the data set; therefore, instead of giving a ratio, it should be noted that the absolute size of the deficit may make it difficult for fiscal policy to align with disinflation.

External Balance and External Financing

As of September 2026 the current account posted a surplus of 2.72 billion dollars (previous surplus of 4,053 billion dollars). The continuation of the surplus is positive; however, the decline in its size points to weakening external demand. The trade balance posted a deficit of -6.7 billion dollars in October (previous -5,232 billion dollars); exports were 25.9 billion dollars and imports 32.58 billion dollars. Exports staying flat while imports rise shows domestic demand's dependence on imports and the fragility of the external balance.

Reserves and Tourism

Foreign exchange reserves rose to 59.25 billion dollars (previous 57.02 billion dollars); this shows that the external financing buffer has strengthened. Tourism revenues jumped to 25.53 billion dollars in the third quarter (previous 15.87 billion dollars) and the number of tourists rose 0.4% annually. Tourism continues to be a critical item supporting the current account balance.

6. Energy, Commodities, Foreign Exchange and Bond Markets

Oil and Commodities

Brent crude fell to 89.95 dollars (previous 91.98), while WTI is flat at 83.41 dollars. This level of energy prices continues to be a pressure factor on the current account balance and inflation for a net energy importer like Turkey. Gold, meanwhile, fell sharply to 3.515.40 dollars per ounce (-4.62%); gram gold dropped to 5.712.98 TL (-4.64%). This correction in gold shows that safe-haven demand has weakened or profit-taking has occurred even in a risk-off environment.

Exchange Rates

USD/TRY is flat at 50.55; EUR/TRY rose slightly to 55.56. The exchange rate's nominally stable course, combined with the CBRT's real effective exchange rate index rising from 105.1 to 107.76, shows that the TL has appreciated in real terms. Although this serves somewhat as an anchor in the fight against inflation, it carries the risk of eroding export competitiveness.

Bond Market

The decline in US bond yields implies that global interest rate expectations are tilting toward easing. However, the jump in the VIX suggests that this easing expectation may stem from "bad news."

7. Transmission Mechanisms: From Macro to Market

Interest Rate Channel

Policy rate 36.0% → deposit rate 41.86% → consumer loan rate 66.46% → domestic demand. Each link in this chain creates friction: the deposit rate is above the policy rate (banks are drawing in liquidity), while the loan rate is much higher (risk premium and regulations). Result: domestic demand receives weak support from the credit channel; retail sales staying at 7.7% annually, below inflation, confirms this. Company sales are contracting in real terms, margins are under pressure, valuation multiples are compressed.

Exchange Rate Channel

USD/TRY is nominally flat at 50.55, but the real effective exchange rate at 107.76 means the TL is overvalued in real terms. Imported input cost pressure is easing through the exchange rate channel; this is consistent with PPI staying flat at 27.41% annually. However, if real appreciation continues, exporter margins will erode and the external balance will deteriorate.

Global Interest Rate Channel

The US real interest rate is neutral at +0.29 points; the Fed's projections do not promise rapid easing. In this environment, foreign capital flows are volatile; the VIX jumping to 35.89 is widening the risk premium. Turkey's positive real interest rate creates appeal, but global volatility may overshadow this appeal.

8. Reflections on BIST 30: Sector Channels

Banking

In a high interest rate environment, banks are relatively protected in terms of net interest margin; although the deposit rate staying above the policy rate increases funding costs, the high spread in loan rates supports the margin. However, slowing credit growth and possible deterioration in credit quality are risk factors.

The 2.1% monthly jump in industrial production is positive, but the 2.61% annual figure is moderate. The real overvalued TL is pressuring exporter industrial margins; energy costs remain high. Since domestic demand is weak, the profitability of industrial companies depends on external demand and cost control.

Holding and Retail

Holdings differ according to the sector distribution in their portfolios; indebted structures are at a disadvantage in a high interest rate environment. In retail, the real contraction in retail sales is creating revenue pressure; the recovery in consumer confidence has not yet been reflected in sales.

Who Wins, Who Loses?

In a high interest rate environment, companies with strong net cash positions and low debt win; companies with high leverage and dependence on domestic demand lose. The real overvalued TL benefits importers and domestic-market-focused companies, and harms exporters.

9. Scenarios and Monitoring Framework

Base Scenario (55%): Gradual Disinflation, Sideways Market

CPI slowly declining into the 29.77-30 band, the TCMB continuing cautious cuts, global risk appetite fluctuating. In this scenario, BIST 30 moves in a volatile-sideways band; sector divergence becomes more pronounced. Development that would invalidate it: monthly inflation falling permanently below 2% or the VIX declining below 25.

Optimistic Scenario (25%): Rapid Disinflation and Recovery in Risk Appetite

Recovery in global risk appetite (VIX decline), an easing signal from the Fed, a rapid decline in core inflation in Turkey. In this case, the risk premium narrows, foreign inflows strengthen, and BIST 30 gains upward momentum. Development that would invalidate it: core inflation rising again or a tightening signal from the Fed.

Pessimistic Scenario (20%): Global Volatility Shock and Inflation Inertia

The VIX maintaining its high course, oil prices rising, monthly inflation in Turkey exceeding 3%. In this scenario, despite the real interest rate advantage, the risk premium widens and BIST 30 is suppressed. Development that would invalidate it: the VIX falling below 30 and monthly inflation declining below 2%.

Indicators to be Monitored

Monthly CPI and core inflation, TCMB policy rate decisions, VIX, US 10-year bond yield (the dataset has 5- and 30-year), DXY, Brent oil, USD/TRY course, credit growth, current account balance, tourism revenues.

10. Conclusion and Evaluation

The Turkish macroeconomy is in the most challenging phase of disinflation: annual inflation is flat at 29.77%, core inflation is declining modestly at 28.3%, while monthly inflation at 2.58% is above target. The TCMB's 36.0% policy rate provides a positive real interest rate (+4.80 points); this is a stance that supports disinflation but brakes domestic demand. The loan rate staying very high at 66.46% shows that transmission works with a lag and at a cost.

On the global side, there is a risk-off picture combining resilient growth and stubborn inflation in the US, negative real interest rates and re-heating inflation in the Euro Area, and the VIX jumping by -1.3. This environment overshadows Turkey's real interest rate advantage; BIST 30's 2.40% decline is a reflection of this.

External balance is relatively comfortable: the current account surplus is 2.72 billion dollars, reserves rose to 59.25 billion dollars, tourism is strong. However, the budget deficit (-481.0 billion TL) and the widening trade deficit (-6.7 billion dollars) are elements of fragility. The overvalued TL is putting pressure on exporters.

In the period ahead, the key question is this: can monthly inflation fall permanently below 2%? If it does, the CBRT's room for rate cuts widens, domestic demand revives, and the BIST 30 revalues. If it does not, real interest rates stay high for a long time and growth potential is suppressed. Global risk appetite, meanwhile, is the external variable in this equation: the course of the VIX will determine the direction of capital flows to Türkiye.

Methodology and Terms

  • Signal (general): Stock-based general technical evaluation (STRONG BUY / BUY / NEUTRAL / SELL / STRONG SELL); seen on the board and in the technical screening table.
  • Oscillator vote: Individual BUY/SELL votes of indicators such as RSI, StochK, MACD, CCI, WT; given in the format "11 buy/8 sell" and may conflict with the general signal (for example, a stock may diverge positively in oscillator votes while the board signal is SELL).
  • WT (Wave Trend): short-term cycle (peak/trough) oscillator; generates BUY/SELL signals.
  • Channel (%N): position of the price within the 60-day regression channel; 0% near the channel bottom (trough area), 100% near the channel top (peak area).
  • ADX: Average Directional Index; values above 25 indicate a strong trend, values below 20 indicate weak/sideways movement.
  • r: Pearson correlation coefficient calculated over a 60-day window (trend strength indicator); given in parentheses in the model portfolio table.
  • CASH: the portion not allocated to stocks in the model portfolio, held for protection.
