2. Turkey's Macroeconomic Outlook

The Stage of Inflation and Disinflation

In Türkiye, in the October 2026 reference period, annual inflation is 29.77%, monthly 2.58%; core inflation 28.3%; PPI 27.41% year-on-year, 1.65% month-on-month. The policy rate is 36.0%; the real rate is +4.80. The course of annual inflation close to the previous 29.73% shows that disinflation is proceeding without losing speed but slowly. Monthly inflation of 2.58% implies that the momentum needed for an annual decline is not yet strong. Core remaining above PPI indicates that services- and demand-driven inertia continues.

Growth, Employment and Capacity

In the second quarter of 2026, growth is 1.1% quarter-on-quarter, 2.3% year-on-year; the previous year-on-year figure was 2.6%. Unemployment is 8.5%, economic participation 52.7%; the capacity utilization rate is 74.5%. Industrial production in the September 2026 reference period is 2.1% month-on-month, 2,6144% year-on-year. These data show that growth is positive but close to below potential and that industrial momentum is fragile.

Confidence and Expectation Channels

Consumer confidence is 84.8 points, previous 91.9; economic sentiment 97.3, previous 101.3; business confidence 99.8, previous 102.0. The decline in confidence indicators shows that the high interest rate and inflation environment is weighing on household and firm perception. As the expectation channel weakens, the likelihood of postponing consumption and investment decisions increases. Therefore, disinflation needs to be confirmed not only in prices but also in expectations.

3. Domestic Demand, Credit and Liquidity

Interest Rate Pass-Through and Credit Cost

While the policy rate is 36.0%, the deposit rate stands at 41.86% and the consumer loan rate at 66.46%. The fact that the deposit rate is above the policy rate and the consumer loan rate is markedly higher shows that funding costs are strongly reflected onto households. This structure implies that banks are operating the liquidity and risk premium channels simultaneously. The high level of the loan rate limits interest-sensitive spending such as durable goods and housing.

Credit Growth and Money Supply

Annual credit growth is 35,289.2891%, previous 36,3222%; M3 money supply annual 28,168.1681%, previous 28,805%. Although credit growth remains high, it is slowing; M3 growth is also receding. This shows that tight monetary policy is effective in the liquidity channel, but credit expansion has not yet cooled to the desired extent. Retail sales at 0.1% monthly and 7.7% annual imply that domestic demand is weakening but has not come to a complete halt.

Liquidity Conditions and Domestic Demand

With consumer confidence at 84.8 and the economic expectation at 97.3, the high cost of credit suggests that domestic demand will become more selective in the period ahead. The deposit rate at 41.86% shows that saving remains relatively attractive compared with spending. This strengthens the channel of shifting from consumption to saving. However, the credit growth level of 35,289.2891% implies that this transition will be gradual.

US 30-year bond yield 5,401%, 5-year 4,866%, 3-month 4,147%; policy rate 4.0%. Fed rate projections stand at 3.6% for the first year and 3.4% for the second year. The high level of long-term yields shows that the market is cautious about inflation and the term risk premium. In this environment, a gradual and conditional easing framework is more consistent than assuming that global rates will fall quickly.

Reflection on BIST

5. Budget and External Balance

Budget Outlook

In the October 2026 reference period, the budget balance is -481.0 billion TRY, previous -532.0 billion TRY. The deficit continues but is narrowing somewhat compared with the previous period. Since the budget/GDP ratio is not available in the data set, it should be stated qualitatively, without giving a ratio, that fiscal space is limited. In a high interest rate environment, borrowing cost stands out as one of the most important transmission channels on the budget.

Current Account Balance and Trade

The current account balance in the latest reading shows a surplus of +2.72 billion $, previous +4,053 billion $; the trade balance is -6.7 billion $, previous -5,232 billion $. Exports are 25.9 billion $, imports 32.58 billion $. The current account surplus forms despite the trade deficit, with the contribution of services and tourism revenues. Tourism revenues are 25.53 billion $ (third quarter) and the number of tourists has increased by 0.4% annually. This fragile composition shows that external financing is sensitive to services revenues.

Reserves and External Financing Fragility

Foreign exchange reserves are 59.25 billion $, previous 57.02 billion $. Although the increase in reserves is positive, when evaluated together with the external financing need and the global interest rate environment, it can be said that the buffer remains limited. The US current account deficit is -246.0 billion $, the trade deficit -92.83 billion $; the Euro Area current account surplus is 27.5 billion €, the trade deficit -10.3 billion €. Global imbalances form the background affecting the risk premium in economies that need external financing, such as Türkiye.

Energy and Gold

Brent crude at 90.05 USD/barrel, WTI at 83.16 USD/barrel; gold at 3.627.90 USD/ounce, gram gold at 5.891.17 TL/gram. The Brent level of 90.05 shows that energy costs are still high. Gold at 3.627.90 per ounce and 5.891.17 TL per gram reflects both global and exchange-rate-driven pricing. Exchange Rates and Real Exchange Rate

USD/TRY 50.51, EUR/TRY 55.46; the real effective exchange rate is 107.76 index, previous 105.1. Exchange rate levels lie at the center of the pass-through between imported input costs and inflation. The real effective exchange rate at 107.76 implies that the TL is relatively strong in real terms. While this creates a channel that limits inflation, it may create separate pressure on exporter margins.

Bond Yields and Exchange Rate-Inflation Interaction

US 30-year bond yield 5,401%, 5-year 4,866%, 3-month 4,147%. Turkish bond yields are not included in the dataset; therefore, without giving numbers, it should be said that the high policy rate and inflation push the risk premium higher in bond pricing. The exchange rate channel is transmitted through USD/TRY 50.51 and EUR/TRY 55.46 to imported input costs, and from there to PPI and CPI. Therefore, a permanent rise in the exchange rate could delay the disinflation process.

7. Transmission Mechanisms: From Macro to Market

Interest-Credit-Domestic Demand Chain

Policy rate 36.0%, deposit rate 41.86%, consumer loan rate 66.46%. In this chain, the policy rate is first reflected in banks' funding costs, then in deposit and loan rates. A consumer loan rate of 66.46% raises household borrowing costs, limiting demand for durable goods and housing. With credit growth at 35,289.2891 and M3 growth at 28,1681, it appears that domestic demand has not fully cooled but is slowing.

Exchange Rate-Imported Input-Inflation Chain

USD/TRY 50.51, EUR/TRY 55.46; PPI annual 27.41%, CPI annual 29.77%. The exchange rate channel is transmitted through imported intermediate goods and energy costs first to PPI, then to CPI. Brent at 90.05 USD/barrel strengthens this channel for an energy-importing economy. A real effective exchange rate of 107.76 implies that the real appreciation of the TL somewhat limits this pass-through.

Global Interest Rate-Capital Flow-Risk Premium Chain

US policy rate 4.0%, 30-year bond yield 5,401%, VIX 28.44, DXY 103.93. As global rates and volatility rise, foreign capital's orientation toward emerging markets becomes selective. This affects Turkey's risk premium and external financing costs. BIST 30's daily return of -0.78%, and -0.92% in dollar terms, shows that this channel is reflected in market pricing.

8. Reflections on BIST 30: Sector Channels

Banks and the Interest Rate Environment

For the banking sector within BIST 30, the policy rate of 36.0%, deposit rate of 41.86% and consumer loan rate of 66.46% are decisive. The high interest rate environment activates the deposit cost and credit risk channels simultaneously. Banks with a strong deposit base and pricing power may remain relatively resilient; banks with weakening credit quality are adversely affected by the risk premium. A credit growth level of 35,289.2891 shows that maturity and risk management, as well as volume, come to the fore in the banking sector.

Industry, Holding and Retail

For industrial companies, USD/TRY 50.51, EUR/TRY 55.46, Brent 90.05 and PPI 27.41% form the cost channel. With capacity utilization at 74.5% and industrial production at 2.1% month-on-month, weakness in export markets may limit margins. In holdings, foreign currency position and the subsidiary mix are decisive; in retail, consumer confidence at 84.8, retail sales at 0.1% month-on-month and 7.7% year-on-year show the demand channel. In a high interest rate environment, companies with low leverage and strong cash flow are relatively advantaged, while leveraged and interest-rate-sensitive companies are disadvantaged.

Valuation and Risk Perception

BIST 30 at 14.300.01 points, daily -0.78%; BIST 100 at 11.400.09 points, daily -0.67%. VIX 28.44 and DXY 103.93 may create discount pressure on valuation multiples. In a high interest rate environment, the present value of future cash flows falls; therefore, companies with long-term growth stories are more sensitive. Conversely, companies that generate short-term cash and have strong balance sheets may show relative resilience during periods of volatility.

9. Scenarios and Monitoring Framework

Base Scenario: Gradual Disinflation (50%)

In the base scenario, annual inflation is expected to decline gradually from 29.77%, core from 28.3% and PPI from 27.41%, while the policy rate is expected to remain cautious around 36.0%. With the deposit rate at 41.86% and the consumer loan rate at 66.46%, the slowdown in domestic demand and retail sales hovering at 0.1% month-on-month fit this framework. The development that would invalidate this scenario is monthly inflation rising significantly above 2.58% or credit growth re-accelerating above 35,2891. Indicators to watch are monthly CPI, core inflation, PPI, deposit and loan rates, M3, the current account balance and reserves.

Optimistic Scenario (25%)

In the optimistic scenario, monthly inflation falling below 2.58%, core dropping below 28.3% and PPI remaining below 27.41% would strengthen disinflation. In this case, deposit rates falling from 41.86% and consumer loan rates from 66.46% could support the domestic demand and valuation channel. VIX falling below 28.44 and DXY below 103.93 would improve risk appetite. The development that would invalidate this scenario is monthly inflation momentum rising again or USD/TRY becoming permanent above 50.51.

Pessimistic Scenario (25%)

In the pessimistic scenario, monthly inflation remaining above 2.58%, core deviating above 28.3% and PPI rising above 27.41% would delay disinflation. In this environment, the policy rate remaining around 36.0% for a long time and the consumer loan rate at 66.46% exerting pressure would weaken domestic demand. VIX remaining above 28.44, DXY above 103.93 and reserves falling below 59.25 billion $ would increase external financing fragility. The development that would invalidate this scenario is a rapid decline in inflation that spreads to core, along with a marked recovery in reserves.

10. Conclusion and Evaluation

Overall Balance Sheet

On the global side, inflation in the US is 3.7%, the policy rate is 4.0%, VIX is 28.44 and DXY is 103.93; in the Euro Area, inflation is 4.0%, the policy rate is 2.65% and industrial production is -1.5% year-on-year. This picture shows that global liquidity is not abundantly one-directional and that risk appetite remains fragile. In Türkiye, inflation is 29.77%, the policy rate is 36.0%, the real interest rate is 4.80%, growth is 2.3% year-on-year and unemployment is 8.5%. The disinflation process is progressing, but monthly inflation of 2.58% and core inflation of 28.3% imply that the process is not yet complete.

Risks and Conditional Outlook

On the domestic demand channel, the deposit rate of 41.86%, the consumer loan rate of 66.46% and credit growth of 35,289.2891 continue to be decisive. On the external balance side, the current account surplus is +2.72 billion $, the trade deficit -6.7 billion $ and reserves 59.25 billion $; this composition requires caution in terms of external financing. BIST 30 at 14.300.01 points with a daily return of -0.78% shows sensitivity to global volatility. Conditionally, a decline in monthly inflation momentum and an improvement in global risk appetite could support valuations; in the opposite case, risk premium and volatility pressure would persist.

Methodology and Terms

  • Signal (general): Stock-based general technical evaluation (STRONG BUY / BUY / NEUTRAL / SELL / STRONG SELL); seen on the board and in the technical screening table.
  • Oscillator vote: Individual BUY/SELL votes of indicators such as RSI, StochK, MACD, CCI, WT; given in the format "11 buy/8 sell" and may conflict with the general signal (for example, a stock may diverge positively in oscillator votes while the board signal is SELL).
  • WT (Wave Trend): short-term cycle (peak/trough) oscillator; generates BUY/SELL signals.
  • Channel (%N): position of the price within the 60-day regression channel; 0% near the channel bottom (trough area), 100% near the channel top (peak area).
  • ADX: Average Directional Index; values above 25 indicate a strong trend, values below 20 indicate weak/sideways movement.
  • r: Pearson correlation coefficient calculated over a 60-day window (trend strength indicator); given in parentheses in the model portfolio table.
  • CASH: the ratio held for precautionary purposes in the model portfolio that is not allocated to equities.
