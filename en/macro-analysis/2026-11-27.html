1. Global Macroeconomic Outlook

US: Resilient Growth, Sticky Inflation

The US economy grew 4.3% quarter-on-quarter in the third quarter of 2026, markedly above estimates (1.5% forecast), displaying strong momentum; annual growth was 2.3%. The data accompanying this picture paint a contradictory image: while manufacturing PMI at 54.6 and services PMI at 55.4 remain in expansion territory, consumer confidence fell to 88.6 points, signaling erosion in household sentiment. On the inflation front, headline annual inflation at 3.7% and core PCE at 3.3% remain markedly above target; PPI rising to 5.5% year-on-year shows that cost pressures have not yet broken. Although the labor market has cooled somewhat (unemployment 4.1%, U-6 7.9%), the 165 thousand increase in NFP shows employment resilience is maintained.

Euro Area: Fragile Recovery

In the Euro Area, quarterly growth at 0.4% points to a moderate recovery, while annual growth was 1.3%. However, industrial production contracting by -1.5% year-on-year and retail sales declining by -0.7% month-on-month show that the recovery remains weak in the manufacturing and consumption legs. Inflation at 4.0% annually is above target; PPI jumping to 11.1% year-on-year signals that energy-driven cost pressures are strengthening again. The ECB policy rate is held steady at 2.65%; the real rate at approximately -1.3% is still negative, meaning monetary policy is effectively loose. Unemployment at 6.5% remains historically low, while the current account posts a surplus of €27.5 billion.

Global Liquidity and Risk Appetite

The most striking phenomenon in the global picture is that, despite the US real rate (approximately 0.3%) having turned positive, inflation remains sticky above target. The Fed's rate projections stand at 4.1% for the first year and 3.9% for the second year; that is, market expectations are close to a "higher for longer" scenario. The VIX at 26.8, above its historical average, shows that risk appetite is fragile and volatility is being priced at a premium. The DXY remaining strong at 103.8 is a limiting factor for capital flows to emerging markets.

2. Turkey's Macroeconomic Outlook

The Stage of Inflation and Disinflation

In Türkiye, annual CPI in October 2026 remained nearly flat at 29.77% compared to the previous month (29.73%); monthly inflation came in at 2.58%, above the 2.4% estimate. Core inflation at 28.3% annually is below the headline and declining somewhat (previous 28.7%); PPI at 27.41% annually remains below CPI, implying that cost-side pressure is limited. This picture shows that disinflation is in what could be called the "last mile" — a phase that is slowing but has not yet broken: monthly inflation hardening in the 2.5% band delays the targeted decline in the annual path.

Monetary Policy and Real Interest Rate

The TCMB policy rate was cut from 37% to 36% in October 2026. Despite this cut, the real policy rate at approximately 36% is markedly positive; that is, monetary policy still maintains its tight stance. However, deposit rates declining to 41.78% (previous 42.75%) and consumer loan rates rising to 66.46% (previous 64.74%) show that the policy rate cut is not fully reflected in credit costs, and that the risk premium on the credit side has even increased. This divergence points to a blockage in the credit channel of the transmission mechanism.

Growth, Employment and Confidence

In the second quarter, quarterly growth at 1.1% was above the previous 0.3%; annual growth, however, at 2.3% remained below the 2.9% estimate. Industrial production recovered by 2.1% month-on-month, while capacity utilization remained flat at 74.4%. Unemployment at 8.5%, participation rate at 52.7%. Confidence indicators are mixed: while consumer confidence rose to 86.8, the economic sentiment index fell sharply from 101.3 to 97.3 and business confidence dropped below the threshold to 99.2. This points to a deterioration in the business world's forward-looking expectations despite the moderate improvement in household sentiment.

3. Domestic Demand, Credit and Liquidity

Interest Rate-Credit Channel

Despite the policy rate being cut to 36%, consumer loan rates rising to 66.46% show that transmission works asymmetrically. Behind this asymmetry lies the widening spread between banks' funding costs and the credit risk premium. Although credit growth remains high at 35.29% annually, it is slowing from the previous 36.32%; that is, credit expansion is losing momentum. The annual increase in M3 money supply slowing to 26.86% (previous 28.81%) confirms that liquidity expansion is decelerating.

Domestic Demand Outlook

Retail sales are nearly stagnant at 0.1% month-on-month; the 7.7% annual increase, however, is below inflation, meaning a contraction in real terms. Despite the moderate recovery in consumer confidence, retail data remaining weak shows that households' spending appetite is constrained by interest rates and income pressure. This combination implies that domestic demand is contributing to the disinflation process, but that this comes at the cost of growth.

4. Global Risk Appetite and Interest Rate Expectations

Market Indicators

The decline of VIX from 28.29 to 26.80 points to a limited improvement in risk appetite; however, the 26 level is still the "anxiety" zone. The US bond curve is noteworthy: 3-month 4%.16, 5-year 4%.66, 30-year 5%.24. The fact that long-term yields are significantly above short-term ones shows that the term premium remains high and that the market is pricing long-term inflation/cost risks. DXY staying strong at 103.8 is a competitive obstacle for emerging market assets.

Reflection on BIST

The rise of BIST 100 to 10.743 points (0.82% daily) and the 0.67% increase in BIST 30 are consistent with the moderate improvement in global risk appetite. However, the daily return staying at 0.71% in dollar terms shows that TL assets are partly eroded by exchange rate movements. If the decline in VIX continues, a positive reflection of risk appetite on BIST can be expected; however, as long as DXY remains strong, foreign capital inflows are likely to remain limited.

5. Budget and External Balance

Budget

The October 2026 budget balance shows a deficit of -481 billion TL; however, this is a slight improvement compared to the previous -532 billion TL. The persistence of the budget deficit shows that fiscal policy does not support the disinflation process, but on the contrary indirectly supports domestic demand. This is a factor that increases the burden on monetary policy.

External Balance

The current account posted a surplus of 2.72 billion $ in September 2026 (previous 4.05 billion $); the continuation of the surplus is positive, but it is on a declining trend. The trade balance posted a deficit of -6.7 billion $ in October (previous -5.23 billion $); with exports at 25.9 billion $ and imports at 32.58 billion $, the foreign trade deficit widened. Tourism revenues were strong at 25.53 billion $ in the third quarter; however, the -5.23% year-on-year decline in the number of tourists (previous 0.4%) is an important warning signal. Foreign exchange reserves declined markedly to 50.38 billion $ from the previous 59.25 billion $; this shows that the external financing buffer has weakened.

External Financing Fragility

When the decline in reserves, the drop in the number of tourists and the widening of the trade deficit are evaluated together, it can be said that external financing fragility has increased. The continuation of the current account surplus partly balances this fragility; however, the narrowing trend of the surplus and the loss of reserves imply that the buffer against external shocks has thinned.

6. Energy, Commodities, Foreign Exchange and Bond Markets

Energy and Commodities

While Brent crude declined -3.20% daily to 91.58 $/barrel, WTI rose 1.46% to 84.90 $; the divergence between the two benchmarks points to intra-market volatility. Gold rose 1.59% to 3.592 $/ounce; gram gold increased 1.70% to 5.857.86 TL in TL terms. The strong performance of gold confirms that global risk perception is still cautious and that safe-haven demand continues.

Exchange Rate and Inflation Interaction

USD/TRY rose modestly (0.11%) to 50.72, while EUR/TRY rose 0.28% to 55.70. The moderate course of the exchange rate increase shows that exchange-rate-driven inflation pressure remains limited for now. However, the rise in the real effective exchange rate from 105.1 to 107.76 implies that the TL has appreciated in real terms; this is a medium-term risk for export competitiveness. In the exchange rate-inflation interaction, the critical threshold is whether the pace of exchange rate increase remains below monthly inflation; current data show that this threshold is being maintained.

Bond Market

The US 30-year bond yield remaining high at 5.24% indicates that global long-term interest rates will stay high. This environment is a factor that pushes up the external borrowing cost of countries with high risk premiums such as Türkiye.

7. Transmission Mechanisms: From Macro to Market

Interest-Credit-Domestic Demand Chain

Policy rate 36% → deposit rate 41.78% → consumer loan rate 66.46% → credit growth 35.29% (slowing) → retail sales 0.1% monthly (stagnant) → corporate sales under pressure → margins tightening → valuation multiples constrained by interest rate pressure. The critical link in this chain is that the loan rate has decoupled from the policy rate; that is, the pass-through of policy rate cuts to loan costs is delayed and partial.

Exchange Rate-Imported Input-Inflation Chain

USD/TRY 50.72 (moderate increase) → imported input cost limited pressure → PPI 27.41% annually (below CPI) → core inflation 28.3% (declining). The exchange rate channel is not currently disrupting disinflation; however, the appreciation of the real effective exchange rate may adversely affect exports and growth in the future through loss of competitiveness.

Global Interest Rate-Capital Flow-Risk Premium Chain

US 30-year 5.24% → global long-term interest rates high → DXY strong at 103.8 → capital flows to emerging markets limited → Türkiye's risk premium remains high → external financing cost high → reserve accumulation becomes difficult. This chain shows that Türkiye can manage its disinflation process only to the extent that external conditions permit.

8. Reflections on BIST 30: Sector Channels

Banking

In a high interest rate environment, banks' net interest margins may widen; however, the slowdown in credit growth and the rise in credit risk premium push provisioning costs up. The deposit rate remaining at 41.78%, above the policy rate, keeps funding costs high. The critical balance for banks is the trade-off between net interest margin and credit quality.

The 2.1% monthly recovery in industrial production is positive; however, the 2.61% annual increase is moderate. The stagnant course of domestic demand limits the sales volume of industrial companies. Weakness in export markets (Euro Area industrial production -1.5% annually) and the appreciation of the real exchange rate pose a risk for exporting industry. Volatility in energy costs (Brent 91.58 $) creates margin pressure.

Holding and Retail

Holdings are partly shielded against macro shocks thanks to the sector diversification in their portfolios; however, subsidiary valuations are sensitive to interest rate and exchange rate movements. The retail sector, on the other hand, is the most fragile link: the contraction of retail sales in real terms creates revenue pressure; the consumer loan rate remaining high at 66.46% adversely affects installment sales. In a high interest rate environment, companies that generate cash and operate with low leverage are relatively advantaged; companies with heavy debt and high working capital needs are under pressure.

9. Scenarios and Monitoring Framework

Base Scenario (50%): Gradual Disinflation, Slow Growth

Monthly inflation hardening in the 2.5% band, annual inflation declining moderately toward year-end, and the TCMB continuing its cautious cutting cycle. In this scenario, domestic demand is stagnant, growth is below potential, and BIST moves volatile but with an upward bias on rate cut expectations. Developments that would invalidate the scenario: monthly inflation rising above 3% or a rapid decline in reserves.

Optimistic Scenario (25%): Rapid Disinflation, Recovery in Risk Appetite

Global risk appetite strengthening as VIX falls below 20, DXY weakening, tourism revenues recovering and reserves being rebuilt. In this scenario the TCMB finds room for faster cuts, loan rates decline, domestic demand revives, and BIST 30 diverges positively, led by banks and industrials. Development that would invalidate it: the Fed revising its rate projections upward or a geopolitical shock.

Pessimistic Scenario (25%): Entrenchment in Inflation, External Financing Pressure

Monthly inflation running above 3%, the exchange rate accelerating, reserve losses continuing and the decline in tourist numbers deepening. In this scenario the TCMB halts the cutting cycle, real rates rise further, domestic demand contracts sharply, and BIST declines under valuation pressure; banks are hurt by provisioning costs and industrials by margin pressure. Development that would invalidate it: a marked widening of the current account surplus and reserves stabilizing.

Indicators to be Monitored

Monthly CPI and core inflation path; TCMB policy rate decisions and statement tone; weekly foreign exchange reserves; tourist numbers and tourism revenues; current account balance and trade balance; VIX, DXY and US long-term bond yields; Fed rate projections; credit growth and the deposit-loan rate spread.

10. Conclusion and Evaluation

The Turkish economy is in a phase of disinflation that could be described as the "last mile" — slowing but not yet broken. The real policy rate being clearly positive at 36% shows the tight stance is being maintained, while loan rates decoupling from the policy rate signals that the transmission mechanism is clogged in the credit channel. Domestic demand is stagnant, growth is below potential, unemployment is moderate; on the other hand, the budget deficit and reserve losses create fragility on the fiscal and external balance side. On the global side, sticky inflation and high long-term rates in the US are limiting capital flows to emerging markets; VIX staying at 26.8 in the anxiety zone confirms that risk appetite is fragile. For BIST 30 specifically, the high-rate environment makes companies that generate cash and operate with low leverage relatively advantageous, while pressuring companies that are debt-heavy and have high working capital needs. In the period ahead, the critical threshold is whether monthly inflation becomes entrenched in the 2.5% band and whether reserve losses stop; these two variables form the divergence point of the scenarios. Methodology and Terms

  • Signal (general): Stock-based general technical evaluation (STRONG BUY / BUY / NEUTRAL / SELL / STRONG SELL); seen on the board and in the technical screening table.
  • Oscillator vote: Individual BUY/SELL votes of indicators such as RSI, StochK, MACD, CCI, WT; given in the format "11 buy/8 sell" and may conflict with the general signal (for example, a stock may diverge positively in oscillator votes while the board signal is SELL).
  • WT (Wave Trend): short-term cycle (peak/trough) oscillator; generates BUY/SELL signals.
  • Channel (%N): position of the price within the 60-day regression channel; 0% near the channel bottom (trough area), 100% near the channel top (peak area).
  • ADX: Average Directional Index; values above 25 indicate a strong trend, values below 20 indicate weak/sideways movement.
  • r: Pearson correlation coefficient calculated over a 60-day window (trend strength indicator); given in parentheses in the model portfolio table.
  • CASH: the portion not allocated to stocks in the model portfolio, held for protection.
