1. Global Macroeconomic Outlook

US: Resilient Growth, Sticky Inflation

The US economy grew 3.4% quarter-on-quarter in the third quarter of 2026, performing markedly above market expectations; annual growth came in at 2.1%. This picture shows how resilient demand has remained in an environment where monetary policy is still restrictive. However, improvement on the inflation front is limited: annual CPI at 3.7% is above expectations, while core PCE at 3.1% is running markedly above target. The 5.5% annual increase in producer prices signals that cost pressures have not yet been broken.

Labor Market and the Fed's Dilemma

While the unemployment rate follows a moderate course at 4.1%, the rise in the broad unemployment measure U-6 to 7.9% implies that idle capacity is increasing. Non-farm payroll growth exceeding expectations at 165 thousand points to an equilibrium in which the labor market is cooling but not unraveling. The policy rate is steady at 4.0%; the real rate is only around +0.29 points, meaning monetary policy is nearly neutral in real terms. The Fed's rate projections stand at 3.6% for the first year and 3.4% for the second year — this points to a gradual but slow easing path.

Euro Area: Weak Momentum, Energy-Driven Price Pressure

In the Euro Area, quarterly growth at 0.4% is below the previous 0.6%; industrial production is contracting with -0.5% monthly and -1.1% annually. The -0.7% monthly decline in retail sales confirms that domestic demand is weakening. On the other hand, annual inflation has risen to 4.0%, and PPI has deviated sharply upward at 11.1% annually. The ECB policy rate is 2.65%; compared with inflation, the real interest rate is clearly negative at approximately -1.30 points. This means the ECB is effectively taking a loose stance.

Global Liquidity and Risk Appetite

The rise in long-term US bond yields (30-year at 5.48%, 5-year at 4.98%) and DXY climbing to 103.41 show that global financial conditions are moving toward tightening. VIX rising to 19.30 points to fragility in risk appetite. This combination — high long-term yields, a strong dollar, moderate but rising volatility — creates an unfavorable backdrop for capital flows to emerging markets.

2. Turkey's Macroeconomic Outlook

Are We in the Final Quarter of Disinflation?

In Türkiye, annual CPI at 29.77% is just above the previous 29.73%; monthly inflation came in at 2.58%, above expectations. Core inflation at 28.3% is below the headline but still high. PPI at 27.41% year-on-year — it has converged toward CPI, which implies that cost-driven pressures have been somewhat balanced. The persistence of monthly inflation at 2.58% suggests that disinflation has entered the stage where the "last mile" is the most difficult: for the annual figure to decline, monthly inflation must permanently fall below this level.

Monetary Policy: Cautious Easing

The TCMB cut its policy rate from 37% to 36%. The real rate at +4.80 points is still markedly positive — a stance that supports disinflation but limits domestic demand. Deposit rates at 42.04%, above the policy rate, show that liquidity preference in the banking system favors deposits. Consumer loan rates rising to 66.46% confirm that the credit channel remains costly for households.

Growth and Labor

In the second quarter, quarterly growth accelerated to 1.1% from the previous 0.3%; however, annual growth remained below expectations (2.9%) at 2.3%. Although industrial production at 2.1% month-on-month signals a recovery, the 2.61% annual increase is moderate. Unemployment at 8.5%, participation rate at 52.7% — the labor market is loose but not fragile. Capacity utilization at 74.5% points to a moderate capacity gap.

Confidence Indicators: Warning Signal

The sharp drop in consumer confidence from 91.9 to 84.8, the decline in the economic expectation index from 101.3 to 97.3, and the fall in business confidence from 102 to 99.8 are noteworthy. Together, these three indicators point to a marked deterioration in households' and firms' perception of the future — this should be read as an early signal that domestic demand may weaken in the coming quarters.

3. Domestic Demand, Credit and Liquidity

Interest Rate-Credit Channel

Although the policy rate has been cut to 36%, the rise in the consumer loan rate to 66.46% shows that pass-through works with a lag and asymmetrically. Due to funding costs and risk premiums, banks do not pass on the decline in the policy rate to loan rates at the same speed. Credit growth at 35.29% year-on-year is high but slowing from the previous 36.32% — in other words, credit expansion is losing momentum.

Money Supply and Deposits

M3 money supply is growing at 26.87% year-on-year, below inflation; this means the real money supply is contracting and is a disinflationary signal. The deposit rate being at an attractive level of 42.04% encourages savings to flow into deposits rather than spending — another transmission channel that limits domestic demand.

Outlook for Domestic Demand

The decline in retail sales to 0.1% month-on-month (previous 1.4%) and to 7.7% year-on-year (previous 10.1%) confirms that domestic demand is cooling. Combined with the sharp drop in consumer confidence, it can be foreseen that consumption spending will follow a moderate course in the period ahead. This is positive for disinflation but a downside risk for growth.

4. Global Risk Appetite and Interest Rate Expectations

Market Indicators

The rise in VIX to 19.30 (previous 18.70) shows that risk appetite has deteriorated. The increase in DXY to 103.41 indicates that the dollar is strengthening — this is negative for emerging market currencies and risky assets. The rise in the US 30-year bond yield to 5.48% implies that the long-term risk premium has increased and that global capital is in search of a "safe haven."

Fed Expectations and Implications

The Fed's rate projections are 3.6% for the first year and 3.4% for the second year — meaning the market is pricing a gradual but slow easing. This path means that capital flows to emerging markets will not be strong. For countries offering high real rates such as Türkiye, this creates appeal on the one hand while increasing vulnerability to fluctuations in global risk appetite on the other.

Reflection on BIST

BIST 100 showing a limited increase (0.27%) to 11.842.82 points reflects a resilient course despite the deterioration in global risk appetite. However, the daily return staying at 0.14% in dollar terms shows that the depreciation of the TL is eroding returns. The high course of global rates will continue to put pressure on BIST's valuation multiples.

5. Budget and External Balance

Budget: A Marked Deterioration

In September 2026, the budget balance showed a sharp deviation to -532 billion TL from the previous surplus of 12.9 billion TL. This is a strong signal that fiscal discipline has weakened. Such a large expansion of the budget deficit may stem from either increased spending or lost revenue; in either case, it implies that fiscal policy support for the disinflation process has weakened.

External Balance: Current Account Surplus, Trade Deficit

The current account balance posted a surplus of 2.72 billion dollars, above expectations (2.4 billion dollars) — this is positive. However, the trade balance deteriorated to -6.7 billion dollars from the previous -5.23 billion dollars. Exports were 25.9 billion dollars and imports 32.58 billion dollars. The strong course of tourism revenues at 25.53 billion dollars supports the current account balance; the 0.4% increase in the number of tourists, however, is limited.

Reserves and External Financing

Foreign exchange reserves show a moderate increase to 57.02 billion dollars. However, this level is still limited in terms of external financing vulnerability. The rise in the real effective exchange rate to 107.76 shows that the TL has appreciated in real terms — a development that could adversely affect export competitiveness.

6. Energy, Commodities, Foreign Exchange and Bond Markets

Energy and Commodities

Brent crude is trading high at 95.84 dollars/barrel; WTI at 88.56 dollars. Energy prices remaining at this level puts pressure on the current account balance and inflation for net energy importers such as Türkiye. Gold is at 3.886.30 dollars an ounce, historically high; gram gold is 6.290.57 TL. The strong course of gold shows that demand for a hedge against global uncertainty and inflation continues.

Exchange Rates

USD/TRY is at 50.35 and EUR/TRY at 55.55. The TL shows limited depreciation against the dollar (0.13%) and slight appreciation against the euro (-0.10%). The real effective exchange rate showing appreciation at 107.76 implies that the TL is nominally stable but strong in real terms — this is supportive in the fight against inflation on the one hand while creating pressure on exports on the other.

Bond Markets

The rise in the US 30-year bond yield to 5.48% shows that global long-term rates are trading high. This is a factor pushing up Türkiye's external borrowing costs and risk premium. Domestic bond yields are not included in the data set; however, in light of the policy rate and inflation dynamics, it can be stated qualitatively that the bond market is following a cautious course.

7. Transmission Mechanisms: From Macro to Market

Interest-Credit-Domestic Demand Chain

Policy rate 36% → deposit rate 42.04% → consumer loan rate 66.46% → credit growth 35.29% (slowing) → retail sales 0.1% (monthly, weak) → corporate sales under pressure → margins narrowing → valuation multiples compressing. This chain shows that the high interest rate environment is cooling domestic demand, but the cost of credit is still high.

Exchange Rate-Imported Input-Inflation Chain

USD/TRY 50.35 → imported input cost → PPI annual 27.41% → pass-through to CPI 29.77%. The appreciation of the real effective exchange rate somewhat eases this chain; however, a possible nominal depreciation of the exchange rate could push imported input costs and inflation higher.

Global Interest Rate-Capital Flow-Risk Premium Chain

US 30-year bond 5.48% → increase in global risk premium → capital flows to emerging markets weak → Turkey risk premium high → external financing cost rising → reserve accumulation limited → fragility continues.

8. Reflections on BIST 30: Sector Channels

Banking Sector

In a high interest rate environment, banks' net interest margin may widen; however, the slowdown in credit growth and the rise in consumer loan rates to 66.46% may negatively affect credit demand and asset quality. The high course of deposit rates at 42.04% increases funding costs. For the banking sector, the balance will be struck at the delicate point between margin expansion and credit quality.

Industry and Holdings

The monthly 2.1% recovery in industrial production is positive; however, the annual 2.61% is moderate. Energy costs (Brent 95.84 dollars) and imported input costs are pressuring industrial margins. Although holdings provide partial protection thanks to portfolio diversification, weak domestic demand and high financing costs may limit consolidated performance.

Retail and Consumption

The decline in retail sales to 7.7% annually and the drop in consumer confidence to 84.8 are negative for the retail sector. High loan rates limit consumption spending; however, attractive deposit rates encourage savings — this is a two-way pressure for retail.

Who Wins, Who Loses?

In a high interest rate environment, companies with strong net cash positions and low indebtedness win; companies that grow with debt and depend on domestic demand lose. Exporting companies may be negatively affected by the real appreciation of the TL; importing companies, on the other hand, are relatively advantaged.

9. Scenarios and Monitoring Framework

Base Scenario (50%): Gradual Disinflation, Moderate Growth

A gradual decline in monthly inflation from 2.58%, the TCMB continuing cautious rate cuts, and growth remaining in the 2%-3% band. In this scenario, a moderately positive course for BIST 30 and a controlled depreciation of the TL are expected. Developments that would invalidate this: monthly inflation rising above 3% or the budget deficit widening further.

Optimistic Scenario (25%): Rapid Disinflation, Recovery in Risk Appetite

Recovery in global risk appetite (decline in VIX, weakening of DXY), monthly inflation falling below 2%, strengthening of the current account surplus. In this scenario, expansion of valuation multiples for BIST 30 and stabilization of the TL are possible. Development that would invalidate it: the Fed returning to rate hikes or a sharp rise in energy prices.

Pessimistic Scenario (25%): Inflation Inertia, Global Tightening

Monthly inflation remaining sticky at 2.58%, uncontrolled widening of the budget deficit, global rates continuing to rise (US 30-year above 5.48%). In this scenario, rapid depreciation of the TL, an increase in the risk premium, and the risk of a sharp correction in BIST 30. Development that would invalidate it: the TCMB returning to rate hikes or a rapid erosion in reserves.

Indicators to be Monitored

Monthly inflation (CPI and core), TCMB policy rate decisions, budget balance, current account balance, foreign exchange reserves, VIX, DXY, US 30-year bond yield, Brent crude oil, consumer confidence, retail sales, credit growth.

10. Conclusion and Evaluation

The Turkish economy is in the most challenging phase of the disinflation process: annual inflation is high at 29.77%, monthly inflation is sticky at 2.58%, core inflation is below the headline at 28.3% but still high. The TCMB's cautious rate cut (36%) and positive real interest rate (+4.80 points) support disinflation; however, the widening of the budget deficit to -532 billion TL shows that the support from fiscal policy is weakening.

The global environment is unfavorable: sticky inflation in the US (3.7%) and high long-term rates (5.48%), a strong dollar (DXY 103.41) and rising volatility (VIX 19.30) are limiting capital flows to emerging markets. In the Euro Area, weak growth (0.4% quarterly) and high PPI (11.1%) are pressuring external demand.

The outlook for BIST 30 is conditional: moderately positive in the base scenario, risk of a sharp correction in the pessimistic scenario. In a high interest rate environment, companies with strong net cash positions are advantaged; indebted companies dependent on domestic demand are under pressure. The real appreciation of the TL (REK 107.76) negatively affects exporters.

Key indicators to be monitored in the coming period: whether monthly inflation falls below 2%, whether budget discipline is ensured, and whether global risk appetite recovers. These three variables will determine the direction of Turkey's macroeconomic outlook.

Methodology and Terms

  • Signal (general): Stock-based general technical evaluation (STRONG BUY / BUY / NEUTRAL / SELL / STRONG SELL); seen on the board and in the technical screening table.
  • Oscillator vote: Individual BUY/SELL votes of indicators such as RSI, StochK, MACD, CCI, WT; given in the format "11 buy/8 sell" and may conflict with the general signal (for example, a stock may diverge positively in oscillator votes while the board signal is SELL).
  • WT (Wave Trend): short-term cycle (peak/trough) oscillator; generates BUY/SELL signals.
  • Channel (%N): position of the price within the 60-day regression channel; 0% near the channel bottom (trough area), 100% near the channel top (peak area).
  • ADX: Average Directional Index; values above 25 indicate a strong trend, values below 20 indicate weak/sideways movement.
  • r: Pearson correlation coefficient calculated over a 60-day window (trend strength indicator); given in parentheses in the model portfolio table.
  • CASH: the ratio held for precautionary purposes in the model portfolio that is not allocated to equities.
