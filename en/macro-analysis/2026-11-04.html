1. Global Macroeconomic Outlook

US: Resilience and Sticky Inflation

The US economy recorded 2.1% annual and 3.4% quarterly growth in the second quarter of 2026, displaying resilience beyond the "soft landing" scenario. This performance was supported by a 3.2% quarterly rise in consumer spending and a 1.1% monthly growth in durable goods orders. However labor-market signals draw a mixed picture: the unemployment rate rose to 4.2% by September 2026, while NFP (nonfarm payrolls) at 29.000 per month fell well below expectations (80.000). Broad unemployment (U-6) runs at 7.6%, JOLTs job openings fell to 7.18 million, and labor-force participation stayed relatively flat at 61.8%. These data show the labor market has moved out of "overheating" to normalization, but has not yet entered a fragile structure.

Inflation Dynamics and Fed Policy

According to September 2026 data, headline CPI inflation was 0.6 month-on-month and 3.7 year-on-year, while core CPI fell to 2.6 year-on-year. A similar structure prevails in the Fed's preferred PCE index: headline PCE 3.6 year-on-year, core PCE 3.1 year-on-year. These figures indicate that inflation remains stuck above the 29.73 target, with pressure persisting especially in the services and housing sub-groups. The Fed kept the policy rate unchanged at 4.0 at its November 3, 2026 meeting, but the June 2026 dot plot projections, pointing to 3.6 one year ahead and 3.4 two years ahead, reinforced the “higher for longer” approach. The 30-year yield at 5.32, the 5-year at 4.74 and the 3-month at 29.73 on the bond curve signal that the market still sees the long-term inflation premium and borrowing costs as high.

Euro Area: Stagnation Risk and Policy Dilemma

While the ECB holds the policy rate at 2.65%, industrial production in the Eurozone, including Germany as the economy's engine, contracted 0.5% monthly and 1.1% annually in August 2026. GDP growth remained weak at 0.4% quarterly and 1.3% annually in the third quarter. Core inflation was at 2.5% in September 2026 approaching the target, but headline inflation rose to 3.8%, and PPI at 8.2% annually (August 2026) reflected import cost pressures. Consumer confidence fell to -17.9 points in October 2026, the economic sentiment index to 96.9 points, revealing pessimism among households and businesses about upcoming periods. Credit growth (companies 4.2%, households 3.1% annually) runs anemic due to low rate sensitivity and weak demand. In this environment, the ECB is expected to maintain a cautious, data-dependent stance on rate cuts; however, if growth risks gain weight, pressure for early easing may increase.

2. Turkey's Macroeconomic Outlook

Current Phase of the Disinflation Process

According to the data of the Statistical Institute of Türkiye (TÜİK), as of September 2026 consumer price inflation (CPI) fell to a monthly 1.84% and an annual 29.73%, and core CPI (excluding B and C) fell to an annual 28.7%. Producer price inflation (PPI) recorded a monthly 2.07% and an annual 27.38% in the same period. This fall in annual inflation (CPI from 31.51% in August to 29.73% in September) signals that the disinflation process has passed from the "break" (disinflation break) stage to the "sustained disinflation" stage. However, since the monthly rise of 1.84% annualized still shows a trend inflation of around 1.84%, an early victory cannot yet be declared for the single-digit inflation roadmap targeted by the Central Bank (TCMB). The policy rate was cut by 100% basis points from 37% to 36% at the October 2026 meeting; the logic behind this decision is that the real rate (approximately 4.7%) remains in the tight monetarist zone, but a controlled normalization of the nominal rate is being started with the improvement of inflation expectations.

Growth, Employment and Capacity Utilisation

The second quarter 2026 GDP data, showing 2.3% annual and 1.1% quarterly growth, lay bare the demand suppression at home and the weakening of the external demand were managed in a balanced way, yet a course below the potential growth was followed. The industrial production contracted 1.0% monthly and 1.86% annually in August 2026, and the capacity utilization rate stayed at the 74.5% level in October 2026 (below the historical averages). The unemployment rate came in at 8.5% in September 2026 and the economic participation rate at the 52.7% level. The business confidence index fell to 99.8 in October and the consumer confidence to 84.8 points, reflecting the concerns of both the producers and the consumers about the future. While the retail sales grew 1.4% monthly and 10.1% annually in August 2026, it is understood this growth remained relatively weak in real terms (adjusted for inflation) and the consumer demand for durables stayed under the erosion of the purchasing power.

3. Domestic Demand, Credit and Liquidity

Money Supply and Real Liquidity Situation

M3 money supply growth stood at an annual 29.10% as of October 2026, almost exactly matching annual CPI of 29.73%. This indicates that real money supply in the economy shows almost zero growth and liquidity is on the "tight" side. While credit growth was at an annual 35.29% as of September 2026, most of this growth should be assessed as a nominal increase reflecting the inflation effect. Although real credit growth (annual 35.29% - 29.73% ≈ 2.3%) is positive, it follows a controlled course compared with the credit booms of previous years.

Interest Rate Pass-Through and Banking Sector Margins

According to TCMB EVDS data, the deposit rate (predominantly TL time deposits) stands at 36% as of October 2026 and the consumer loan rate at 64.74% as of August 2026. The ~42.925% spread between the policy rate (36%) and the deposit rate reflects the banks' liquidity management cost and risk premium, while the ~15.34% spread between deposits and consumer loans shows a broad margin structure encompassing the banks' credit risk premia, operational costs and deposit insurance premiums. This high credit cost acts as the main transmission channel in suppressing domestic demand by restricting, in particular, housing and consumer durables financing. Banks' net interest margins (NIM) are supported in this structure, but profitability may be suppressed if credit risk costs (Stage 2/3 transitions) rise.

4. Global Risk Appetite and Interest Rate Expectations

Bond Markets and Dollar Index

According to 3 November 2026 data, the US 30-year bond yield is 5,324%, 5-year 4,743%, 3-month 4,025%. The curve shows a "bear steepening" tendency at the long end (long-term yields rising faster than short-term), indicating the market prices that the Fed's pace of rate cuts will be slow and long-term inflation/debt premia will stay high. DXY (Dollar Index) follows a relatively strong course at 101.86. VIX (CBOE Volatility Index) is at 20.81, above historical averages but below the panic threshold (30); this shows the market is in "waiting mode", with risk appetite neither fully closed nor fully open.

Foreign Capital Flows and the BIST Context

That Fed rate projections (dot plot) point to 3.6% for end-2027 means US real rates will remain positive and attractive. In this environment, the risk/reward ratio for "carry trade" (interest rate arbitrage) strategies with portfolio flows into emerging markets (EM) becomes difficult. Although the real policy rate for Türkiye (~4.7%) remains attractive, exchange rate risk (USD/TRY 49.99, EUR/TRY 55.98) and policy uncertainty limit foreign investors' entry into BIST and government bonds (GDDS). The slight rise of BIST 100 to 11,023 points and BIST 30 to 13,754 points (0.56%, 0.42%) suggests that domestic investors (funds, individuals) are net buyers and the foreign selling pressure is being balanced.

5. Budget and External Balance

Budget Performance and Fiscal Discipline

According to Treasury data, the September 2026 budget balance posted a deficit of 532 billion TL, a dramatic decline compared with the previous month (August), when there was a surplus of 12.9 billion TL. This development shows that year-end targets (the official program likely points to a deficit of around 5%-6 of GDP) are at risk and that spending quality has deteriorated (current spending / investment spending ratio) due to the real decline in tax revenues and the explosion in interest expenses (domestic borrowing costs). Although the financing strategy of the fiscal authority (Treasury) has shifted toward long-term TL-denominated bond issues (e.g., 2-3 year maturities) in line with market conditions, the real interest cost continues to strain the budget.

Current Account, Reserves and Tourism Shield

According to August 2026 data, the current account closed with a surplus of 4,053 billion USD (above the 3.6 billion USD estimate). The main engine of this surplus is tourism revenues, reaching 25.53 billion USD in the third quarter of 2026 (previous quarter 15.87 billion USD). However, the October 2026 trade deficit of 6.7 billion USD (Exports 25.9 billion $ vs Imports 32.58 billion $) is under the pressure of energy prices (Brent 104.33 $/barrel) and gold imports. The Central Bank's gross FX reserves fell to 59.04 billion USD as of the week of 30 October 2026 (previous week 62.15 billion $), showing that the net reserve position (excluding swaps) remains fragile and the external financing need (short-term debt payments + covering the current deficit) remains dependent on portfolio flows and banks' front-loaded loans.

6. Energy, Commodities, Foreign Exchange and Bond Markets

Oil, Gold and Exchange Rate Inflation Interaction

While Brent crude trades at 104.33 USD/barrel, WTI is at 96.66 USD/barrel, the price spread does not narrow. High oil prices directly drive Türkiye's import input costs (PPI monthly 2.07%). Gold per ounce remains at historical highs at 3,932 USD, gram gold at 6,319 TL. Gold's strength stems from central banks' reserve diversification (especially China and emerging countries), geopolitical risk premia, and low real rate expectations. For Türkiye, gold plays a dual role as an item in the current account (gold imports) and as an inflation hedge for domestic investors (value increase in TL).

Real Effective Rate and External Competitiveness

The Real Effective Exchange Rate (REER) stands at an index level of 105.45 as of September 2026 (on a 2003=100 basis). This level shows that, despite the TL's nominal depreciation, due to the inflation differential (Türkiye 30% vs US 3.7%, EU 3.8%) it is still moving in an "overvalued" or at least "balanced" band in real terms, and that the exporter's competitive strength depends on a cost advantage (labor cost, energy efficiency) rather than an exchange rate advantage. The relatively sideways course at the USD/TRY 49.99 and EUR/TRY 55.98 levels is ensured by the TCMB's market operations (sales/purchases when deemed necessary) and the banks' TL liquidity management with pre-funding credit flows.

7. Transmission Mechanisms: From Macro to Market

Interest Rate Channel: From Policy Rate to Company Valuations

The chain works as follows: TCMB Policy Rate (36%) -> Bank Deposit Cost (42.9%) / Loan Rate (64.7% consumer, lower in commercial loans but still high) -> Actual Credit Cost (Real Rate ~4%-5) -> Domestic Demand (Consumption + Investment) -> Corporate Sales Volume and Pricing Power -> Gross Margins (Input costs PPI 27.38% vs Output prices CPI 29%) -> Net Profit -> Discount Rate in DCF Valuation (WACC). The high discount rate, by suppressing the present value of future cash flows, pulls the Corporate Values / Sales (EV/Sales) and P/E multiples on the BIST below the historical averages. Except the banking sector, companies with high indebtedness (Net Debt / EBITDA > 2-3x) are those most affected by this channel.

Exchange Rate Channel: From Imported Input Costs to Inflation

The USD/TRY (50.0) and EUR/TRY (56.0) levels determine the costs of imported raw materials, intermediates and investment machinery. The PPI annually at 27.38% remaining below the CPI's 29.73% shows either that producers cannot fully pass their cost increase into sales prices (margin squeeze) or that the CPI is being dragged by rent/service/labor costs with their enormous weight. If oil (Brent 104$) and the rate (50 TL) stay at these levels, monthly inflation can move in the 1.5%-2.0 band until January 2027; however, if a rate shock (for example 55 TL) or an oil shock (120$) comes, the disinflation trend is interrupted and strains the TCMB's hand.

Global Liquidity Channel: Foreign Capital and Risk Premium

US 10-year bond yield (in the dataset 5-year 4.74%, 30-year 5.32%, extrapolated ~4.5%-4.8 band) and DXY (101.86) levels determine the "risk-free rate". Türkiye's CDS premium (not in the dataset, qualitatively: estimated ~350-400 bp) is added to this base rate to calculate the "İmtiyazsız Faiz" (privilege-less rate). For a foreign investor, TL return = (TL interest - expected currency loss). If currency expectation is 20%-25 annually (deposit rate 43% - currency loss 25% = real 4%), this is still well above the US bond yield (4.5%). However, the uncertainty of the "currency loss expectation" (volatility) is the main factor keeping foreigners away. VIX at 20.8 shows this risk appetite is at a medium level.

8. Reflections on BIST 30: Sector Channels

Banking: Interest Margin and Asset Quality Balance

Banks (about 36%-40 of BIST 30 weight) preserve profitability in a high policy-rate environment through net-interest-margin (NIM) expansion. Deposit costs are at 43%, loan yields (commercial) in the 50%-55 band. However, positive real rates weaken borrowers' repayment capacity; if KHK (credit loss coverage) and Stage 2 loans rise, risk costs (CoR) may eat the margin expansion. Asset-quality indicators (NPL ratio, coverage ratio) will be the most critical monitoring point in coming quarters.

Industry and Exporters: Rate Advantage vs Demand Collapse

Export-oriented industrial companies such as automotive, white goods, steel and chemicals benefit from the EUR/TRY 56 level. However, the annual 1.1% contraction in Eurozone industrial production and the economic expectation index at 96.9 indicate weakening order books and limited pricing power. The debt structures of these companies (mostly KHK-protected/USD-based) are exposed to currency risk; TL appreciation (REER 105+) erodes margins.

Retail and Consumption: Real Income Erosion

Companies selling food retail, textiles and durable consumer goods (white goods, electronics) face demand weakness due to consumer confidence at 84.8, real wage growth (not in the data; qualitative: high probability of staying below inflation) and the consumer loan rate at 64.7%. While retail sales contract in real terms, companies try to protect margins through inventory management and operational efficiency (opex control).

Holdings: Cyclical Portfolio Manager

Koç, Sabancı, İş Bankası (company) holdings like are the weighted average of the cyclical performance of the bank, energy, automotive and insurance assets in their portfolios. In the high interest environment bank profits rise, the energy (refinery/petrochemical) margins move volatily depending on the oil price and the currency. The holding discount (NAV discount) is inversely proportional to the market risk appetite (VIX, DXY).

9. Scenarios and Monitoring Framework

Base Scenario (Probability 2.3%): Gradual Disinflation and Selective Growth

Inflation runs in the monthly 1.5%-2.0 band, year-end CPI falls to the 1.84%-26 band. The TCMB cuts the policy rate to the 30%-32 band in the first quarter of 2027. The current account imbalance is kept at a manageable level through import controls other than tourism and gold. Reserves move in the $60-65 billion band. The BIST 100 is flat in real terms and keeps pace with inflation nominally.

  • Invalidation Data: If October CPI comes in above 3% month-on-month or reserves fall below $55 billion.

Optimistic Scenario (Probability 36%): Global soft landing and early rate cut

The Fed begins rate cuts from March 2027 (DXY below 101.856, 10y UST below 3.5%). Oil falls below 80 $. In Türkiye, core inflation declines rapidly (monthly below 1%), and the TCMB cuts the rate to 25% in mid-2027. Foreign flows return to BIST and GDDS (10-15 billion $ net inflow during the year). Reserves test 75 billion $.

  • Invalidation Data: US core PCE stays above 3.5% annually or geopolitical risk (Middle East/South Caucasus) holds oil above 110$.

Pessimistic Scenario (Probability 25%): Stagflation Risk and External Shock

US inflation stays sticky (Core CPI 3%+), the Fed holds "higher for longer", DXY rises above 105. Oil becomes 115+. Türkiye's current-account deficit rises to 5-6 billion $ monthly in Q4 2026/Q1 2027. Reserves fall below 50 billion $. The TCMB stops rate cuts/raises rates. The TL tests the 55-60 band. BIST 100 tests levels like 11,024-9,500 points.

  • Invalidation Data: If November CPI monthly exceeds 2.5%, core CPI annual remains above 30%, or the Treasury's monthly budget deficit stays above 300 billion TL.

Critical Indicators to be Monitored (Coming 4-6 Weeks)

  1. CPI/PPI (5 November and 3 December releases): Monthly trend, core subgroups (Services, Rent, Labour).
  2. TCMB Monetary Policy Committee (December 2026): Interest rate decision, outlook report, inflation forecast revision.
  3. Central Bank Weekly Reserve Data (Fridays): Change in net reserves, swap usage.
  4. US CPI/PCE and Fed Remarks: November/December data, Powell's "data dependent" tone.
  5. Brent Oil and DXY: 100$ and 102 psychological/technical resistance/support levels.

10. Conclusion and Evaluation

The economy of Türkiye is, as of October 2026, in the middle of a demanding disinflation process. Annual inflation has begun to fall below the 30% band and the real policy rate has been pulled to positive levels. This is a necessary but insufficient condition for macroeconomic stability. The sustainability of the process depends on the evolution of three critical variables: (1) Breaking the stickiness in services and rent inflation ( this requires managing the indexation mechanisms and the expectations), (2) Supporting the external balance through structural elements other than tourism and gold (export diversification, foreign direct investment), (3) Aligning fiscal discipline (control of the budget deficit) with monetary policy.

The global environment is shaped by the US economy's surprising resilience and the Fed's patient stance, which curbs risk appetite flowing into emerging markets. For BIST, this creates an environment that generates "alpha" (selective stock performance) while "beta" (market direction) is flat/low risk premium. For investors, the focus should be on companies with solid balance sheets (low net debt/EBITDA, TL revenue / TL expense balance), pricing power (exporter or market leader), and free cash flow generation.
