1. Global Macroeconomic Outlook

US: Growth, Inflation and Policy

The US economy recorded 3.4% quarterly and 2.1% annual growth in the third quarter. Monthly inflation was 0.6%, annual inflation 3.7%. Core PCE inflation was 3.1% annually, core inflation 2.6%. While labor-market conditions look strong, the September unemployment rate was 4.2% and new jobless claims remained low at 189.000. While the policy rate was held at 4.0%, the real rate stayed around 0.30%. Consumer spending rose 3.2% in the second quarter; retail sales showed a 4.53% annual increase in July. Manufacturing and services PMIs stayed in expansion at 55.6 and 54.1 points, respectively.

Euro Area: Slowdown and Price Dynamics

The euro area recorded quarterly growth of 0.4 percent in the third quarter; annual growth stood at 1.3 percent as of the second quarter. In October, annual inflation was 4.0 percent and core inflation 2.5 percent. PPI stood at 11.1 percent year-on-year. The policy rate was kept unchanged at 2.65 percent, while the real interest rate remained negative at around 1.35 percent. The unemployment rate stayed low at 6.5 percent in September. Industrial production stood at 1.1 percent year-on-year, while retail sales were at 0.8 percent. The current account posted a surplus of 7.7 billion euros but declined from the previous month's 35.9 billion euros. A deficit of 16.1 billion euros was recorded in the trade balance. The economic expectations index was 96.9, while consumer confidence remained negative at -17.9 points.

Global Liquidity and Risk Appetite

While the VIX index rose to the 28.18 level, it showed a 69.66 percent increase from the previous value of 16.61. This points to a marked risk aversion in the markets. The DXY at the 101.60 level, that is, the dollar index offered a strong appearance. US bond yields came in at 3.987 percent at 3 months, 4.608 percent at 5 years and 5.251 percent at 30 years. The yield curve remaining positively sloped shows the long-term risk premium rose. The Fed's rate projections were at 3.6 percent for the first year and 3.4 percent for the second year. These data show that global liquidity conditions are tending to contract and the pressure on risky assets is increasing.

2. Turkey's Macroeconomic Outlook

Inflation and Disinflation Phase

In Türkiye, monthly inflation in October came in at 2.58 percent and annual inflation at 29.77 percent. Core inflation remained at 28.3 percent annually and the PPI at 27.41 percent. These data show that price pressure is still high and the disinflation process is at an early stage. The TCMB cut the policy rate from 37 to 36 percent. The real rate remained positive at approximately 4.80 percent against the policy and approximately 6.23 percent when calculated over inflation. A positive real rate shows monetary policy is tight, but the slow pace of inflation's decline signals that this tightness is insufficient or that input costs continue.

Growth and Labour Market

As of the second quarter, Türkiye's quarterly growth was at the 1.1% level and its annual growth at 2.3%. In September the unemployment rate was recorded as 8.5%. The economic participation rate came in at 52.7% and the capacity utilization rate at 74.5%. The industrial production, in turn, showed a monthly decrease of 1.0% and an annual one of 1.8618% in August. The business confidence index showed a regression from the previous months with 99.8, the consumer confidence with 84.8 and the economic expectations index with 97.3 points. This data set lays bare the domestic demand is beginning to weaken and the producers cannot fully utilize their capacities. The growth carries a slowdown risk through the consumption and investment channels.

3. Domestic Demand, Credit and Liquidity

Transmission from Policy Rate to Credit Cost

While the TCMB policy rate stands at 36 percent, the deposit rate came in at 42.752 percent and the consumer loan rate at 66.46 percent. These high credit costs restrict the financing access of consumers and companies. While credit growth was at the annual level of 35.289%,2891, a slight slowdown was observed from the previous value of 36.3222 percent. Through the interest channel, a high policy rate first raises deposit and loan rates; then consumption and investment spending decline. This chain directly affects companies' sales and profit margins. As a result, pressure is created on domestic demand and economic activity slows.

Deposits, Money Supply and Consumer Credit

M3 money supply stood at 28.845 percent annually, compared with 29.7792 percent the previous month. This shows that the increase in money supply has slowed, but a still-high liquidity environment continues. Retail sales rose 1.4 percent month-on-month and 10.1 percent year-on-year in August. The consumer confidence index fell to 84.8 points, indicating that households' willingness to spend has decreased. High deposit rates encourage attracting deposits, while high loan rates cause declines in new credit demand. This may also affect banks' lending attitudes.

4. Global Risk Appetite and Interest Rate Expectations

Market Indicators and Risk Premium

While the VIX index rose to the 28.18 level, the DXY stayed at the 101.60 level. This combination shows global risk appetite is weak and investors are turning to safety. The BIST 100 index stayed at 11,333.27 points and the BIST 30 at 14,101.0 points; daily returns were negative at -1.26 percent and -1.60 percent respectively. Dollar-based returns were worse. This situation reduces the probability of foreign capital flows toward Türkiye and raises the country's risk premium. The fall on the BIST shows the global risk aversion is affecting the local market too.

Yield Curve and Expectations

The US 3-month bond yield was at percent 3.987, 5-year yield at percent 4.608 and 30-year yield at percent 5.251. The yield curve staying higher at the long end reflects long-term inflation and risk concerns. The Fed's rate projections indicate it expects a rate cut in the short term but the policy rate stays at percent 4.0. This shows the US real rate may remain positive and global capital flows may continue toward the US. For Türkiye, this may mean external financing costs stay high and the TL carries depreciation risk.

5. Budget and External Balance

Budget Situation and Financing

In September, the budget balance recorded a deficit of 532.0 billion TRY; the previous month had a surplus of 12.9 billion TRY. This sharp shift indicates budget expansion and increased financing needs. If the deficit financing is directed toward domestic borrowing or external sources, it may raise interest expenses and exchange rate sensitivity. Since the budget/GDP ratio is not included in the data block, this ratio should be assessed qualitatively. A large budget deficit may also constrain the independence of monetary policy.

Current Account and External Financing

The three-month current account for August closed with a surplus of 4.053 billion $; the previous value was 0.036 billion $. Exports were 25.9 billion $ in October, while imports were 32.58 billion $. A trade deficit of 6.7 billion $ was recorded. Tourism revenues reached 25.53 billion $ in the third quarter; the previous quarter was 15.87 billion $. FX reserves fell to 56.09 billion $, down from the previous week's 59.04 billion $. While the current surplus and tourism revenues provide positive support in the external balance, declining reserves and a large budget deficit may increase external financing fragility.

6. Energy, Commodities, Foreign Exchange and Bond Markets

Oil, Gold and Currency

Brent crude was at 98.71 USD/barrel, WTI at 90.80 USD/barrel; both rose from the previous day. Gold was at 4,099.30 USD per ounce, gram gold at 6,595.59 TL. USD/TRY was at 50.04, EUR/TRY at 56.24. The real effective exchange rate index was at 107.76, the previous value was 105.1. This shows the TL is appreciating in real terms. High energy prices continue to push local inflation up through imported input costs. The currency's rise reinforces this effect.

Bond Yields and Real Interest

US bond yields were at percent 3.987 for 3-month, percent 4.608 for 5-year and percent 5.251 for 30-year. Since no bond yield data for Türkiye were found in the data block, no quantitative statement should be used about local bond yields. However, US yields staying high may affect Türkiye's external borrowing costs. Türkiye's real interest being positive at about percent 4.80 may affect return expectations in the local bond market. Energy prices and exchange-rate movements are also factors affecting real returns through inflation.

7. Transmission Mechanisms: From Macro to Market

Interest Rate Channel

The policy rate first affects deposit and loan interest rates. In Türkiye, the policy rate is at 36 percent, the deposit rate at 42.752 percent and the consumer loan rate at 66.46 percent. High credit costs reduce consumer spending and company investments. This lowers companies' sales and squeezes their profit margins. Finally, low profit margins and high discount rates negatively affect asset valuations. This chain forms a transmission mechanism from macroeconomic policy to market valuations.

Exchange Rate and Imported Input Channel

USD/TRY was at 50.04 and EUR/TRY at 56.24. While the real effective exchange rate index stood at 107.76, the previous value was 105.1. The rate increase raises the costs of imported energy and raw materials. High input costs push up producer prices (PPI) and later consumer prices (CPI). In Türkiye, annual inflation is at 29.77 percent and the PPI at 27.41 percent. This mechanism makes it difficult to keep inflation under control in rate-volatile environments. The appreciation of the real rate, on the other hand, can reduce exporters' competitive advantage.

Global Interest Rate and Capital Flow Channel

The US policy rate was at percent 4.0, VIX at 28.18. High global rates and risk aversion increase capital flows to developed countries. In emerging countries, capital outflows or a slowdown in inflows may occur. This situation raises country risk premia and lowers local asset prices. For Türkiye, this may have been reflected as a decline in BIST and depreciation of the TL. Global liquidity tightening is a factor that increases local markets' sensitivity.

8. Reflections on BIST 30: Sector Channels

Banking and Finance Sector

A high policy rate can support banks' net interest margins but also raises deposit costs. While the deposit rate is at the 42.752 percent level, credit growth began to slow at 35.289%,2891. This can increase the pressure on credit quality and credit demand. The high real rate leaves bank stocks in a mixed appearance; while interest income rises, credit risk also increases. The banking weight in BIST 30 directly affects the sector's index performance.

Industry, Holding and Retail

Industrial production showed an annual decrease of 1.8618 percent; the capacity utilization rate remained at the 74.5 percent level. High energy prices (Brent 98.71 USD) and the rate increase raise industrial companies' input costs. Retail sales rose 10.1 percent annually but consumer confidence fell to 84.8 points. Holding structures may reflect these sectoral weaknesses in their consolidated profits. In a high interest environment, preference may increase for assets offering bond-like returns such as deposits and bonds; this can reduce equity demand. Export-oriented sectors, on the other hand, may be negatively affected by the appreciation of the real exchange rate.

9. Scenarios and Monitoring Framework

Base Scenario

In the base scenario, inflation in Türkiye declines slowly, the TCMB holds the policy rate at percent 36 or makes slight cuts. Growth continues at around percent 2, the current account surplus remains, but reserves continue to decline. Globally, US growth stays strong, inflation stays above target and the Fed cuts rates slowly. In this scenario, BIST is expected to perform sideways or slightly negatively.

Optimistic and Pessimistic Scenarios

In the optimistic scenario, the inflation falls faster than expected, the global risk appetite increases (the VIX falls), the foreign capital inflows increase and the reserves recover. In the pessimistic scenario, however, the inflation rises up, the budget deficit widens, the reserves rapidly decrease and the global risk escape intensifies. In this case, a value loss of the TL and deeper losses in the BIST can be experienced. The validity of the scenarios will be tested in the coming months with the TÜİK inflation data, the TCMB rate decisions and the currency reserve movements.

Indicators to be Monitored

Among the fundamental indicators to be monitored in the coming period should be the TÜİK CPI and PPI data, the TCMB policy rate, the currency reserves, the current balance, the developments in the budget balance and, globally, the VIX, the DXY and the US bond returns. The BIST 100 and BIST 30 returns will be important in monitoring the local risk perception. The abrupt changes in these indicators will help determine which of the scenarios defined above is more likely.

10. Conclusion and Evaluation

While Türkiye is in a tight monetary policy with a positive real rate, the slow pace of inflation's decline shows the disinflation process is at an early stage. Growth is at a modest level, there are signs of weakening in domestic demand, and in the external balance the current account surplus and tourism revenues provide positive support while declining reserves and a large budget deficit create fragility. Globally, while the US economy looks strong, inflation staying above the target and the VIX being at a high level create a challenging environment for risky assets. BIST 30, directly connected to these macroeconomic channels, shows sectoral differences, and in the high interest environment sectors such as banking, industry and retail are exposed to different effects. In the monitoring framework, inflation, rates, reserves and global risk indicators will play a critical role.

Methodology and Terms

  • Signal (general): Stock-based general technical evaluation (STRONG BUY / BUY / NEUTRAL / SELL / STRONG SELL); seen on the board and in the technical screening table.
  • Oscillator vote: Individual BUY/SELL votes of indicators such as RSI, StochK, MACD, CCI, WT; given in the format "11 buy/8 sell" and may conflict with the general signal (for example, a stock may diverge positively in oscillator votes while the board signal is SELL).
  • WT (Wave Trend): short-term cycle (peak/trough) oscillator; generates BUY/SELL signals.
  • Channel (%N): position of the price within the 60-day regression channel; 0% near the channel bottom (trough area), 100% near the channel top (peak area).
  • ADX: Average Directional Index; values above 25 indicate a strong trend, values below 20 indicate weak/sideways movement.
  • r: Pearson correlation coefficient calculated over a 60-day window (trend strength indicator); given in parentheses in the model portfolio table.
  • CASH: the portion not allocated to stocks in the model portfolio, held for protection.
