1. Global Macroeconomic Outlook

US: Resilient Growth, Sticky Inflation

The US economy grew 3.4% quarter-on-quarter in the third quarter of 2026, markedly above estimates; annual growth stood at 2.1%. This picture shows how resilient demand remains in an environment where monetary policy is still restrictive. Consumer spending rising 3.2% quarter-on-quarter and retail sales jumping 1.5% month-on-month indicate that household balance sheets are still holding up. However, there is no relief on the inflation front: headline annual inflation is 3.7%, core PCE 3.1%, and PPI 5.5% year-on-year. This points to a pricing regime in which goods inflation is reviving.

Labor Market and the Fed's Dilemma

The unemployment rate fell to 4.1% while nonfarm payrolls came in at 165 thousand, well above expectations; however, U-6 broad unemployment rose to 7.9%. This dual picture shows that the labor market has evolved into a split structure of "low unemployment but rising underemployment." The Fed's policy rate is steady at 4.0%; the real rate is just 0.29%, essentially neutral. The Fed's rate projections are 3.6% for the first year and 3.4% for the second year — meaning there is limited alignment between the market and the Fed regarding rate cut expectations. Inflation expectations (4.0% for 1 year; 3.3% for 5 years) running above target are narrowing the Fed's room for maneuver.

Euro Area: Weak Industry, High PPI

In the Euro Area, growth is moderate at 0.4% quarter-on-quarter; 1.3% year-on-year. Industrial production continues to contract at -1.5% year-on-year; retail sales at -0.7% month-on-month confirm that domestic demand is weakening. The most striking data is PPI: 11.1% year-on-year — energy-driven cost pressure is back on the agenda. Nevertheless, headline inflation is 4.0% and core 2.6%. The ECB policy rate is 2.65% with a real rate of -1.30%; in other words, the ECB is still taking a "behind the curve" stance. Unemployment at 6.5% is historically low, and the current account surplus is €27.5 billion — external demand is offsetting the Euro Area's weak domestic demand.

Global Liquidity and Risk Appetite

The US 30-year Treasury yield is 5.29%, the 5-year 4.71%, and the 3-month 4.17% — the yield curve runs high at the long end. DXY at 103.85 signals a strong dollar. VIX at 26.36 is in the "medium-high" stress zone. This combination points to a regime in which global liquidity is expensive and risk appetite is selective: capital is flowing toward high-yield but low-risk-premium assets.

2. Turkey's Macroeconomic Outlook

Are We in the Final Quarter of Disinflation?

According to TÜİK, annual CPI is 29.77% and monthly 2.58%, above the expectation (2.4%). Core inflation at 28.3% is below the headline but still high. PPI is 27.41% year-on-year and 1.65% month-on-month — producer prices converging toward consumer prices shows that cost pressure persists. Monthly inflation at 2.58% tracing a path above 30% annualized confirms that the "last mile" of disinflation is the most challenging phase. The slight rise in annual inflation from 29.73% to 29.77% implies that the base effect is no longer supportive.

Monetary Policy: Cautious Easing

The TCMB policy rate was cut to 36.0% (previous 37.0%). The real rate at 4.80% is still clearly positive — a stance necessary for disinflation but restrictive for growth. The deposit rate at 41.86% is above the policy rate; this reflects banks' liquidity preference and risk premium. The TCMB's cautious easing cycle is rational for anchoring inflation expectations; however, monthly inflation coming in at 2.58% may lead to questions about the pace of cuts.

Growth and Labor

In the second quarter, growth was 1.1% quarter-on-quarter and 2.3% year-on-year, below the estimate (2.9%). Industrial production is recovering at 2.1% month-on-month; capacity utilization at 74.4% is moderate. Unemployment is 8.5%, and the participation rate is 52.7%. While consumer confidence rose to 86.8, the economic sentiment index fell to 97.3 (previous 101.3) — in other words, households are optimistic while the business world is cautious. This divergence shows that domestic demand is supported through the consumption channel but investment appetite is weak.

3. Domestic Demand, Credit and Liquidity

Credit Cost Channel

The consumer loan rate is 66.46%, above the previous 64.74% — nearly double the policy rate. This spread shows that banks' risk appetite is low and that credit demand has broken down among rate-sensitive segments. Credit growth at 35.29% year-on-year is high but slowing (previous 36.32%). M3 money supply is 28.17% year-on-year — just below inflation, meaning the real money supply is contracting. This confirms that the tight monetary stance is also working through the quantity channel.

Outlook for Domestic Demand

Retail sales nearly stalled at 0.1% month-on-month; at 7.7% year-on-year, far below inflation — meaning real retail is contracting. The contradiction between consumer confidence rising to 86.8 and retail sales stalling shows that confidence is not translating into "spending intent." High loan rates prevent households from consuming through borrowing; deposit rates being attractive at 41.86% encourages saving. This signals that domestic demand will follow a moderate course in the coming quarters.

4. Global Risk Appetite and Interest Rate Expectations

Market Pricing

VIX at 26.36 is in the "anxious" zone; DXY at 103.85 is strong. US 30-year at 5.29% — long-term rates staying high restricts capital flows to emerging markets. US rate projections (Year 1 3.6%; Year 2 3.4%) confirm the Fed's "higher for longer" stance. In this environment, risk appetite is selective: investors prefer high-yield but liquid assets.

Reflection on BIST

BIST 30 took its share of the global risk-off with a -1.26% daily return and -1.39% in dollar terms. VIX trading above 26 implies that appetite for emerging market equities will remain limited. However, Turkey's positive real interest rate (4.80%) makes TL assets attractive for carry trade; this could be a selective supporting factor for BIST.

5. Budget and External Balance

Budget Discipline

The October budget balance improved to -481 billion TRY from -532 billion TRY previously. However, this is still a high deficit; the budget/GDP ratio is not in the data set, and qualitatively it is possible to speak of an "expansionary fiscal policy." The budget deficit remaining high shows that fiscal policy is not supportive in the disinflation process — this increases the TCMB's burden.

External Balance: Current Account Surplus, Trade Deficit

The current account balance posted a 2.72 billion $ surplus, below the previous 4.05 billion $ but still positive. The trade balance runs a deficit of -6.7 billion $ (previous -5.23 billion $). Exports 25.9 billion $, imports 32.58 billion $ — the foreign trade deficit is widening. Tourism revenues are strong at 25.53 billion $, but the number of tourists is contracting at -5.23% year-on-year; that is, the revenue increase is price-driven, not volume. Foreign exchange reserves are rising moderately to 59.25 billion $. The current account surplus remaining positive despite the trade deficit shows dependence on service revenues (tourism, transportation) — this is a fragile balance.

6. Energy, Commodities, Foreign Exchange and Bond Markets

Energy and Commodities

Brent at 88.41 $/barrel, WTI at 81.64 $/barrel — energy prices remain high. Gold fell -2.62% daily to 3.555.70 $/ounce; gram gold at 5.789.27 TL, -2.49%. The decline in gold is consistent with DXY staying strong and real rates remaining positive. High energy prices pressure Turkey's import bill and the PPI — part of the 27.41% annual PPI is energy-driven.

Exchange Rate and Bonds

USD/TRY flat at 50.64; EUR/TRY at 55.52. The real effective exchange rate at 107.76 is above the previous 105.1 — meaning the TL is appreciating in real terms. This means support for disinflation but pressure on export competitiveness. In terms of the exchange rate-inflation interaction: the TL's real appreciation limits inflation by lowering imported input costs; however, it squeezes exporter margins.

7. Transmission Mechanisms: From Macro to Market

Interest Rate Channel

Policy rate 36% → deposit rate 41.86% → consumer loan rate 66.46% → domestic demand (retail 0.1%) → company sales → margins → valuation. Every link in this chain is tight: deposit rates encourage saving, loan rates discourage borrowing, retail is stalled. Company sales are contracting in real terms; margins are squeezed between cost pressure (PPI 27.41%) and weak demand. In terms of valuation: the high discount rate (real interest rate 4.80%) pressures the present value of equities.

Exchange Rate Channel

Real appreciation of the TL (REER 107.76) → imported input costs fall → inflation is contained; however, exporter margins are squeezed. This is a two-way effect for industrial companies: cost advantage but competitive disadvantage.

Global Interest Rate Channel

US 30-year at 5.29% → global risk premium → foreign capital flow → Turkey risk premium. Long-term US rates staying high restricts capital flow to Turkey; carry trade is attractive but volatile.

8. Reflections on BIST 30: Sector Channels

Banking

In a high interest rate environment, banks' net interest margins widen; the spread between the 41.86% deposit rate and the 66.46% loan rate supports profitability. However, slowing credit growth (35.29%) and non-performing loan risk are pressure factors in the medium term. The banking sector is the "winner" of high rates but has the potential to be the "loser" of slowing growth.

Industrial production is recovering at 2.1% month-on-month but moderate at 2.61% year-on-year. Capacity utilization is at a medium level of 74.4%. Export-oriented industry is negatively affected by the TL's real appreciation; domestic-demand-focused industry by the high loan rate. Energy-intensive sectors are under cost pressure with Brent at 88.41 $.

Holding and Retail

Holdings are more resilient to macro shocks thanks to portfolio diversification; however, subsidiary valuations are affected by the high discount rate. Retail is negatively affected by the contraction in real sales (7.7% year-on-year, below inflation) and the high consumer loan rate. Consumer confidence rising to 86.8 is a limited glimmer of hope for retail.

9. Scenarios and Monitoring Framework

Base Scenario (50%): Moderate Disinflation, Slow Growth

Inflation declining to the 29.77%-30% annual band, the TCMB continuing cautious cuts, growth remaining in the 2%-3% band. In this scenario, BIST exhibits selective stock performance; banks and export-oriented industry stand out. The development that would invalidate it: monthly inflation rising above 3% or growth turning negative.

Optimistic Scenario (25%): Rapid Disinflation

Inflation declining below 29.77%, the TCMB cutting rates faster, the risk premium falling. In this scenario, valuation multiples expand on BIST; domestic-demand-focused sectors (retail, holdings) stand out. The development that would invalidate it: deterioration in global risk appetite (VIX >30) or rapid depreciation in the exchange rate.

Pessimistic Scenario (25%): Inflation Accelerates Again

Inflation rising above 35% as energy prices (Brent >95 $) and the exchange rate (USD/TRY >55) rise, forcing the TCMB to raise rates. In this scenario, a sharp correction on BIST; banks and domestic-demand-focused sectors are most affected. The development that would invalidate it: inflation declining below 25%.

Indicators to be Monitored

Monthly inflation (especially core), TCMB policy rate decisions, exchange rate level, global risk appetite (VIX, DXY), US bond yields, current account balance, budget balance, number of tourists.

10. Conclusion and Evaluation

The Turkish economy is in the most challenging phase of disinflation: annual inflation is high at 29.77%, monthly inflation is above target at 2.58%, and the real interest rate is tight at 4.80%. Growth is moderate (2.3%), domestic demand is weak (retail 0.1%), and the external balance is fragile (current account surplus but the trade deficit is widening). The global environment is not supportive: sticky inflation in the US, high long-term interest rates, a strong dollar. This combination requires "selective optimism" for BIST: banks and exporting industry may be relatively positively affected by high interest rates and real exchange rate appreciation; domestic demand-focused sectors, however, are under pressure.

The critical question in the period ahead is this: can the TCMB continue its rate-cut cycle despite monthly inflation coming in at 2.58%? If it does, the real interest rate narrows and domestic demand revives; but if inflation expectations deteriorate, there is a risk of credibility loss. If it does not, growth slows further. This balance will be the main theme of the coming quarters.

Methodology and Terms

  • Signal (general): Stock-based general technical evaluation (STRONG BUY / BUY / NEUTRAL / SELL / STRONG SELL); seen on the board and in the technical screening table.
  • Oscillator vote: Individual BUY/SELL votes of indicators such as RSI, StochK, MACD, CCI, WT; given in the format "11 buy/8 sell" and may conflict with the general signal (for example, a stock may diverge positively in oscillator votes while the board signal is SELL).
  • WT (Wave Trend): short-term cycle (peak/trough) oscillator; generates BUY/SELL signals.
  • Channel (%N): position of the price within the 60-day regression channel; 0% near the channel bottom (trough area), 100% near the channel top (peak area).
  • ADX: Average Directional Index; values above 25 indicate a strong trend, values below 20 indicate weak/sideways movement.
  • r: Pearson correlation coefficient calculated over a 60-day window (trend strength indicator); given in parentheses in the model portfolio table.
  • CASH: the portion not allocated to stocks in the model portfolio, held for protection.
