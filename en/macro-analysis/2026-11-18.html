1. Global Macroeconomic Outlook

US: Resilient Growth, Sticky Inflation

The US economy grew 3.4% quarter-on-quarter in the third quarter of 2026, performing markedly above the 1.5% market expectation; annual growth came in at 2.1%. This picture shows that demand is not cooling easily in an environment where monetary policy is still restrictive. The labor market confirms this resilience: the unemployment rate is 4.1%, and the change in non-farm payrolls is 165 thousand, well above expectations. However, improvement on the inflation front is slow: annual CPI is 3.7%, core PCE 3.1%, and the PCE deflator 3.6%. Producer prices rising to 5.5% annually indicate that cost pressures have not yet been broken.

Euro Area: Weak Industry, High PPI

The picture in the Euro Area is more fragile. Quarterly growth is a moderate 0.4%; annual growth 1.3%. Industrial production contracted by -1.5% year-on-year, while retail sales fell -0.7% month-on-month. The most striking data is PPI jumping to 11.1% annually: energy-driven cost pressures are back on the agenda. Nevertheless, headline inflation is 4.0% and core inflation 2.5%. The ECB policy rate at 2.65% leaves the real interest rate in negative territory at approximately -1.30 points; in other words, monetary policy is still loose. Unemployment is 6.5%, and consumer confidence at -17.9 points is historically low.

Global Liquidity and Risk Appetite

In the US, the policy rate is 4.0% and the real interest rate is only +0.29 points; this implies that the Fed stands close to the "neutral" zone, but that the room for aggressive easing has narrowed due to inflation stickiness. The Fed's rate projections are 3.6% for the first year and 3.4% for the second year — meaning the market is pricing a gradual but limited cutting path. Global liquidity conditions have a "abundant but expensive" character due to strong growth in the US and high long-term yields.

2. Turkey's Macroeconomic Outlook

Inflation: The Stage Where Disinflation Slows

In Türkiye, annual CPI in October 2026 came in at 29.77%, slightly above the previous 29.73%; monthly inflation was 2.58%, above the 2.4% expectation. Core inflation at 28.3% is below the headline but its pace of decline has been lost (previous 28.7%). PPI is 27.41% annually and 1.65% monthly — cost pressures have nearly converged with the headline. This picture shows that we are in the most difficult and slow-moving phase of the disinflation process, referred to as the "last mile". Annual inflation hardening in the 30% band increases the risk of inertia.

Monetary Policy and Real Interest Rate

The TCMB policy rate was cut from 37% to 36% in October 2026. This means the real interest rate remains markedly positive at +4.80 points (approximately +6.23 points). A positive real interest rate is a stance that is necessary for disinflation but puts pressure on growth. The continuation of the rate cut cycle depends on the pace of the slowdown in inflation; monthly inflation running above 2.5% narrows the room for cuts.

Growth and Labor

Growth in the second quarter was 1.1% quarter-on-quarter and 2.3% annually, below the expectation (2.9%). Although industrial production at 2.1% monthly signals a recovery, annual 2.61% is limited. Unemployment is 8.5%, the participation rate 52.7%. Capacity utilization at 74.5% is moderate; business confidence fell to 99.8, while consumer confidence dropped sharply from 91.9 to 84.8, showing that domestic demand has weakened.

3. Domestic Demand, Credit and Liquidity

Interest Rate-Credit Channel

Although the policy rate fell to 36%, the deposit rate remains high at 42.04%; the consumer loan rate at 66.46% is above the previous 64.74%. This divergence is critical: credit costs rising while the policy rate falls shows that the transmission mechanism is not working fully and that risk premiums and funding costs are being reflected in household loans. Credit growth at 35.29% annually is high but slowing (previous 36.32%); M3 money supply growth fell to 26.87%.

Domestic Demand Is Weakening

Retail sales are only 0.1% monthly and 7.7% annually (previous 10.1%). Together with the sharp drop in consumer confidence, these data confirm that high credit rates are cooling domestic demand. The mechanism works as follows: high nominal rates → higher loan installment burden → postponement of durable consumption → sales contraction in interest-sensitive sectors such as retail and automotive. This is a development that is desirable for disinflation but negative for company revenues.

4. Global Risk Appetite and Interest Rate Expectations

Market Volatility and the Dollar

The VIX index jumped 11.13% from 22.91 to 25.46, showing that risk appetite has deteriorated. DXY is flat at 103.56; we can say the dollar remains globally strong but stable. The US yield curve is notable: 3-month 4.14%, 5-year 4.90%, 30-year 5.46%. Long-term yields being clearly above the short end shows that the "term premium" has returned and the market is pricing long-term inflation/fiscal risks.

Reflection on BIST

The jump in the VIX and high US long-term yields work to limit capital flows to emerging markets. Indeed, BIST 100 fell 1.53% during the day; the loss in dollar terms was 1.72%. The deterioration in risk appetite sets the stage for foreign investors to reduce positions in high-beta markets like Turkey.

5. Budget and External Balance

Budget Deficit and Current Account Balance

The central government budget posted a deficit of 481 billion TL in October 2026 (previous 532 billion TL). The high course of the deficit implies that fiscal policy is not supporting the disinflation process, but on the contrary is working in a way that supports domestic demand. On the other hand, the current account posted a surplus of 2.72 billion dollars in September 2026 (previous surplus of 4.05 billion dollars) — thanks to energy prices and tourism revenues, the external balance is favorable.

Foreign Trade and Reserves

In October, exports were 25.9 billion dollars and imports 32.58 billion dollars; the foreign trade deficit widened to 6.7 billion dollars (previous 5.23 billion dollars). Tourism revenues were strong at 25.53 billion dollars in the third quarter; the number of tourists rose 0.4% year-on-year. Foreign exchange reserves are in a moderate increase at 57.02 billion dollars. The real effective exchange rate index rose to 107.76, showing that the TL has appreciated in real terms — this is a factor that erodes export competitiveness.

6. Energy, Commodities, Foreign Exchange and Bond Markets

Oil and Gold

Brent crude is trading high at 93.07 dollars/barrel and WTI at 85.61 dollars/barrel. This level of energy prices is a pressure factor on the current account balance and inflation for a net energy importer like Turkey. Gold is at 3.851.90 dollars per ounce and gram gold at 6.246.19 TL — gold is both a vehicle for global risk aversion and for expectations of TL depreciation.

Exchange Rate and Bonds

USD/TRY is at 50.44 and EUR/TRY at 55.51, with the TL under limited depreciation pressure. The exchange rate-inflation interaction is critical: every 1% depreciation in the TL passes through to the PPI via imported input costs and, over time, to the CPI. The real effective exchange rate being strong at 107.76 shows that this channel is currently working in a limited way but carries the risk of reversing.

7. Transmission Mechanisms: From Macro to Market

Interest Rate Channel

Policy rate (36%) → deposit rate (42.04%) → loan rate (66.46%) → investment/consumption decisions → company sales → margins → valuation. In this chain, the loan rate being far above the policy rate shows that transmission is working on the "tight" side. High credit costs pressure profit margins, especially in the banking and retail sectors.

Exchange Rate and Global Interest Rate Channel

Exchange rate channel: USD/TRY increase → imported input cost → PPI → CPI. Global interest rate channel: US 30-year bond 5.46% → global risk premium → foreign capital flow → demand for TL assets → BIST valuation. The jump in the VIX confirms that this channel is currently working in the "risk-off" direction.

8. Reflections on BIST 30: Sector Channels

Banks and Industry

High deposit rates create a twofold effect for banks as a funding cost but also in terms of net interest margin; slowing credit growth limits profitability. For industrial companies, high interest rates raise investment costs, and the real appreciation of the TL pressures export margins. Holdings, meanwhile, are partially balanced through portfolio diversification.

Retail and Defensive Sectors

The slowdown in retail sales limits the revenue growth of the retail sector; the decline in consumer confidence deepens this pressure. On the other hand, the high course of energy prices creates cost pressure in energy-intensive branches of industry, while companies with defensive cash flows may remain relatively resilient in a high interest rate environment.

9. Scenarios and Monitoring Framework

Base Scenario (50%): Gradual Disinflation

Monthly inflation falling to the 2-2.5% band, the TCMB continuing with limited cuts, and growth remaining in the 2-3% band. In this scenario, BIST trades sideways-choppy under the pressure of high interest rates.

Optimistic Scenario (25%): Rapid Disinflation

A marked decline in core inflation, a fall in the risk premium and an acceleration in foreign capital inflows. The development that would invalidate this scenario: monthly inflation rising back above 3%.

Pessimistic Scenario (25%): Inertia and Exchange Rate Pressure

Rising energy prices, an accelerating depreciation of the TL and strengthening inflation inertia. Indicators to watch: monthly CPI, PPI-CPI gap, VIX, USD/TRY, reserves, loan rate-deposit rate gap.

10. Conclusion and Evaluation

The Turkish economy is in a challenging phase of a disinflation process supported by a positive real interest rate (4.80%) but carrying inertia risk. On the global side, the US's resilient growth and high long-term yields are limiting capital flows to emerging markets; the jump in the VIX confirms that risk appetite has deteriorated. Domestically, loan rates running well above the policy rate show that transmission is working on the tight side and that domestic demand is cooling. The high budget deficit is weakening fiscal policy's support for disinflation. In the period ahead, the monthly inflation path, exchange rate stability and global risk appetite will be decisive for the course of BIST 30. Methodology and Terms

  • Signal (general): Stock-based general technical evaluation (STRONG BUY / BUY / NEUTRAL / SELL / STRONG SELL); seen on the board and in the technical screening table.
  • Oscillator vote: Individual BUY/SELL votes of indicators such as RSI, StochK, MACD, CCI, WT; given in the format "11 buy/8 sell" and may conflict with the general signal (for example, a stock may diverge positively in oscillator votes while the board signal is SELL).
  • WT (Wave Trend): short-term cycle (peak/trough) oscillator; generates BUY/SELL signals.
  • Channel (%N): position of the price within the 60-day regression channel; 0% near the channel bottom (trough area), 100% near the channel top (peak area).
  • ADX: Average Directional Index; values above 25 indicate a strong trend, values below 20 indicate weak/sideways movement.
  • r: Pearson correlation coefficient calculated over a 60-day window (trend strength indicator); given in parentheses in the model portfolio table.
  • CASH: the portion not allocated to stocks in the model portfolio, held for protection.
