3. Macro Outlook and News Flow

As of the morning of October 6, on the macro side, investors face two clear indicators: the CBRT's September inflation data and the foreign trade figures announced last evening. Annual inflation came in at 29.73%, below the expectation of 30.3%, while the monthly increase remained at 1.84%, the same as the previous month. This picture supports the real interest rate remaining in positive territory in an environment where the policy rate is held at 37.0%. However, the more notable point is that, despite the CBRT's statement that "the main trend of inflation declined in September," on the US side, PPI came in at 5.4% annually, above expectations, and the US 30-year Treasury yield rose to 4%. Global interest rate pressure stands out as the main external factor limiting inflows into TL assets. The horizontal course of USD/TRY at the 49.17 level shows that this pressure is managed through yield expectations rather than through the exchange rate.

Trade data paint a picture that indirectly concerns banking stocks through the current account balance. Exports rose to $25.98 billion compared with the previous month, while imports climbed to $31.21 billion and the trade deficit came in at -$5.23 billion. This figure is close to the previous month's deficit of -$5.24 billion; in other words, there is no marked improvement in the external balance. Nevertheless, the budget balance posting a surplus of 12.9 billion TL in September and the current account remaining positive at $0,036 billion, albeit below expectations, are favorable signals for macroeconomic stability. This mixed picture, the positive differentiation in banking stocks (ISCTR +3.37%, YKBNK +% 29.73) forms the basis: the improvement in public finances and the downtrend in inflation strengthen expectations that pressure on net interest margins will ease. However, the condition for invalidating this view is the continued rise in US bond yields and the slowdown of fund flows to emerging markets; in this scenario, the rally in banking shares may also be limited.

The selling pressure in the energy and aviation sectors directly aligns with the macro data. Brent crude's decline of 1.93% to 100.28 dollars/barrel TUPRS' explains its -3.07% loss partially, yet the real determinant is the profit sales coming after the company's 60-day 43.9% return. The noteworthy point here is that, despite the oil price fall's potential to affect the refinery margins positively, the stock-specific technical signals (WT SELL) are in the sell direction. This contradiction between the macro datum and the technical signal shows the investors prioritize profit realization in the short term. The spread of the selling pressure in these sectors to the index should be monitored as the fundamental risk element that could break the consolidation in the main scenario in the 15,300-15,400 band.

On the fund flows side, CBRT Governor Karahan's statement that "outflows from funds have turned toward deposits" is a critical indicator for investor psychology. This confirms that risk appetite is weak and investors are turning to safer havens. The decline in deposit interest rates to 43,795% shows that, although funds exiting have moved into deposits, deposit returns have fallen; this could create a potential source of funds for the stock market. However, for this potential to materialize, the index needs to make persistent closes above 15.400 and the horizontal course in USD/TRY must be maintained.
