Risk Management and Strategy Current market conditions contain both downside and upside risks. When we evaluate short-term scenarios, downside risks are more prominent. The escalation of Iran-Israel tensions and its potential to push energy prices higher again is the biggest risk factor. If this scenario occurs, energy-intensive stocks like EREGL, SISE, THYAO could experience significant value losses. Additionally, rising global recession concerns and the resulting interest rate decisions from central banks could immediately negatively affect risk appetite, pulling down the BIST 30 index. The upside potential is more limited. If geopolitical tensions ease and energy prices stabilize, recovery movements could begin in sectors that are technically weak, such as banking and retail. In particular, stocks seen as "safe havens" like BIMAS, KCHOL, and TUPRS could outperform the index in such an optimistic environment. To create portfolio protection shields, the following strategies should be followed: First, maintain a high cash ratio (40-45%) to create purchasing power during market downturns and reduce portfolio volatility. Second, determine clear stop-loss levels for each stock and close positions disciplined at these levels. For example, critical support levels like 210 TL for KCHOL and 415 TL for BIMAS are vital for risk management. Third, completely avoid using leverage to minimize the risk of market volatility eroding capital. Finally, it's important to diversify the portfolio sectorally and not be overly dependent on a single sector. 6.
