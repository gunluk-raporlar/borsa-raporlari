3. Macro Outlook and News Flow

At yesterday's close, while USD/TRY was almost unchanged (+0,009%) at 49.00, BIST 30 fell -2.77% in dollar terms to 14.842.04; this divergence shows that the selling pressure stems from local portfolio rebalancing rather than a currency depreciation. While the CBRT policy rate remained stable at 2.65%, the real interest rate (annual CPI 3.2%, monthly 2.57%) still remains in positive territory; however, September industrial production falling monthly -0.1%, annually -0.1%, and the capacity utilization rate declining to 74.2% indicate that tight monetary conditions continue to impact the real sector. The banking index losing at -37% levels (ISCTR -5.79%, YKBNK -36,322) signals that net interest margin is under pressure as credit growth slowed from 2.3% annually and deposit interest fell to 43.94%.

European-side macro data is dampening risk appetite from the top: Eurozone economic expectation index fell to 97.9 (forecast 99.0), consumer confidence dropped to -16.5; despite the ECB policy rate remaining at 2.65%, while core inflation remains at 2.4%, Germany's consideration of measures against China and German inflation rising to 3.3% in September (a 3-year high) increase demand-side risk for pro-EU exporter stocks (TUPRS, FROTO, TOASO). On the US side, the 30-year Treasury yield rising to 4% and DXY holding at 101.40 prove that Fed rate cut expectations have been delayed and global liquidity conditions remain tight; this also shows that foreign portfolio inflows (with Goldman Sachs' 'financial conditions are easing' emphasis in the news flow of the last 7 days) have not turned into net selling on BIST.

In the news flow, the BDDK's transfer of 3 banks and 2 factoring companies to TMSF and the SPK's decision to make advance payments of up to 1 million TL to fund victims remind us that the financial stability-focused inspection process is deepening; the end of the Esel Mobil system in fuel oil and the application of a gradual exit model in gasoline remind us that cost-push inflation risk will remain on the agenda towards year-end. Volkswagen and BMW's global strategy revisions (union contract terminations, new strategy) could reshape the cost/efficiency balance for the automotive chain (TOASO, FROTO). In summary; in an environment where the exchange rate is stable, interest rates are low, real activity is weak, and external demand risk is upward, the bank-weighted selling is expected to continue its path consistent with technical signals (RSI below 35 intensity, WT full SELL); however, the oversold formation in the RSI 20-25 band (DSTKF 20.3, ASTOR 23.1) could trigger a sharp technical reaction (short covering) in case of any positive macro surprise (e.g. current account data or tourism revenue revision).
