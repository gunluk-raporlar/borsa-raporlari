News and Macroeconomic Assessment The first issue dominating the agenda is the uncertainty created by the US Federal Reserve's (Fed) interest rate decision in September. While markets expect an interest rate cut, the possibility of the Fed maintaining or raising rates by adopting a more "hawkish" stance is negatively affecting global liquidity conditions and risk appetite. This situation brings the risk of capital outflows and exchange rate pressure for emerging market markets. On the other hand, news that Germany is preparing an economic package measures towards China is escalating global trade and growth concerns. Domestically, inflationary pressures continue, with the annual inflation rate announced by TÜİK remaining at 31.5%, suppressing consumer demand and corporate costs. High inflation is eroding corporate profit margins, especially for companies with high pricing power outside the retail and service sectors. Fluctuations in energy prices constitute a critical risk factor; natural gas prices staying above 71 €/MWh directly affects energy-intensive industries (petrochemicals, steel, glass) and creates pressure on the current account. On the other hand, rising geopolitical tensions and energy prices permanently exceeding the 71 €/MWh level will create additional pressure on energy and industrial companies. To protect the portfolio against these risks, it is essential to maintain a high cash ratio (40-50%) and make new purchases extremely selective. Leverage should be completely avoided.
