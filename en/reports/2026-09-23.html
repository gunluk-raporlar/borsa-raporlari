Risk Management and Strategy Under current market conditions, risk management continues to be the most decisive factor in portfolio performance. Investors should carefully observe the following risk factors and the precautions to be taken against them. Macroeconomic Risks: The annual inflation of 31.5 is eroding companies' profit margins and reducing consumers' purchasing power. This situation is particularly negatively affecting sectors that are energy-intensive and have low pricing power (steel, aviation, construction). The Fed's interest rate policy can put pressure on global liquidity, reducing foreign capital inflows. Against these risks, weight should be given to inflation-resistant stocks with high dividend potential (BIMAS, KCHOL) in the portfolio. Additionally, a high cash rate should be maintained to provide flexibility against market volatility. Geopolitical Risks: Geopolitical developments such as Iran-Middle East tensions and Germany-China trade disputes can instantly affect energy prices and global trade flows. Rising energy prices directly increase the costs of energy-intensive companies like TUPRS, EREGL, and TOASO, pressuring their profitability. Against these risks, complete exit should be made from sectors exposed to geopolitical risks (aviation, steel). In the energy sector, companies with solid fundamental data like TUPRS should be monitored only until a short-term buy signal emerges. Technical Risks: Whether the BIST 30 index breaks the 16.217 resistance band is the most critical risk factor from a technical perspective. If this band is not broken, there is a risk of the index falling to the 16.217 support level. If this scenario occurs, the cash ratio in the portfolio should be increased to ensure capital preservation. On a stock-by-stock basis, stop-loss levels (for example, 210 TL for KCHOL, 415 TL for BIMAS) must be strictly adhered to. Stop-loss should be an indispensable part of risk management and should not be postponed due to emotional decisions. Strategic Approach: In the current environment, "capital protection" is a priority. New purchases should only be made for stocks that have a "strong buy" signal with technical and fundamental alignment. Leverage should absolutely be avoided. The portfolio should be limited to a maximum of 8-10 stocks, and the remaining cash should be kept ready for market opportunities. This strategy will limit a potential downward move while ensuring portfolio participation when an upward trend begins. 6.
