2. Market Outlook and Transmission Mechanism

As of 10:52 on Friday, October 2, 2026 — BIST 30 (XU030) close 15.218.47; previous close 14.842.04 (daily +2.54%); daily performance in +2.30% in USD terms. XU100 also moved in the same direction, rising from 11.947.18 to 12.249.04 (daily +2.53%). USD/TRY at 49,029 level; previous close 49.03 (daily +0.23%). This table gains meaning when read in conjunction with the previous session's background: the index fell 2.77% yesterday, experiencing its third consecutive down day and falling below the 15.000 psychological level; today's close is realizing a strong rebound above this threshold.

The first link of the transmission chain doesn't go through the currency: almost all of the +2.54% return in TL terms is also preserved in USD terms (+2.30%); the difference between the two returns is explained by the calm +0.23% movement of USD/TRY. In other words, the movement is not a product of currency conversion but of domestic demand — this is the fundamental distinction that separates the quality of this rally from yesterday's position-driven decline and the basis for the "authentic strength" interpretation. The second link of the chain is breadth: 25 of the 30 stocks are positive, 5 are negative; the upward movement is not concentrated in a single stock or sector. Mechanistically, in the banking leg (centered yesterday's selling, with ISCTR -5.79% and YKBNK -5.44% closing the day) accumulated oversold conditions and low positions are simultaneously feeding bottom-up buying via short position covering today; VAKBN's +5.15% prominence and its single BUY recommendation on the oscillator card (14 AL) are the counterparts of this reversal in the sector legs. In stock impact, TUPRS maintains its trend leader status with a 60-day +53.6% return and 0.91 correlation with the index; the index's continued carry of it is the key indicator to determine whether the reaction is systemic or singular.

Two contradictions should be monitored in the risk leg. First, the mismatch between price movement and the SELL dominance on the signal card: despite broad-based gains, technical signals have yet to confirm the reversal, keeping the possibility of the rally being a reaction within a downtrend alive; confirmation comes when the signal card shifts to the BUY side and the banking legs also sustain their closing levels — if this condition breaks, the probability of a reaction sell increases. Second, the persistence of singular risk despite broad gains: DSTKF -9.98% hits a bottom while deepening its 60-day loss to -79.8% and RSI 19.3 in oversold territory; this shows that liquidity and contagion risk in individual names remain active today. Given it's Friday and the weekend gap risk, disciplined management of position sizes even during a swift rally would serve as a buffer for capital protection if the signal card shifts back to SELL dominance.

3. Macro Outlook and News Flow

Durability signals in the US economy are strengthening the Fed's "higher for longer" narrative, while the 4% rally in BIST 30 (15.218.47) stands out as technical resistance against this external pressure. DXY's rise to 102.07 (+4%) and the 2-year Treasury yield projection remaining at 3.9% (September dot plot) show global liquidity conditions remain tight; however, the decline of US 10-year Treasury yields to 3,985% (3-month) and 30-year to 162 reflects position lightening ahead of Friday's September NFP data release. The US Manufacturing PMI remaining strong at 58.8 in expansion territory and Retail Sales rising 1.2% month-on-month keep inflation risks alive, while the Consumer Confidence index falling to 81.9 (forecast 89.2) indicates fragility on the demand side; this contradiction shows the market operates on a "good data is bad data" logic and direction will be determined by today's NFP result (forecast 56k, previous 162k).

Turkey's macro data prove that the inflation-interest rate spiral is gradually breaking, but the real impact via the credit channel has not yet fully materialized. September CPI at annual 31.51% (monthly 1.84%) and PPI at annual 5.8% (monthly 2.65%) keep real policy interest rate around +5.5% positive; while credit growth fell from annual 36.32% to 3.5%, and M3 money supply growth fell from 1.2% to 29.71%, showing that the squeeze is working through the money volume. The Central Bank's decision to increase KOBI credit growth limits, while signaling slight easing at the micro-level, leaves deposit rates at 43,795% and consumer credit rates at 63.74%, creating a structure where banks preserve their Net Interest Margin (NIM) but continue to face demand pressure. The current account balance nearly reaching zero (+36 mln $, quarterly) and the trade deficit narrowing to $5.24 bln are positive, but the decline in foreign exchange reserves from $62,808 bln to $62.8 bln reminds that currency stability (USD/TRY 49,0285, +0.04%) is maintained by central bank supply management; this structure helps reduce inflation expectations without currency shocks.

The reflection of geopolitical risk premium on energy prices forms fundamental support for the index's carrier TUPRS (+53.6%, 60d); the UN's warning that fuel subsidies in an Iran conflict scenario could exceed $1 trillion keeps Brent at $100.86/barrel level, while ASELSAN's €23.47 mln contract boosts industrial export motivation. In contrast, DSTKF's TMSF option exercise news and "trust issues" toward the factoring sector, combined with a 60-day performance of -79.8%, trigger a systemic risk alert; although the SPK's "no systemic problem" statement calms the market, liquidity and contagion risk remains on the watchlist. The implementation of the Turkey-Ukraine Free Trade Agreement could support the structural improvement of the current account by opening alternative markets for exporters (especially machinery, chemicals, food) in the medium term.

The contradiction between the technical rally and macro fundamentals depends on three critical conditions for sustainability: 1) Today's US NFP data staying within the 100-150 thousand range keeps Fed rate cut expectations alive, 2) USD/TR peacefully remaining within the 49.00-49.50 band and reserve flows turning positive, 3) The slowdown in bank loans (annual 36% level) falling below the excessive threshold allowing the real sector to breathe. If any of these conditions break (especially NFP >200k or USD/TRY >49.50), the market's "broad-based recovery" (25 rising / 5 falling) could quickly turn into a "selling opportunity"; for now, the market is pricing the "golden slice" scenario where inflation is slowing, interest rates have peaked, and the currency is stable.
