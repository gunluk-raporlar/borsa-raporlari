1. Executive Summary and Market Overview

The day should be considered as a turning point where the BIST 30 index took a "breather" and consolidated within the critical resistance band of 4.200-4.250. The index is not giving a clear trend reversal signal for a sustained recovery above this area, and as long as it remains below this level, there is a risk of continued downward pressure. This situation is causing the market to adopt a cautious and selective stance. Retail and energy sector stocks, previously highlighted as "safe havens" in previous analyses, continue to be key factors supporting portfolio performance. In particular, KCHOL and BIMAS are showing positive divergence against the index, serving as anchors in our portfolio. However, the persistence of geopolitical risks (Iran-Middle East tensions) and fluctuations in energy prices (natural gas prices above 71 €/MWh) continue to be a pressure factor, especially on energy-intensive and geopolitically vulnerable sectors. Uncertainty regarding the US Federal Reserve's September interest rate decision and global recession concerns are reducing risk appetite and causing capital to flow to safer assets. In this environment, the fundamental perspective of fund management is to prioritize risk protection with a high cash ratio while continuing to create controlled positions in selected stocks with strong technical and fundamental strength. The market must be prepared for both the Fed's interest rate decision and the volatility created by geopolitical risks.

2. News and Macroeconomic Assessment

The macroeconomic agenda is shaped around two fundamental factors determining the pressure on BIST 30 companies: global monetary policy and geopolitical risks. Expectations regarding the US Federal Reserve's September meeting and interest rate decision directly affect global liquidity conditions and act as the main catalyst determining risk appetite for emerging markets. Although markets are moving with expectations of an interest rate cut, an unexpected increase in inflation data could disrupt this scenario. This situation could weaken the expectation of "easing of financial conditions" for Turkish markets and create pressure on the exchange rate. In particular, inflation expectations remaining at the 28.4% level are negatively affecting consumer spending and companies' cost structures. Domestically, steps such as the additional 1.1 billion dollars provided to Halkbank support the banking sector, but the impact of these supports on long-term profitability remains limited. Social and economic indicators such as a decrease in employment are increasing concerns that the recovery in domestic demand may be slow. In the geopolitical arena, developments related to Iran and "economic war" rhetoric are creating direct pressure on energy and logistics costs. Natural gas prices remaining above 71 euros are seriously threatening the margins of energy-intensive industrial companies such as EREGL, TUPRS, and TOASO. Additionally, Germany's preparation of an economic measures package towards China and the slowdown in global demand emerge as a risk factor for export-oriented companies. When these flows are evaluated together, it can be said that an environment where both cost pressures and demand uncertainty continue for BIST 30 companies. Investor psychology remains fragile due to this dual risk structure.

5. Risk Management and Strategy

Current market conditions are placing risk management at the center of portfolio management. Short-term possible scenarios should be addressed with predetermined shields.

Short-Term Downside Scenarios and Protection Shields:

  1. Scenario: The Fed adopting a more hawkish stance than expected at its September meeting, signaling a rate hike.

    • "Impact:" Sudden contraction of global liquidity, collapse in risk appetite, and capital flow to safe havens (US Treasuries, gold).

    • "Reflection:" BIST 30 index testing the 4.000 support level, putting pressure on banking and stock markets.

    • Protection Shield: Maintaining a high cash ratio in the portfolio (35-40%). Strict adherence to stop-loss levels (395 TL for TUPRS, 210 TL for KCHOL, 415 TL for BIMAS). Complete avoidance of leverage.

  2. Scenario: Escalation of Iran-Middle East tension and energy prices (especially natural gas) rising above 80 €/MWh.

    • "Impact:" Explosion of energy costs, slowdown in industrial production, and further increase in inflation.

    • "Reflection:" Sharp selling pressure on energy-intensive stocks like EREGL, SISE, TOASO. TÜFE data exceeding expectations and CB continuing its tight monetary policy.

    • Protection Shield: Complete avoidance of energy-intensive stocks. Focus on inflation-resistant retail (BIMAS, KCHOL) and basic metal (TUPRS) stocks in the portfolio.

  3. Scenario: Breaking of critical support levels (Index 4.000, THYAO 290 TL, EREGL 37 TL).

    • "Impact:" Technically triggered stop-loss orders coming in and the market being dragged further down through a domino effect.

    • "Reflection:" Widespread portfolio value loss.

    • Protection Shield: Tracking of pre-determined and technically supported stop-loss levels for each stock with automated orders. Closing of positions when these levels are broken.

Short-term Upward Scenarios and Opportunities:

  1. Scenario: Fed cutting interest rates or using a "dovish" tone.

    • "Impact:" "Global risk appetite increasing, capital inflow to emerging markets."

    • "Reflection:" "BIST 30 index breaking the 4.250 resistance and rising to the 4.300-4.400 band."

    • "Strateji:" "High cash ratio offers an opportunity to participate in this upward wave. With stop-loss protection, position increases can be made in technically strong stocks like TUPRS and KCHOL."

  2. "Scenario: Sudden easing in geopolitical tensions and decline in energy prices."

    • "Impact:" "Reduced cost pressure and increased corporate profit expectations."

    • "Reflection:" "Recovery in energy and industrial stocks."

    • "Strateji:" "This scenario offers an opportunity for profit-taking in already held stocks like TUPRS in the portfolio, while allowing for a cautious re-evaluation of other weakened industrial stocks (SISE, TOASO)."

Strategic Approach:
In the current environment, the correct strategy is to adopt a "cautious stance, high liquidity, and selective buying" approach. It is essential not to panic sell, but at the same time to limit new purchases to expectations of a clear trend reversal signal. Risk management should be ensured not only through stop-loss levels but also through sectoral distribution and cash ratio management.
