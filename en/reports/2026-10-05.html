3. Macro Outlook and News Flow

What is new on the inflation side is that while consumer prices are slowing, producer costs are re-accelerating. September consumer inflation fell to 31.51% annually from 31.75% previously, while monthly at 1.84% came in below the 1.93% estimate; in contrast, producer inflation was announced at 27.95% annually and 2.57% monthly, higher than the previous 27.83% and 1.52%. The slowdown in consumer inflation could positively affect interest rate expectations and the valuation discount; however, the resistance on the producer side alone is not sufficient confirmation for the easing of real interest rates and credit demand. For BIST 30, companies with strong pricing power are relatively protected, while the recovery of gross margins in industrial companies using imported inputs may be delayed. This assessment strengthens on the condition that producer inflation also slows compared to previous periods and that company sales can cover cost increases; if producer prices remain high, even if inflation appears to fall, improvement in profit margins will be limited.

On the interest rate and credit side, deposit costs have declined while consumer credit pricing has risen. While the policy rate remained unchanged at 37.0%, the deposit rate fell from 43,795% to 43,795%; the consumer credit rate rose from 64.74% to 64.74%. M3 money supply growth fell from 2.3.2218% to 29,7058%, and credit growth fell from 36,322.5992% to 36,3222%, while these developments indicate only a gradual slowdown in the chain transmitted from money supply to credit, then to consumption and company sales. For banks, although the declining deposit cost supports the funding side, the high consumer credit price and the lack of data on non-performing loans do not provide sufficient confirmation in favor of net interest margin and asset quality. Although YKBNK's technical outlook is positive, the continued decline in deposit costs and the non-deterioration of asset quality should be considered as macro confirmation; if the slowdown in credit demand becomes evident, the sustainability of the positive pricing in bank stocks may weaken.

The prominent change on the exchange rate side is the depreciation of the Turkish lira despite global dollar weakness. USD/TRY is at 49,134602 TL and rose 0,2174%, while DXY fell 0,1743% at the 101,921997 index. This divergence may indicate that the risk premium on Turkish assets or local liquidity preference is weakening independently of the global dollar move; the cause cannot be determined alone. Although the real effective exchange rate index falling from 105.04 to 105.04 is positive for competitiveness, this indicator is lagging. September exports were $23.47 billion, imports $28.71 billion, and the trade balance was -$5.24 billion, with a narrower deficit than the previous period; however, Brent oil remaining at $102,419998/barrel sustains the imported energy cost risk. Although the weakening of the exchange rate supports export revenues, its effect on net margins varies according to companies' foreign exchange income-exposure balance by increasing imported input, working capital, and debt costs. Exchange rate stabilization and a pullback in oil strengthen this view; if geopolitical oil and exchange rate shocks move in the same direction, exchange rate gains in exporting companies could turn into margin support.

In the news flow, statements that disinflation is broadening, financial conditions are easing, and there is no downward pressure on the credit rating support risk appetite, while the contract news regarding ASELS could create a positive catalyst specific to the company. In contrast, the US 5-year Treasury yield rising from 4% to 5,068% and the US one-year inflation expectation rising from 4.0% to 4.6% show that the pressure of global discount rates on BIST 30 valuation has not completely disappeared. The VIX falling to 15.79, down 3,6608%, supports that short-term risk appetite has not deteriorated. From an investor psychology perspective, falling inflation could create an "interest rate cut expectation" and support valuation; however, if the exchange rate remains weak, imported inflation could rise again, and if this leads to high nominal interest rates, this relief could reverse. Therefore, the current BIST 30 rise should be considered a narrow revaluation supported by falling inflation and selective company catalysts, not a complete resolution of the macro risk premium; unless inflation expectations and the exchange rate stabilize together, caution is needed regarding the breadth of the rise.

Among the triggers to watch are USD/TRY moving outside the band around 49.16, the upper band reaction in Türk Hava Yolları remaining unconfirmed, and the direction of resolution of the vote divergence within banking; if two of these triggers work negatively in the same session, it is recommended to reduce position sizes and not take new risk until breadth improvement is confirmed.
