3. Macro Outlook and News Flow

What is new on the inflation side is that while consumer prices are slowing, producer costs are re-accelerating. September consumer inflation fell to 31.51% annually from 31.75% previously, while monthly at 1.84% came in below the 1.93% estimate; in contrast, producer inflation was announced at 27.95% annually and 2.57% monthly, higher than the previous 27.83% and 1.52%. The slowdown in consumer inflation could positively affect interest rate expectations and the valuation discount; however, the resistance on the producer side alone is not sufficient confirmation for the easing of real interest rates and credit demand. For BIST 30, companies with strong pricing power are relatively protected, while the recovery of gross margins in industrial companies using imported inputs may be delayed. This assessment strengthens on the condition that producer inflation also slows compared to previous periods and that company sales can cover cost increases; if producer prices remain high, even if inflation appears to fall, improvement in profit margins will be limited.

On the interest rate and credit side, deposit costs declined while consumer loan pricing rose. While the policy rate remained unchanged at 37.0%, the deposit rate fell from 43.795% to 43.795%; the consumer loan rate, however, rose from 64.74% to 64.74%. While M3 money supply growth fell from 2.3%,2218 to 29.7058% and credit growth from 36.322%,5992 to 36.3222%, these developments point only to a gradual slowdown in the chain transmitted from money supply to credit and then to consumption and corporate sales. Although falling deposit costs support the funding side for banks, the high consumer loan rate and the lack of data on non-performing loans do not provide sufficient confirmation for the net interest margin and asset quality. YKBNK' although the technical appearance is positive, the deposit cost continuing to fall and the asset quality not deteriorating should be evaluated as the macro confirmation; if the slowdown in the credit demand becomes marked, the continuity of the positive pricing in the bank stocks can weaken.

The prominent change on the exchange rate side is the depreciation of the Turkish lira despite global dollar weakness. USD/TRY is at 49,134602 TL and rose 0,2174%, while DXY fell 0,1743% at the 101,921997 index. This divergence may indicate that the risk premium on Turkish assets or local liquidity preference is weakening independently of the global dollar move; the cause cannot be determined alone. Although the real effective exchange rate index falling from 105.04 to 105.04 is positive for competitiveness, this indicator is lagging. September exports were $23.47 billion, imports $28.71 billion, and the trade balance was -$5.24 billion, with a narrower deficit than the previous period; however, Brent oil remaining at $102,419998/barrel sustains the imported energy cost risk. Although the weakening of the exchange rate supports export revenues, its effect on net margins varies according to companies' foreign exchange income-exposure balance by increasing imported input, working capital, and debt costs. Exchange rate stabilization and a pullback in oil strengthen this view; if geopolitical oil and exchange rate shocks move in the same direction, exchange rate gains in exporting companies could turn into margin support.

While statements in the news flow that disinflation is broadening, financial conditions are easing and there is no downward pressure on the credit rating support risk appetite, ASELS' the contract news can form a company-specific positive catalyst. In contrast, the rise of the US 5-year bond rate from 4% to 5.068% and the rise of the US 1-year inflation expectation from 4.0% to 4.6% show the pressure of the global discount rates on the BIST 30 valuation has not completely disappeared. The VIX retreating to 15.79 with a 3.6608% fall supports that the short-term risk appetite is not damaged. In terms of investor psychology, the fall in inflation can support the valuation by creating a "rate cut expectation"; however, if the currency stays weak, import inflation rises again and leads to nominal rates moving high, this relief can reverse. Therefore, the current BIST 30 rise should be evaluated not as a completely resolved macro risk premium but as a narrow revaluation supported by the falling inflation and selective company catalysts; as long as inflation expectations and the currency do not stabilize together, caution is needed about the breadth of the rise.
