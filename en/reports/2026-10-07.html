3. Macro Outlook and News Flow

As of Wednesday, October 7, 2026, the macro framework indicates that the tightening cycle continues despite the downward trend in inflation. According to TUIK data, September annual inflation fell to 29.73%, below the previous month's 31.51%, while monthly inflation came in at 1.84%, well below expectations (2.3%). This picture provides a basis supporting the CBRT keeping its policy rate at 37.0%. However, in the same period, the rise in US PPI to 5.4% year-on-year and US 10-year Treasury yields remaining at high levels keep global pressure on emerging market assets alive. This coincides with the BIST 30 index losing 0.49% at yesterday's close to fall to 15.381.69 points; since the dollar-based loss (-1.03%) indicates approximately twice the TL-based loss, it shows that selling pressure stems from currency rather than local dynamics.

On the currency front, the limited rise in USD/TRY (+0.04%) and EUR/TRY rising 0.16% to 55.42 TL indicate that margin pressure continues for companies with high imported input costs. For companies that are particularly importers of energy and raw materials, this situation poses a downside risk to operational profitability. However, in the same data set, Brent oil falling 1.09% to $99.23 per barrel stands out as a partially mitigating factor for this pressure. Domestically, September exports increasing to $25.98 billion compared to the previous month and the current account balance approaching balance at $0,036 billion in July show that the improvement in the foreign trade channel continues. While this improvement supports the positive reflection of currency movements on the revenues of exporting companies, it limits the impact of weak domestic demand on retail and consumer-oriented sectors.

In terms of investor psychology, the real rates remaining high despite the fall in inflation continue to be the fundamental factor limiting entries into the equity market. The TCMB keeping the policy rate fixed at 37.0% and the deposit rates being at the 43.8% level show the appeal of alternative return possibilities is preserved. In this environment, FTSE Russell keeping Türkiye in the "Advanced Emerging Market" status and not placing it on the watch list can be evaluated as positive news for passive fund flows; however, for this situation to provide the index a new buying return, global risk appetite also needs to be supportive. ASELSAN' its new 195 million dollar contract, in turn, stands out as a positive company news for the defense industry stocks, yet for this development's spreading to the whole of the index, a similar news flow should also be expected from the other companies in the sector.

Looking at the stock-level effects of macro data, the outlook for the banking sector can be said to be gradually improving. The downward inflation trend and strengthening expectations of a TCMB rate cut may ease pressure on banks' deposit costs. However, consumer loan rates remaining high at 64.74% indicates that the recovery in credit demand will remain limited. Therefore VAKBN andYKBNK' to be lasting, the net interest margin data need to confirm this improvement. On the industry side, while the capacity utilization rising to 74.2% and the relative stability in job confidence give a recovery signal on the production side, the monthly fall in industrial production (-1.0%) shows this recovery is not yet strong. In this framework KRDMD andPETKM the sustainability of the positive signals in the stocks like will take shape depending on the course of the global commodity prices and the domestic demand conditions.
