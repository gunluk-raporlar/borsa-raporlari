Macro Outlook and News Flow 3. Macro Outlook and News Flow As of Wednesday, October 7, 2026, the macro framework indicates that the tightening cycle continues despite the downward trend in inflation. According to TUIK data, September annual inflation fell to 29.73%, below the previous month's 31.51%, while monthly inflation came in at 1.84%, well below expectations (2.3%). This picture provides a basis supporting the CBRT keeping its policy rate at 37.0%. However, in the same period, the rise in US PPI to 5.4% year-on-year and US 10-year Treasury yields remaining at high levels keep global pressure on emerging market assets alive. This coincides with the BIST 30 index losing 0.49% at yesterday's close to fall to 15.381.69 points; since the dollar-based loss (-1.03%) indicates approximately twice the TL-based loss, it shows that selling pressure stems from currency rather than local dynamics. On the currency front, the limited rise in USD/TRY (+0.04%) and EUR/TRY rising 0.16% to 55.42 TL indicate that margin pressure continues for companies with high imported input costs. For companies that are particularly importers of energy and raw materials, this situation poses a downside risk to operational profitability. However, in the same data set, Brent oil falling 1.09% to $99.23 per barrel stands out as a partially mitigating factor for this pressure. Domestically, September exports increasing to $25.98 billion compared to the previous month and the current account balance approaching balance at $0,036 billion in July show that the improvement in the foreign trade channel continues. While this improvement supports the positive reflection of currency movements on the revenues of exporting companies, it limits the impact of weak domestic demand on retail and consumer-oriented sectors. From an investor psychology perspective, despite the decline in inflation, high real interest rates continue to be the main factor limiting inflows into the stock market. The CBRT keeping its policy rate at 37.0% and deposit rates at 43.8% show that alternative return opportunities remain attractive. In this environment, FTSE Russell keeping Turkey in the "Advanced Emerging Market" status and not placing it on the watchlist can be considered positive news for passive fund flows; however, for this to bring new buying to the index, global risk appetite also needs to be supportive. ASELSAN's new $195 million contract stands out as positive company news for defense industry stocks, while for this development to spread to the broader index, similar news flow from other companies in the sector should be expected. Looking at the stock-level impacts of macro data, it can be said that the outlook for the banking sector is gradually improving. The downward trend in inflation and strengthening expectations for CBRT rate cuts could ease pressure on banks' deposit costs. However, consumer loan rates remaining high at 64.74% indicate that the revival in credit demand will remain limited. Therefore, for the technical BUY signals in VAKBN and YKBNK to be sustainable, net interest margin data needs to confirm this improvement. On the industrial side, although the capacity utilization rate rising to 74.2% and relative stability in business confidence signal a recovery on the production side, the monthly decline in industrial production (-1.0%) shows that this recovery is not yet strong. In this framework, the sustainability of positive signals in stocks like KRDMD and PETKM will be shaped by the course of global commodity prices and domestic demand conditions. 4.
