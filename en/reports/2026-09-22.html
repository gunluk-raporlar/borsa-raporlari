1. Executive Summary and Market Overview

Today's market dynamics are forcing the BIST 30 index to undergo intense consolidation in the 4.200-4.250 resistance band with a "breakout" risk. In our past analysis memory, a break above this level would signal an upward trend reversal, while failure to break it would highlight the risk of a pullback to the 4.000 support level. Today's market picture creates a critical "conflict zone" for these scenarios to become clear.

The macroeconomic base continues to dampen risk appetite. With the annual inflation rate at 31.5%, Fed interest rate decisions and global geopolitical tensions (particularly Germany-China trade and AI discussions) are keeping investors cautious. However, in the local market, news flow indicating "easing financial conditions" and positive foreign trade balance (exports to the EU exceeding 72 billion dollars, Iraqi oil) has the potential to protect the index from a sharp downward correction.

From a technical analysis perspective, although "STRONG SELL" and "SELL" signals dominate the index, portfolio protection and selective buying strategies should be adopted. Particularly, "STRONG BUY" signals in the energy and retail sectors stand out as rare points of positive divergence despite the market's generally cautious sentiment. From a fund management perspective; capital protection should be prioritized, while exits from risky sectors (steel, aviation, banking) should continue, and weight should be given to inflation-resistant and dividend-focused stocks.

2. News and Macroeconomic Assessment

The fundamental macroeconomic and news flow shaping market dynamics directly impact risk management strategies.

  • Global Geopolitical and Interest Rate Pressure: Germany's economic measures against China and tensions in AI discussions continue to disrupt global trade flows and risk appetite. Fed interest rate pressure on precious metals increases interest in safe havens like gold, which could negatively impact foreign capital flows to BIST 30. The Fed's September interest rate decision remains the most critical agenda item determining global liquidity.

  • Local Macroeconomic Data: With the annual inflation rate at 31.5%, it continues to put pressure on consumer spending and corporate costs. This situation poses a risk of squeezing profit margins, especially in energy-intensive industries and construction, while potentially creating a "pricing power" advantage in the retail sector.

  • Financial Conditions and Foreign Trade: Goldman Sachs' assessment that "financial conditions have eased in Turkey" could soften interest rate expectations in the local market; while the $1.1 billion source provided to Halkbank and EU exports exceeding $72 billion are evaluated as positive news that reduces current account deficit risk. The increase in Iraqi oil exports, on the other hand, supports the external balance.

  • Sectoral News Flow: New collaborations in the real estate sector and public investments to be allocated to technology carry long-term positive divergence potential. However, Volkswagen's car recall operation and Jaguar Land Rover's layoffs reflect global demand uncertainty in the automotive sector.
