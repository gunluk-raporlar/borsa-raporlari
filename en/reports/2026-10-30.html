3. Macro Outlook and News Flow

In yesterday evening's data, USD/TRY rose 0.06% to 49.83, indicating continued currency pressure, while DXY fell 0.21% to 101.98, suggesting that the dollar's global weakness was not fully reflected in the local exchange rate. With the CBRT policy rate held at 36.0%, real interest rates remain in negative territory; this situation fuels the divergence of credit growth (annual 36.3% as of August data) from money supply growth (M3 annual 28.6%), keeping inflation risk alive through domestic demand. If USD/TRY permanently breaks the 50.00 psychological resistance, industrial margins (especially automotive and white goods) could narrow again in the second half through imported input costs.

In the Eurozone, September annual inflation rising to 3.8% and core inflation staying at 2.5% strengthens the likelihood of the ECB slowing its easing cycle; this was effective in EUR/TRY appreciating 0.63% to 55.79. While EU industrial production fell 0.5% month-on-month and 1.1% year-on-year in August, reflecting weakness on the European demand side, Turkey's September exports coming in at 25.97 billion dollars (estimate 25.98) shows resilience in external demand, yet the trade deficit widening to 5.16 billion dollars reminds of the fragility of the current account balance. Although EU growth of 0.6% quarter-on-quarter in the second quarter is positive, the economic sentiment index falling to 97.9 signals weakening demand in the coming periods.

In local macro data, October consumer confidence falling to 84.8 (previous 91.9) and business confidence to 99.8 (previous 102.0) indicates deteriorating consumer psychology in an environment where real wages lag behind inflation; retail sales rising 1.4% month-on-month in August remains limited due to early purchasing and inventory restocking effects. In light of these data, net interest margin (NIM) pressure persists in the banking sector, while the currency advantage protects margins in export-oriented industry; however, unless real interest rates turn positive, the credit demand-driven growth model will remain fragile.

On the US side, the Fed keeping rates at 4.0% and the continued contraction of the central bank's balance sheet keep global liquidity conditions tight; the decline in US 10-year Treasury yields (around 4.80%) provides limited support to risk appetite. US consumer confidence jumping to 91.7 in October and durable goods orders rising 1.8% in September support the "soft landing" scenario for the US economy, while core PCE staying at 3.1% reminds that the inflation fight is not over. If US inflation data (especially CPI and PCE) cannot fall below 3.0% in the coming months, the Fed's "higher for longer" narrative could strengthen and pressure emerging market portfolio flows; this could drive foreign ownership in BIST 30 (particularly bank and holding-heavy) toward selling moves.
