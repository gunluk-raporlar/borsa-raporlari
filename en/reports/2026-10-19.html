3. Macro Outlook and News Flow

Although the Eylül inflation data (TÜFE annual 29.73%, monthly 1.84%; ÜFE annual 27.38%) show that the disinflation process continues, the Merkez Bankası's determination to keep the policy rate at 37% and maintain positive real interest rates remains the central axis of market pricing. The slowdown seen in growth data (Q2 annual 2.3%, below the 2.9% forecast) and in industrial production (Ağustos annual -1.86%) reveals the cost of tight monetary policy to the real sector. Conversely, the Eylül budget deficit rising to 532.2 milyar TL (previous month +12.9 milyar TL increase) triggers fiscal discipline concerns and borrowing needs, creating downside risk on the banking sector's net interest margin expectations. The slight slowdown in credit growth (annual 36.3%) and the contraction of M3 money supply (26.6%) indicate that the cooling on the demand side is also confirmed by monetary aggregates.

The most critical shock from abroad was the ABD September ÜFE coming in much hotter than expected at monthly 1.3% (forecast 0.5%) and annual 6.3% (forecast 5.3%); this data strengthened the probability of the Fed delaying its rate-cut roadmap, while the 10-year Treasury yields holding at 4.90% and the VIX rising 21.4% to approach the 20 level weakened global risk appetite. The DXY's 0.16% decline and Brent oil's rise of 3.7% to the 110 dollar band increased margin pressure on the Turkish industry with high imported input costs. Gold's rise to 4181 dollars per ounce signals that the search for safe haven continues despite dollar weakness.

The USD/TRY closing virtually unchanged at 49.5152 (0.02%) shows that the Merkez Bankası managed to keep exchange rate pressure under control through active market operations and reserve flows; the rise in weekly reserves to 64.57 milyar dollars (previous 60.33 milyar) and the August current account balance turning to a 4.05 milyar dollar surplus confirm net foreign exchange inflow behind this resilience. The real effective exchange rate index being at 105.45 as of September (previous 105.0) in a slightly appreciated position partly weakens exporters' competitiveness but provides a critical buffer for the sustainability of the current account. This stability suggests that foreign selling pressure on bank stocks is shaped not by exchange rate concerns but by expectations of global risk-off and narrowing interest rate differentials.

The decisions of the 6th Fon Kurulu meeting on the agenda, the upcoming year's transportation budget reaching 1 trilyon TL, and the EPDK's electricity market changes create policy-supported demand expectations for the construction, cement, iron steel, and energy sectors. Türkiye's increase in investment in response to China's sale of US Treasuries shows the continuation of a diversification strategy in reserve management; the Russia-Ukraine-Triple Summit agenda and volatility in oil prices reflect as supply-side uncertainty underlying the short-term sell signals of refinery stocks. The keeping of electricity and natural gas price hikes off the agenda (Bakan Bayraktar's statement) provides a temporary relief element for industrialists under cost pressure.
