3. Macro Outlook and News Flow

3. Macro Outlook and News Flow

On the Turkish side, the improvement on the inflation front is today's most decisive input for investor psychology. September annual inflation came in at 29.73%, below both the forecast (30.3%) and the previous month (31.51%), while the decline in annual PPI increase to 27.38% shows that a second cost-driven wave is for now suppressed. This picture supports the real interest rate remaining in positive territory in an environment where the policy rate is held at 37.0%; however, the increase in consumer loan interest rates (64.74%) and the slowdown in credit growth (36.32%) indicate that tightening is gradually curbing domestic demand. Although a positive ground is formed for banking stocks on the net interest margin side, the slowdown in credit volume growth stands out as the main factor limiting this optimism.

The flat course on the exchange rate side reduces exchange rate-induced pressure on TL assets. USD/TRY's decline to 49.08 and EUR/TRY's fall to 55.21 allow internal dynamics to come to the fore in an environment of low exchange rate volatility. The real effective exchange rate continuing its strengthening trend at 105.45 relatively eases imported input costs while potentially having an eroding effect on the competitiveness of exporting companies. In this context, the monthly contraction in industrial production (-1.0%) and the negative trend in annual production show that the stabilization in the exchange rate has not yet positively reflected in export volume; this reveals that the strong signals in metal and energy stocks are sector-specific and do not confirm the general industrial outlook.

On the global side, the rise in commodity prices creates a supportive ground for metal and energy stocks within BIST 30. The 1.43% increase in gold's ounce price and Brent oil holding at 104.72 USD/barrel draw a consistent picture between the positive technical signals in stocks like TRMET and TUPRS and macro data. However, the data confusion on the US side (manufacturing PMI declining to 54.5, non-farm payrolls coming in at 29 thousand, well below expectations) keeps global growth concerns alive; this carries the risk that weakness in external demand will be reflected in the sales revenues of Turkish exporting companies. In the Eurozone, inflation rising to 3.8% and PPI jumping to 8.2% could delay the European Central Bank's rate cut expectations; this requires close monitoring of demand conditions in Turkey's main export market.

In the news flow, ASELSAN's 195 million dollar contract stands out as the most concrete example of company-specific positive divergence in the defense industry. In contrast, the draft law on investment funds being submitted to the Turkish Grand National Assembly and the tightening in tax audits can be considered regulatory elements limiting market participants' risk appetite. Although macro data generally draw a positive framework through the decline in inflation and stability in the exchange rate, weakness in industrial production and the slowdown in credit growth stand as the main stabilizers supporting the index's flat course. This outlook reveals that for the sustainability of selective strong signals in banking and energy stocks, recovery in credit volumes and momentum in industrial production are needed.

However, the weak overall outlook in the technical scan ("STRONG SELL" signals in BIMAS, KCHOL, SAHOL, and AKBNK) and the shift of trading volume to selective stocks limit the index's upward movement.

Downside Risk Scenario: SAHOL's daily decline of 5.92% and the "STRONG SELL" votes in oscillators (19 sell/1 buy) indicate that selling pressure in holding and banking stocks continues. Against this scenario, it is recommended to maintain the cash ratio in the portfolio and reduce positions in holding/bank stocks with weak signals.

Upside Scenario and Portfolio Shield: The continuation of "STRONG BUY" signals in VAKBN and TRMET and the preservation of the strong trend exhibited by TUPRS with a 60-day return of 35.9% could enable the index to rise above the 15.200 resistance. Therefore, the cash/liquidity buffer in the portfolio should be kept flexible both to protect against downside risks and to take new positions in a possible recovery.
