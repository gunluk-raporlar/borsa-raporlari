2. Market Outlook and Transmission Mechanism

As of October 14, 2026, Wednesday, at 07:22 — BIST 30 (XU030) last close 15.191.57; previous close 15.001.28 (daily +1.27%); daily performance in dollar terms +1.17%. USD/TRY is at 49.39, showing a slight depreciation of +0.09% compared to the previous close, while the index's TL-based rise remaining positive even after stripping out the exchange rate effect indicates that local buying power is absorbing the currency pressure. XU030's clear outperformance (+1.27%) relative to XU100 (+0.82%) suggests that the index's weight center has shifted toward large bank stocks, and the heavy buying pressure in these stocks is pulling the index upward; a "selective rally" structure prevails, with broad market participation (weak performance of the broad index) remaining limited.

In this transmission mechanism, the main dynamics are: the exchange rate moving sideways in the 49.30-49.40 band while risk appetite is supported, foreign-origin selling pressure declining, and local institutional/portfolio managers turning to banking-heavy position buying. The locomotive role of the banking index is fueled by net interest margin (NIM) improvement expectations and scenarios of relative easing in deposit costs; meanwhile, technical buy signals in energy (TUPRS, PETKM) and BIM (BIMAS) stocks indicate that sectoral rotation has also spread beyond banking. The dollar-based return tracking the TL return (+1.17% vs +1.27%) reveals that the currency risk premium remains low, foreign investors are in a "wait-and-see" mode, and local liquidity is steering the market.

From a risk transmission perspective; the index approaching the 15.200 psychological resistance, if combined with short-term overbought conditions in banking stocks (VAKBN, YKBNK), could trigger profit-taking. The persistence of selling pressure in the broad market (weakness in low-beta/defensive stocks such as SASA, MGROS) weakens the index's non-banking legs and makes the sustainability of the rally "selective". In a scenario where the exchange rate breaks above 49.50, the current low-volatility environment would deteriorate, and dollar-based returns could fall below TL returns, accelerating foreign outflows; therefore, position sizes should be kept disciplined until the 15.200 resistance is tested, and stop-loss levels in banking stocks should be moved below the 20-day moving average.

3. Macro Outlook and News Flow

Macro Outlook and News Flow

Inflation and Interest Rates: In Turkey, September inflation came in at 1.84% monthly, below expectations (2.3%), while annual inflation declined to 29.73%, maintaining its downward trend. PPI at 27.38% annually, remaining below CPI, indicates that producer cost pressures are easing. This picture supports the policy rate being held at 37.0%, keeping real interest rates in positive territory; however, the rise in consumer loan rates to 64.74% shows that tightness in the credit channel continues to constrain domestic demand. The sustainability of the strong trend in banking stocks depends on the extent to which this rigidity in loan pricing will support the net interest margin, despite the sideways movement in deposit costs.

Exchange Rate and External Balance: The sideways movement of USD/TRY at 49.34 keeps currency volatility low despite the weekly decline in foreign exchange reserves ($60.33 billion). The current account balance approaching equilibrium at $0,036 billion in July and September exports rising to $25.98 billion confirm that the improvement in the trade deficit continues. This outlook justifies BIST 30's positive divergence from the broad market, supported by dollar-based returns backing the TL; however, imports remaining significantly higher than exports at $31.21 billion reminds us that the improvement in the external balance is still fragile.

Global Risk Appetite and Commodities: Following attacks on Saudi Arabia, Brent crude holding at $106.46/barrel and WTI rising 1.23% to $94.89/barrel keep inflationary pressure alive through energy costs. In the US, the policy rate remaining at 4.0% and the 30-year Treasury yield declining to 5,563% stand out as factors supporting global risk appetite. In contrast, US September non-farm payrolls coming in at 29 thousand, well below expectations, keeps growth concerns on the agenda; this situation, despite the upward move in oil prices supporting energy stocks like TUPRS, causes selling pressure in the broad market (SASA, MGROS) to persist.

Investor Psychology and Sectoral Reflections: Although consumer confidence rising to 91.9 points and retail sales increasing 1.4% monthly signal a revival in domestic demand, the 1.0% monthly contraction in industrial production confirms weakness on the production side. This contradictory picture causes investors to act selectively and turn toward banking and energy groups with strong earnings expectations. The deepening selling pressure in aviation and automotive stocks is consistent with the cancellation of some flights from Istanbul following the attacks on Saudi Arabia and concerns about global growth; this outlook suggests that the scenario of the index testing the 15.200 resistance could occur under banking leadership, but weakness in the broad market could limit this move.

Therefore, it should be remembered that in position changes, not only balance sheet sizes but also sectoral momentum and technical signals should be monitored together.

6. Risk Management and Scenarios

6. Risk Management and Scenarios

Main Scenario (probability: high): Strong buy signals in banking stocks (VAKBN, YKBNK) and the index's positive divergence in dollar terms support the potential for XU030 to test the 15.200 resistance. For this scenario to be valid, the horizontal course in USD/TRY (49.39) must be maintained and the selling pressure in the broad market (SASA, MGROS) must not spill over into the index. In this case, it is recommended to maintain banking-weighted positions in the portfolio, but to gradually deploy the existing cash buffer (consistent with the CASH weight in section 7) according to risk appetite.

Risk Scenario (probability: medium): The deepening of selling pressure in the broad market and the acceleration of losses in stocks with weak signals, especially SASA (-0.53%) and MGROS (-0.75%), could lead to the index failing to test the 15.200 resistance and giving back current gains. In this scenario, if the strong signals in banking stocks (VAKBN, YKBNK) weaken and the exchange rate rises above the 49.39 level, protection should be provided by increasing the cash ratio in the portfolio. In particular, zeroing out positions in aviation (THYAO, PGSUS) and Ford Otosan (FROTO) stocks carrying STRONG SELL signals will reduce risk.

Protective Shields: Two key tools stand out to protect the portfolio against possible downward movements. First, not carrying positions in stocks giving a STRONG SELL signal (THYAO, PGSUS, FROTO, MGROS) or rapidly reducing existing positions; second, keeping the cash/liquidity ratio high, to be activated if the index fails to break the 15.200 resistance. In this way, both the portfolio value loss is limited in a possible correction arising from selling pressure in the broad market, and funds are reserved for new buying opportunities if strong signals in banking stocks continue.
