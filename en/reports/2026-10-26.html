This picture strengthens the scenario that net interest margin (NIM) pressure in the banking index could extend into coming quarters and the improving trend in non-performing loan ratios could reverse; even if technical signals say "Buy" (e.g., SAHOL WT signal), long position risk/reward remains weak until margin confirmation arrives.

In FX markets, USD/TRY remaining almost flat at 49.71 (daily -0.03%) and DXY holding at 101.80 provides a temporary breather for currency-driven cost pressure on industrial companies (especially import-heavy automotive, white goods, and chemicals). September US retail sales rising 0.8% monthly, industrial production up 0.7%, and core inflation staying at 2.6% revive expectations that the Fed may slow its rate cut pace; this increases selective selling pressure on emerging market portfolio inflows (EPFR data should be monitored). In the Eurozone, industrial production contracting -0.5% monthly and -1.1% annually in August, and Germany's locomotive still failing to pull, raise demand-side risks for exporting BIST 30 companies (especially automotive, machinery, chemicals). Technical oversold signals (RSI <30, CCI20 <-300) could trigger a rebound buying, but this external squeeze in the macro function keeps the rebound's durability limited.

In local demand indicators, industrial production contracting -1.0% monthly and -1.86% annually in August, and retail sales remaining weak in real terms (annual 10.1% growth is negative real growth relative to inflation), indicate that sales volume emphasis in consumption-focused stocks (FOOD, CONSUMER, AUTO) makes margin improvement difficult.
