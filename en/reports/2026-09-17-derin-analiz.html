1. Market Summary and Overview

Turkish markets, under pressure from global and local uncertainties, are following a cautious and consolidated path. The BIST 30 index is taking a breather in the 4.200-4.250 resistance band; as long as it remains below this critical level, the ground for a clear uptrend has not yet been formed. The short-term momentum in energy and retail stocks that stood out in previous days has largely weakened today. While over 90% of all stocks in the BIST 30 index are giving "STRONG SELL" or "SELL" signals, market risk appetite has weakened. In this environment, the fundamental perspective of fund management is to prioritize capital protection, maintain high cash positions, and look for limited buying opportunities only in technically resilient stocks. The market's main focus is on macroeconomic risks such as the volatility that the Fed's interest rate decision will create and the impact of geopolitical tensions on energy prices.

2. Sector-Based Evaluation

Heat map and signal density data show a clear weakness and risk concentration in the sectoral distribution of BIST 30. The dominant theme in the market is that the financial sector and energy-industry groups are under general selling pressure. The banking sector is experiencing a complete "STRONG SELL" pressure with VAKBN, YKBNK, GARAN, AKBNK and ISCTR stocks. Similarly, the energy and petrochemical sector is also weak with ASTOR, PETKM, ENKAI and TUPRS stocks. In particular, ASTOR stands out with a dramatic 18.10% value loss in the last 5 days. The holding group (KCHOL, SAHOL) is also dominated by "SELL" and "STRONG SELL" signals. In this general weakness environment, the only exception is BIMAS (BİM). BIMAS, unlike all other stocks, gives a "NEUTRAL" signal. The retail sector shows relative resistance due to stable consumer demand and inflation resilience. Other sectors such as defense, automotive, telecommunications, real estate and aviation are almost entirely closed with "STRONG SELL" signals, so they have taken their share of the risk spread across the market. This situation emphasizes the critical importance of sectoral distribution in portfolio creation and the need to avoid portfolios heavily weighted in financial and energy stocks.

5. Risk Map and Scenarios

Under current market conditions, risk management is the most critical factor for protecting portfolio value. Among potential short-term downside scenarios are the Fed implementing tighter-than-expected policy and increasing global recession concerns. This scenario increases the risk of the BIST 30 index testing the 4.000 support level. On the other hand, rising geopolitical tensions and energy prices permanently exceeding the 71 €/MWh level will create additional pressure on energy and industrial companies. To protect the portfolio against these risks, it is essential to maintain a high cash position (40-50%) and be extremely selective with new purchases. Leverage should be completely avoided. Stop-loss levels for positions in the portfolio must be determined. The upside scenarios are more limited. Scenario 1: A positive surprise regarding the Fed's interest rate decision boosts global risk appetite, causing the BIST 30 index to break the 4.250 resistance and rise to the 4.350-4.400 band. Scenario 2: An unexpected drop in energy prices leads to improved profit margins in petrochemical and energy industrial companies and a recovery in these sectors. However, the probability of these scenarios occurring is considered low given current macroeconomic data. Therefore, the "survival" principle is central to the strategy. The portfolio should be separated from fundamentally weak stocks by holding inflation-resistant stocks with solid financial indicators. As a split strategy, opening small positions for potential upside opportunities while keeping the majority of the portfolio in safe assets would be the most logical approach.
