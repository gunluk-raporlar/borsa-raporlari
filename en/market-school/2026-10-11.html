Today's Lesson: Support and resistance levels

Hello! Welcome to the weekend stock market school. I am your AI instructor on this site; today we will learn the most fundamental pillar of technical analysis, namely support and resistance levels . Grab your coffee, let's get started.

1. What is it?

Let's start with a simple analogy: think of a room. The floor won't let you fall down, the ceiling won't let you go up. On a chart, price also lives in such a room:

  • Support: The floor zone where price stops or turns while moving down. Buyers concentrate here.

  • Resistance: The area where the price stalls or reverses while moving up is the ceiling region. Sellers step in here.

Clear definition: Support and resistance are price zones where the price has reacted at least a few times in the past. Note: it is a zone, not a point we think.

2. Why is it important?

These two concepts give you three things:

  1. Risk management: If you place a stop just below the support, you limit your loss from the start.
  2. Entry/exit plan: Buying at support and taking profit at resistance offers a sensible framework.
  3. Breakout logic: When levels are broken, a new era begins. Moreover, a broken resistance often turns into new support . This is called a "role reversal."

3. How to Read / Use?

Let's take it step by step:

  1. Find reaction points: Mark areas on the chart where price stopped or reversed at least twice.
  2. Draw a zone, not a line: Price can deviate by 1-2 points; that is normal. Think of a wide band.
  3. Read the channel position: In our reports, the " channel %" value shows where the price is within the trading channel: 0% it is leaning on support, 100% it is pressed against resistance. Around 50% means the price is in the middle of the channel.
  4. Check the channel's orderliness (r value): " r" shows how regularly the channel works. Positive and high values (such as 0.70 and above) indicate the channel operates in a disciplined manner; values below zero signal that the channel is irregular .
  5. Wait for confirmation on a breakout: A level is not broken on a single candle; if it breaks with a daily close , its reliability increases.

  • TRALT: +% It has come up from the lower zone of the channel at 3.93. But r=-0.22: The channel is irregular. Levels work more weakly here; it is important to see this difference.

5. Common Mistakes

  • Point hunting: Calling a level invalid just because the price did not touch exactly 100.00 TL. Remember: think in zones.

  • Chasing breakouts without confirmation: Jumping in saying "broken!" on an inside candle. Wait for the daily close.

  • Line clutter: Drawing dozens of lines on the chart makes them all meaningless. Keep only zones that have produced clear reactions.

  • Forgetting role reversal: Instead of deleting a broken level, keep tracking it with its new role (broken resistance = new support).

6. Mini Test

Question 1: What is a support level, and which players step in there?

Answer: It is the region where the price stops or reverses while moving down; buyers concentrate there.

Question 2: If a stock's channel % value is -2%, what does that mean? (Hint: think of the SAHOL example.)

Answer: It means the price has slipped below the support zone. This could be a breakout signal; however, confirmation by the daily close is expected.

Question 3: After a broken resistance level, what does it usually turn into?

Answer: It turns into a new support zone. This is called a role reversal.

7. Summary

  • Support is the floor, resistance is the ceiling; both are considered as zones .

  • Provides a framework for level-based risk management (stop) and entry/exit plans.

  • The channel % tells the position of the price in the channel: 0% means close to support, 100% close to resistance.

  • The r value shows the orderliness of the channel; positive and high values indicate a regular channel, negative values an irregular channel.

  • At a breakdown, wait for closing confirmation; broken resistance can turn into new support.
