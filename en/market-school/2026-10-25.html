Today's Lesson: Ceiling and Floor Price Margin

Welcome to the BIST 30 Günlük Raporlar site's Hafta Sonu Borsa Okulu!
Today, ceiling and floor price margin we will examine the topic in depth.
Rather than an academic theory, we will work with real market data "with the pen in your hand".

1. What is it?

A simple analogy

Imagine you are waiting at a bus stop. The bus has a rule of "maximum 10 minutes delay". This rule limits how far the bus can go (delay); if it exceeds 10 minutes, the bus does not stop, giving passengers a warning "we are late".

The stock market also applies a similar "delay limit": on each trading day, a stock's price cannot exceed 10% of the previous closing price. This 10% limit is two-way:

  • Ceiling price (upper limit) = Previous close × (1 + 10%)

  • Floor price (lower limit) = Previous close × (1 – 10%)

These limits are set to curb excessive volatility, make market manipulation more difficult, and limit investors' sudden losses.

Clear definition

Ceiling price margin, is the highest price level a share can reach in a trading day; floor price margin is the lowest level it can reach within the same day. Both values are determined within a 10% framework based on the previous closing price and form the basis of Borsa İstanbul's "circuit‑breaker" (circuit breaker) mechanism.

2. Why is it important?

  1. Risk control – Since a movement of more than 10% within the day is not expected, you can plan your stop‑loss levels within this framework.
  2. Liquidity and trading volume – When limits are exceeded, the exchange halts trading; this preserves liquidity and reduces excessive buying‑selling pressure.
  3. Price forecast – If the price reaches the ceiling or floor at the end of the day, it gives that day's "momentum" signal; this signal can affect short‑term buy‑sell decisions.
  4. Market sensitivity – Stocks that hit the ceiling or floor levels typically react quickly to news flow, macro data (e.g., inflation), or company announcements.

Therefore, knowing the ceiling‑floor margin, when shaping your daily strategy It lets you see both your profit potential and your loss risk more clearly.

3. How to Read / Use?

Step 1 – Find the previous closing price

In BIST 30 Daily Reports, take the closing value of the relevant stock from the previous trading day from the "Close" column.

Step 2 – Calculate the ceiling and floor prices

[
\ text{Ceiling Price} = \text{Close} \times (1 + 0.10)
]

[
\ text{Floor Price} = \text{Close} \times (1 - 0.10)
]

Step 3 – Compare with the daily percentage change

Check how close the intraday percentage change (% Change) ceiling or floor value it is to.

  • % A drop close to 10 → means the price has likely hit the floor.

  • % A rise close to 10 → means the price has likely hit the ceiling.

Step 4 – Relate to the price channel

In the reports, the "channel" column shows the range of movement of the stock price around the average. You can find the upper and lower bands by multiplying the channel % value by the average price (MA):

[
\ text{Upper Band} = \text{MA} \times (1 + \text{Channel})
]

[
\ text{Lower Band} = \text{MA} \times (1 - \text{Channel})
]

These bands, independent of ceiling-floor limits as, in technical analysis, it shows the "normal" fluctuation boundaries of the price.

Step 5 – Shape your decision

  • Hitting the floor: Review your stop-loss level, is it a short-term buying opportunity or panic selling?

  • Hitting the ceiling: Set your take-profit level, is it an overbought signal or a continuation of the trend?

5. Common Mistakes

Error Why is it wrong?
1. Assuming the price ceiling/floor is taken as "exact" and that the price will definitely hit that level. During the day, the price may touch the ceiling or floorcan touch but it usually closes without exceeding that level. Market liquidity, news flow, and the order book "soften" these levels.
2. Confusing the channel % value with the price ceiling/floor limit The channel shows the technical trading range around the average price; the price ceiling/floor is the legal 10% limit. They serve different purposes.
3. Comparing the daily 10% limit with annual inflation Inflation is a long-term macro measure; the daily 10% limit is merely a rule that limits price movement within a single trading day. The comparison leads to a false risk perception.
4. Setting the price ceiling/floor level as an "absolute" stop‑loss Stop‑loss levels should be set according to the investor's risk tolerance; setting them solely based on the price ceiling/floor level can cause unnecessary early exits.

6. Mini Test

Question 1
If a stock's previous closing price is 120 TL, what are the ceiling and floor prices for that day?

Answer

  • Ceiling = 120 × 1.10 = 132 TL

  • Floor = 120 × 0.90 = 108 TL

Question 2
Given that EREGL's current price is 31.60 TL and its daily change is –9.97%, which price ceiling/floor limit did this stock hit?

Answer
It hit the floor price (31.60 TL).

Question 3
ASELS's 30‑day price range is 322.25 TL – 393.75 TL. If the close 30 days ago was 350 TL, is this range within the ceiling‑floor limits?

Answer
Yes. Ceiling = 385 TL, floor = 315 TL; the range (322‑394 TL) stays within these limits.

7. Summary

  1. Ceiling = Previous close × 1.10, Floor = Previous close × 0.90.
  2. The daily 10% limit curbs excessive volatility and clarifies the investor's risk framework.
  3. The daily percent change is the most practical indicator showing how close you are to the ceiling‑floor levels.
  4. Channel (price channel) gives the price's "normal" movement range in technical analysis; ceiling‑floor is a legal limit.
  5. To avoid misinterpretations, keep ceiling‑floor, channel, inflation, and stop‑loss strategies separate.
