Today's Lesson: Stop-loss and risk management

1. What is it?

Imagine a mountaineer. While climbing to the summit, they tie a rope around their waist. The rope doesn't mean "it will catch you if you fall"; it means "if you fall, you know where you'll stop." Stop-loss is exactly this: knowing in advance where you'll stop when the market turns against you.

A stop-loss is a price level you set in advance to close a position. When the stock reaches that level, you sell; thus you stop a small loss from turning into a big one. But a stop-loss alone doesn't mean much. Three concepts work together with it:

  • Position size: How much money you put into a trade.

  • Stop level: The price point at which you accept the loss.

  • Risk/reward ratio: The ratio between the money you risk and the profit you target.

Risk management, on the other hand, is managing these three together. In other words, before the question "which stock will I buy," it means answering the question "how much can I lose at most in this trade." This is the most fundamental difference that separates the professional investor from the novice: the professional plans the loss first, then the profit.

2. Why is it important?

Because just as winning in the market is a skill, protecting gains is also a skill. If an account drops 50%, it needs to rise 100% to return to its former level. This math is ruthless. Stop-loss and correct position sizing significantly reduce the likelihood of falling into this trap.

This knowledge gives the investor the following:

  • It reduces emotional decisions: In a moment of panic, you don't wonder "what will I do"; you have already made your plan in advance.

  • It protects capital: Small losses accumulate but don't finish off the account. Large losses, however, end the game.

  • It improves decision quality: " The question "What is my risk in this trade?" automatically eliminates unnecessary trades.

  • It ensures expectation management: There is no guarantee that every trade will win; what matters is that the winners are bigger than the losers.

In short, risk management is balancing the question "how much will I earn" with the question "how much am I willing to lose."

3. How to Read / Use?

Let's take it step by step:

Step 1 — Determine the risk percentage per trade. General rule: Do not risk more than 1% to 2% of your account in a single trade. This means "one trade shouldn't knock me out of the game."

Step 2 — Determine the stop level. The stop is not an arbitrary percentage; it is placed at a technical point on the chart such as a support, a moving average, or the last low. The difference between the entry price and the stop is your risk per share.

Step 3 — Calculate the position size. The formula is as follows:

Position Size (shares) = (Account Size × Risk % per Trade) / (Entry Price − Stop Price)

Where is each component here read from? Account size is your portfolio value. The risk percentage is your rule. The entry price is the price at which you plan to buy. The stop price, meanwhile, is the technical level you determine on the chart.

Step 4 — Check the risk/reward ratio. Formula:

Risk/Reward = (Entry Price − Stop Price) / (Target Price − Entry Price)

General acceptance: At least 1 to 2, meaning for every 1 unit you risk, aim for at least 2 units of potential gain. You also read the target price from the resistance zones on the chart.

Step 5 — Apply and maintain discipline. Once the stop level is set, it is not moved down. Shifting the stop is like a climber cutting their own rope.

4. Real Example

Let's go through our lesson chart using GARAN. Over the last 30 trading days the change is +19.1%, range 125.20-155.40 TL. Let's say your account size is 100.000 TL and your per-trade risk rule is 1%, i.e. 1.000 TL.

Scenario: Entry price 150.00 TL. The last support on the chart is 140.00 TL; you set the stop level at 140.00 TL. Your risk per share:

150.00 − 140.00 = 10.00 TL

Position size:

( 100.000 TL × 1%) / 10.00 TL = 1.000 TL / 10.00 TL = 100 shares

So if you buy 100 shares, your total position becomes 15.000 TL, and if the stop triggers your loss is exactly 1.000 TL, i.e. 1% of your account. This is a trade that complies with your rule.

Risk/reward check: You set your target as 175.00 TL, the resistance zone on the chart.

Risk/Reward = (150.00 − 140.00) / (175.00 − 150.00) = 10.00 / 25.00 = 1 to 2.5

This ratio is healthy: with 1 unit of risk you target 2.5 units of potential gain. If you did the same calculation with an entry near the upper end of the range, such as 155.40 TL, your position size would shrink because the stop distance would widen; this is risk management's automatic brake.

An example from volatile stocks: ASTOR last 163.80 TL, daily −7.56%. A drop exceeding 7% in a single day shows why stop discipline matters. While an investor without a stop watches this move thinking "it'll turn around anyway," an investor with a stop limits their loss at the level they determined in advance. The same rule applies on positive days like TOASO at 243.00 TL and TRMET at 110.80 TL: even on winning days, a trade without a stop plan is an unplanned trade.

5. Common Mistakes

  • Setting the stop too close: The reason is wrong; normal daily fluctuation triggers the stop and cuts you off from the trend. The stop should be based on a technical level, not an arbitrary percentage.

  • Moving the stop down: The reason is wrong; "let me wait a little longer" is an emotional decision and turns a small loss into a big loss. The stop is set once and applied with discipline.

  • Buying without calculating position size: The reason is wrong; even if the stop is correct, if the position is too large, a single trade puts a large part of your account at risk. Risk management has two legs: stop + size.

  • Focusing only on profit and skipping the risk/reward ratio: The reason is wrong; a trade with a 1 to 0.5 ratio, even if it wins, mathematically produces a loss in the long run.

6. Mini Test

Question 1: Your account is 200.000 TL, your risk rule per trade is 1%. Entry 50.00 TL, stop 46.00 TL. How many shares should your position size be?

Answer: Risk amount 2.000 TL. Risk per share 4.00 TL. 2.000 / 4.00 = 500 shares.

Question 2: Entry 100.00 TL, stop 95.00 TL, target 115.00 TL. What is the risk/reward ratio and is it acceptable?

Answer: (100 − 95) / (115 − 100) = 5 / 15 = 1 to 3. According to the general rule it is acceptable, even good.

Question 3: Why is the stop level placed at a technical point instead of an arbitrary percentage?

Answer: Because technical levels are the points where the market actually reacts; arbitrary percentages get triggered early in normal fluctuation and lead to unnecessary exits.

7. Summary

  • Stop-loss is stopping the loss at a predetermined point; like a climber's rope.

  • Risk management has three legs: position size, stop level, risk/reward ratio.

  • Limit the risk per trade to 1-2% of your account; calculate the number of shares with the formula.

  • Risk/reward should be at least 1 to 2; never move the stop down.

  • Small losses keep the game going; big losses end the game.
