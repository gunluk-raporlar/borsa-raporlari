Today's Lesson: Regression channel

Hello friends! The most enjoyable hour of the weekend: Stock Market School. Today we are fitting a statistical eyeglass to your charts. If you are ready, let's begin.

1. What is it?

First an analogy: Imagine a cyclist riding on a road. The road is a straight slope; the cyclist, however, sways from side to side along the road. There are guardrails on both sides of the road.

The regression channel is exactly this:

  • The middle line: The straight trend line that best fits the price action of the last 60 days (the road the cyclist follows).

  • Lower band: The 0% level of the channel (left guardrail).

  • Upper band: The 100% level of the channel (right guardrail).

Net definition: The regression channel is the statistical band drawn around the linear trend of the last 60 days. The price shows its position within these bands; 0% means the lower band, 100% the upper band.

2. Why is it important?

Because our eyes deceive us, the statistic does not.

  • It objectifies the trend: " Instead of saying "I think it's rising," you look at the 60-day math.

  • It gives positional awareness: Where is the price relative to the bands? At the bottom or at the top? You see it at a glance.

  • It measures confidence: Thanks to the r value, you understand how "real" the trend is. Drawing shapes is easy; r tells you whether the shape fits the data.

In short: the regression channel allows you to evaluate the stock within its trend.

3. How to Read / Use?

Ask the three questions in order:

Step 1 – What is the trend direction?
Look at the slope of the middle line. Upward slope = uptrend. Downward slope = downtrend. Flat = sideways market.

Step 2 – Where is the price in the channel? (Channel % value)

  • % Close to 0: Price is at the lower band. A "floor" zone relative to the trend.

  • % Around 50: Price is exactly in the middle of the channel. Neutral zone.

  • % Close to 100: Price is at the upper band. A "ceiling" zone relative to the trend.

  • % Below 0 (negative value): Price has broken below the channel. Look carefully.

Step 3 – How reliable is the trend? (r value)

  • r = 0.70 and above: Strong fit. The trend line represents the data well.

  • r = 0.30 – 0.70: Moderate fit. The channel gives an idea but is not sufficient on its own.

  • r below 0.30: Weak fit. Trust little of what the channel says.

  • If r is negative: The 60-day slope is downward, meaning there is a downtrend.

Golden rule: Answer these three questions together together. None of them alone is a signal.

4. Real Example

Let's read four stocks together with today's data.

VAKBN – From the site's "strong buy" list:
Last price 33.90 TL, daily change +0.71%. Channel 32%, r = 0.76.
Reading: The trend fit is strong (r = 0.76), so the channel we drew is meaningful. The price is in the lower half of the channel (32%); there is quite a distance to the upper band. Combining the "strong buy" label with the channel, we say: "The trend is established, and the price is in the lower zone of the trend."

SAHOL:
Last price 83.50 TL, daily change -5.92%. Channel -2%, r = 0.11.
Reading: The price has broken below the channel (-2%). But r = 0.11! The trend does not fit the data; the channel is "not talking." We should not put too much meaning into the below-band appearance.

TRMET:
Last price 137.30 TL, daily change +4.97%. Channel 52%, r = 0.26.
Reading: The price is right in the middle (52%). Since the fit is weak, the channel is a neutral frame; it does not give striking information.

TRALT:
Last price 43.90 TL, daily change +3.93%. Channel 24%, r = -0.22.
Reading: The real lesson is here! R is negative, so the 60-day slope is downward. The daily +3.93% rise is a move against the falling trend. A single strong day does not change the trend.

Have you seen it? Same day, four different stories.

5. Common Mistakes

  • Thinking that touching the upper band alone is a sell signal: In strong trends, price can stick to the upper band and keep moving. The band does not say "sell" in one word.

  • Making meaningful inferences from a channel with weak r: Channels with r between 0.10 and 0.25 do not provide reliability. Look at r first, then interpret.

  • Drawing trend conclusions from a single day's move: Don't forget the TRALT example; a daily +3.93% and a 60-day negative slope do not cancel each other out.

  • Looking for a channel in every chart: In flat and mixed markets, the regression channel loses its meaning. If r is weak, do not draw the tool.

6. Mini Test

Question 1: If a stock's channel % value is 100%, where is the price?
Answer: It is exactly at the upper band. It is in the "ceiling" zone relative to the trend; but this alone is not a sell signal.

Question 2: How much should we trust a channel with r = 0.20?
Answer: Very little. The fit is weak; the trend line may not represent the price movement. Using the channel as an interpretation tool is misleading.

Question 3: TRALT rose +3.93% daily but r = -0.22. What do these two together say?
Answer: The 60-day slope is downward; the day's rise is a move against the trend. A single day is not enough to change an established trend.

7. Summary

  • The regression channel is a statistical band drawn around the linear trend of the last 60 days; 0% is the lower band and 100% is the upper band.

  • Channel % It shows the position of the price within the bands, r it indicates the strength of the trend's fit to the data.

  • If r is high the channel speaks; if r is weak or negative the reading changes.

  • Bands are, depending on the trend, caution zones; they are not a standalone buy-sell signal.

  • Always ask three questions together: Direction? Position? Confidence?
