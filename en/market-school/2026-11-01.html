Today's Lesson: Uptick Rule

The transaction we call "short-selling" aims to sell a share without owning it and buy it back later at a lower price. However, if prices keep falling, this strategy may choke the market with excessive selling pressure. At this point Up-Tick Rule (Uptick Rule) comes into play. In this lesson we will examine what the rule is, why it is important, how it is applied and the step-by-step sampling over a real stock.

1. What is it?

Simple analogy:
At a bus stop, passengers are only allowed to board when the bus moves “forward” (i.e., leaves the stop and heads to the next stop). When the bus tries to go backward, you do not take on new passengers; because the bus is already going backward and adding passengers would only congest traffic further.

The uptick rule works with the same logic:
Short selling only a step up (uptick) – that is, the price's rising to a higher level than the previous transaction – is permitted when it takes place. At the moments the price moves at the same level (zero-tick) or downward (downtick), opening a new short position is prohibited.

This rule was applied at the US stock exchange between 1938‑2007 and was reinstated between 2007‑2010 as the "alternative uptick rule" (Rule 201). In Türkiye, Borsa İstanbul's "Short-Sale Restriction Rule" (short‑sale restriction rule) similar regulations carry the same principle, especially in high-volatility periods.

Full Definition (150‑250 words):
The Up-Tick Rule, when the price of a security within a downtrend new short sale orders may only to a level higher than the previous transaction (uptick) makes its acceptance mandatory on the condition of it rising. The rule blocks the entering of new short positions in the "downtick" (the price falling to the same or a lower level) or "zero-tick" (the price staying the same) cases. Thereby, the market participants do not unilaterally increase the price's downward acceleration; they preserve the liquidity and have limited the oversold pressure. The rule, generally under the name "Short Sale Restriction Rule", comes into force automatically in Borsa İstanbul on the days a specific stock's price shows a fall of more than 10%.

2. Why is it important?

  1. Market stability – Limiting new short positions in a downtrend prevents sudden and excessive price collapses.
  2. Liquidity protection – Sellers constantly adding "shorts" reduces buyers' power to lift the price. The rule preserves the buyer-seller balance.
  3. Investor confidence – As investors see that sudden panic sales have been prevented, their confidence in the bourse increases.
  4. Strategic decisions – Investors considering short selling can time their positions by knowing when the rule is in effect; this improves risk management.
  5. Protection in an inflationary environment – In Türkiye, a high annual inflation like 29.7% can strain real returns. Short selling restrictions soften speculative collapses in periods of inflation-created uncertainty.

3. How to Read / Use?

3.1. To understand whether the rule is triggered

  1. Track the price movement – The stock's daily closing price, lowest and highest levels.
  2. % 10 decline control – If a stock, compared with the previous trading day 10% or more, the rule comes into force.
  3. Uptick check – While the rule is in effect, if you want to place a new short-sale order, to a level higher than the previous transaction (uptick) must come out.

3.2. Formula (in plain text)

Short Sale Permission = (Price Change ≥ -10%) OR (Price Change < -10% AND Current Trading Price > Previous Trading Price)

  • Price Change = ( Today's Close – Previous Day's Close) / Previous Day's Close × 100

  • Previous Transaction Price = the last price realized within the same day (example: 100 TL at 09:30)

  • Current Trading Price = the price at the moment a new order is entered

ENKAI over the last 30 days ‑10.6 % having fallen, 10% exceeded. Consequently Uptick Rule for this stock come into effect is the state.

Step 2: Check current price

Let's say ENKAI's current trading price 78.00 TL. Previous transaction price (as an example, the price of the previous minute) 77.80 TL let it be.

  • Price Change (daily) = (78.00 – (78.00 / (1‑0.106))) / (78.00 / (1‑0.106)) × 100 ≈ ‑10.6 % ( the given value)

  • Uptick check: Current price 78.00 TL, the previous price 77.80 TL → 78.00 TL > 77.80 TL → Uptick occurred.

5. Common Mistakes

Error Why is it wrong?
1. To interpret the word "Uptick" as "uptrend" Uptick, only a single transaction means the price is higher than the previous level during this period; it is not directly related to the trend direction.
2. to think the rule applies only to "short selling" and not to "long" (buy) transactions The rule only restricts short selling; there is no restriction on buy orders.
3. To ignore the 10% decline threshold and not check the rule daily The rule only applies to declines of more than 10%; no uptick check is needed in low-volatility conditions.
4. to think one can sell short without limit when there are multiple upticks intraday For each new short sale each transaction requires an uptick; if the price stays at the same level (zero-tick), a new short sale is not accepted.

6. Mini Test

Question 1: A share fell 12% from the previous day's close. Today the price is 45.00 TL and the previous trade price is 44.80 TL. Can a new short-sale order be placed?

Answer: Yes. Since the decline exceeds 10%, the rule is in effect, but the price made an uptick from 44.80 TL → 45.00 TL, so short selling is allowed.

Question 2: ENKAI's price fell from 78.00 TL to 77.90 TL. I want to place another short-sale order on the same day. Is that possible?

Answer: No. A price downtick ( made; since no uptick occurred, a new short sale is restricted.

Question 3: % 9.5 decline share is the uptick rule applied?

Answer: No. The rule only applies to declines of 10% or more; at a 9.5% decline, no uptick check is needed.

7. Summary

  • Uptick Rule, in a price fall allows new short sales only on the condition that the price rises to a level higher than the previous transaction (uptick).

  • % 10 decline threshold determines the rule's activation; when this threshold is not exceeded, the rule is not applied.

  • The rule, the market stability, liquidity andthe investor confidence protects; it especially softens the speculative collapses in a high inflation environment.

  • Through a real stock (ENKAI) step by checking, we can see how the short sale permission is determined.

  • Among the common mistakes are mistaking the uptick for the trend, thinking of the rule only with respect to short selling and% to forget the threshold 10 is included.
