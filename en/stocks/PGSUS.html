🌍 Macroeconomic Evaluation

The macro equation for the aviation sector is three-legged: jet fuel price, exchange rate and domestic demand. The moderate trend in oil prices supports fuel costs, while Pegasus's high foreign currency obligations make the USD/TL trajectory the most critical variable in the balance sheet; TL remaining balanced at 48.83 level limits the pressure on losses. The decline in inflation to 31.5% and interest rate cut expectations carry potential relief on financing and leasing costs. Domestic demand remains strong; extension of tourism season and capacity increase on international routes support occupancy rates. The strong trend in foreign tourist inflows feeds international route revenues in foreign currency. In an environment where gram gold is at 6.786 TL, the non-postponement of travel expenses is a positive signal in terms of consumer behavior. Main risks; exchange rate shock, impact of geopolitical developments on tourism flows and sharp increase in fuel prices.

🧾 Balance Sheet Evaluation

Pegasus, despite increasing its sales by 26.5% in the first half of 2026, showed a loss-adjacent appearance with a 602.5% decline in net profit; announced a net loss of 4.8 billion TL for the second quarter with 45.7 billion TL sales (+19.0). The main driver of the loss is the exchange rate difference and monetary position expenses created by the high foreign currency open position under inflation accounting; operational side remains relatively resilient. The company, which generated 198.0 billion TL sales and 1.1 billion TL net profit in the last four quarters, has a market value of ~75 billion TL; due to low profit, P/E ratio appears unreasonably high at ~65.8x, therefore valuation should be followed via P/B ~0.7x and EV/Sales. Financial debt/equity at 226.4 with one of the highest leverages in BIST-30; ~185 billion TL net debt (aircraft financing weighted) is structurally necessary for the sector but creates full sensitivity to interest and exchange rate volatility. ROE at 1.0 is at break-even level. Occupancy rates and unit revenues are strong; the problem is not operational but in the financial structure. In summary, Pegasus is a growth story operationally healthy but with high balance sheet leverage; it has the capacity to quickly repair the profit statement with exchange rate stability and interest rate cuts.
