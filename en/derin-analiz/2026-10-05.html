During the trading session, the BIST 30 index showed a narrow rise with a daily increase of 2.03%. The 1.95% return in USD terms is explained by the slight depreciation of the TL, while the only 0.08% increase in USD/TRY indicated stable market liquidity. The 1.80% rise in BIST 100 points to more limited participation compared to BIST 30's performance; this suggests that the index's rise was driven mainly by a few large codes within BIST 30.

From a sectoral perspective, technical scan signals are concentrated with "BUY" and "STRONG BUY" predominantly in banking (Yapi Kredi), real estate (Emlak Konut GYO), aviation (TAV Airports), metal-mining (Kardemir, Eregli Demir Celik), and defense (Aselsan) stocks. On the other hand, "SELL" and "STRONG SELL" signals dominate in codes such as energy-petrochemicals (Astor Enerji), finance-other (Destek Finans Faktoring), and aviation (Turk Hava Yollari).

In the macro environment, September consumer inflation showed a slight slowdown at 31.51% annually (1.84% monthly), while producer inflation continued its upward trend at 27.95% annually (2.57% monthly). With the policy rate remaining steady at 37.0%, deposit rates declined to 43,795%, while consumer loan rates remained high at 64.74%. Loan growth continues at 2.3% annually, and M3 money supply is up 29.71%. This structure offers an environment where funding costs for the banking sector may improve slightly, but loan demand could slow down.

As for exchange rate movement, USD/TRY showed limited weakness with a 0.08% increase, while the 0,1743% decline in DXY indicates a slight decrease in global risk appetite. The real effective exchange rate remained unchanged from the 23.47 index level; this provides a competitive advantage for exporters while increasing cost pressure on producers using imported inputs.

In summary, the rise of BIST 30 with narrow participation is based on the performance of a limited group of stocks giving technical "BUY" signals. Macro data show that although consumer-side inflation is slowing, producer prices still create pressure, while credit conditions are tending to soften. In this framework, the sustainability of the market movement depends on the ability of codes giving "BUY" signals to broaden participation and on macro risks (inflation, exchange rate, loan growth) maintaining stability.
