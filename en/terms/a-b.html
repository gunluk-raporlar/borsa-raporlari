Terms & Definitions

of the dictionary of economic, financial and stock market terminology A–B group. The definitions are compiled from official sources such as the TCMB, the Competition Authority and the economics dictionaries. Other letter groups:

A–B C–D E–F G–İ (letters G to İ) J–M N–P R–S T–Z

A

Abuse of a Dominant Position

Undertakings holding significant market power can influence competitive conditions in the market more easily than their rivals. The abuse by dominant undertakings of this power in ways that restrict competition in the market is prohibited under Article 6 of the Competition Act (Law No. 4054). What matters in assessments under Article 6 is that a dominant position itself is not prohibited. An undertaking's market power may result from technological superiority, efficiency or a quality product/service.

Fair income distribution fair distribution of income

It is having equal opportunities in production and consumption and receiving one's due share in a country.

Fair insurance policy a reasonably priced insurance policy

It is the determination of the insurance premium such that it equals the expected value of the insurance policy concerned.

Administered Price

They are prices determined by the enterprise or the public authority and which do not change in response to short-term fluctuations in supply/demand conditions. Some economists attribute this price stickiness to market power.

Moral hazardmoral hazard

It is the fact that economic units make more risky choices when other people will bear the cost of an unfavourable outcome.

Letter of CreditLetter of Credit

A type of guarantee concerning the payment for exported goods, under which payment will be made once certain conditions are fulfilled. Banks or other financial institutions intermediate letter-of-credit transactions. Accordingly, a letter of credit is a kind of letter of guarantee sent by a financial institution to a correspondent bank in a foreign country, committing it to extend credit on its own account for a certain period, in a certain amount and in favor of a third party.

Active job search active job search

; activities such as applying to a job-finding institution or directly to an employer, searching for a job via the Internet or newspapers, taking oral or written job exams or interviews, preparing to start one's own business.

Rational expectations hypothesis rational expectations hypothesis

It is the view that economic agents, when making forecasts about the future, take all information about the past and the future into account.

Fluidity or liquidity liquidity

It is a conceptual measure of how easily and quickly a financial asset can be converted into cash of an equivalent amount.

Algorithmic Collusion

An algorithm is expressed as a path designed to reach a goal, a set of steps followed; in computer science it is defined as the method by which software performs a task. Algorithms can, for example, be used by undertakings as a price-setting tool in online shopping. An undertaking can monitor a rival's product prices through algorithms and set its own prices accordingly. At a more advanced level, algorithms cease to be a tool of competition

Allocation of Shared Costs

The allocation by a multi-product undertaking of its general expenses separately to the production costs of these products. While distributing general expenses among its products, the undertaking may take into account the demand elasticity of those products or the competitive constraints in the markets where they operate. For example, it may exclude general expenses from a product operating in a competitive market with high demand elasticity while keeping its price low in that market.

Buying Delegation

A delegation of representatives of institutions and organizations, media members, producers and importers brought to the country to purchase goods or services, coordinated by the Ministry of Trade in cooperation with exporters' associations and/or organizing bodies, with the aim of increasing exports.

Call option Call Option

It gives the option holder the right to purchase the underlying good, value or financial indicator at the price stated in the contract without creating any obligation at contract maturity.

Written call options are treated as liabilities.( Written call options are treated as liabilities.

English definition: contracts granting the right to buy an asset.

Buy Bid

It is the price at which market participants are willing to buy the assets traded in the market (such as foreign exchange and securities).

Bid-ask spread Bid-Ask Spread

It expresses the difference between the bid and ask prices of any traded value.

A narrow bid-ask spread indicates a liquid market.( A narrow bid-ask spread indicates a liquid market.

English definition: the gap between the best bid and the best offer price.

Purpose independence goal independence

It is the central bank's own determination of its monetary policy objectives.

Amnesty Plus

Under the additional leniency rule, those who cannot benefit from the leniency program in an investigation concerning one market may, if they apply with respect to a cartel in another market, benefit from immunity for the second cartel and receive a reduction in the fines arising from the first cartel. In Turkish competition law, the provision on this subject is made in Article 7(2) of the Fining Regulation.

Main policy rate prime interest rate

It is the rate that forms the basis for the interest banks demand on credit.

Analytical Balance Sheet/TCMB Analytical Balance Sheet Analytical Balance Sheet/CBRT Analytical Balance Sheet

An analytical balance sheet is one in which the accounts on the asset and liability sides are netted against one another and consolidated under appropriate groupings. The CBRT Analytical Balance Sheet consolidates the items on the CBRT balance sheet taking into account the items relevant to the effective conduct of monetary policy, without detailed breakdowns. In line with the functioning of monetary policy, 'Foreign Assets', 'Domestic Assets' and 'Valuation Account' appear under 'Assets', while 'Total Foreign Currency Liabilities' and 'Central Bank Money' appear under 'Liabilities'. For the CBRT Analytical Balance Sheet

Anti-Competitive Agreements

It refers to secret or open agreements between enterprises that restrict competition among them. Such agreements can be horizontal or vertical.

Intermediate targetintermediate target

It is a target that can directly influence the main target of monetary policy and that is clearer and more measurable than the main target.

Intermediate good intermediate good

They are the products that are used up in the production process or that become part of the final good.

Intermediary Institution

These are joint-stock companies that are authorised to act as intermediaries by the Capital Markets Board.

Example: An investment entity does not consolidate its controlled investees but measures them at fair value.(An investment entity does not consolidate its controlled investees but measures them at fair value.)

English definition: an entity raising funds from investors, so as to provide them with investment management services, while measuring its investments at fair value via profit or loss.

Interim profit trade Carry Trade

It consists of borrowing in a lower-interest-rate (cheaper) currency and investing the borrowed amount in a higher-interest-rate (higher-yielding) currency. The net return on the carry trade is, assuming the credit risk remains constant, determined by exchange rate movements.

Fluctuation within the range floating within a range

It is the foreign exchange rate regime in which nominal exchange rates are allowed to vary within a certain range around a central parity determined by the central bank.

Instrument independence instrument independence

It is the central bank's own determination of the policy instruments it will use to achieve its objectives.

Arbitrage Arbitrage

The transaction of making a profit by simultaneously buying a security, currency or commodity in one market and selling it in another (exchanging the securities with one another). Arbitrage transactions lead to a reduction in price differences between markets. Today, with the growth of communication technology and the falling cost of accessing information, price differences between markets are extremely low, and any differences that appear are instantly eliminated by such transactions.

Ascending price auction ascending-price auction

Or English auction, the type of auction in which participants submit open bids and the price is progressively raised from a low starting level until a winner is determined.

Increasing-cost industry industry facing rising costs

It is an industry in which firms' average total cost increases as the market grows.

Progressive taxation progressive taxation

It is a taxation system in which the tax rate rises as the taxable amount increases.

Supply supply

It is the basic economics concept describing how much of a product the sellers in the market want to sell and are able to sell in a given period.

Supply elasticity elasticity of supply

Or price elasticity of supply, abbreviated as ε, a basic type of elasticity showing by what percentage the quantity supplied of a product changes as a result of a one percent increase in its price.

Supply curve supply curve

A graphical tool showing the relationship between the price of a good and the quantity supplied, other things being equal.

Supply surplus excess supply

Or surplus, the situation in which the quantity supplied in the market exceeds the quantity demanded.

Supply schedule supply schedule

It is a list showing how much of a product is supplied at different price levels in a given period.

Supply shock supply shock

It is a change in aggregate supply in the economy caused by a factor other than the general price level.

Price elasticity of supply price elasticity of supply

Or elasticity of supply, abbreviated as ε, a basic type of elasticity showing by what percentage the quantity supplied of a product changes as a result of a one percent increase in its price.

Subsistence minimum allowance minimum living allowance

It is the exemption of a certain part of the wage from tax.

Minimum wage minimum wage

It is the minimum wage determined by the state as the counterpart of labour for a certain period of time and which cannot be reduced by mutual agreement.

Principal-agent problem principal-agent problem

It is the principal-agent situation where, due to asymmetric information, the agent acts in its own interest after authority to carry out a transaction has been delegated.

Asymmetric information asymmetric information

It is one of the parties to an economic activity having more information than the other.

Acid-test ratio Quick ratio / Acid-test ratio

It is the liquidity ratio calculated by deducting inventories from current assets ((Current Assets - Inventories) / Short-term Liabilities); it measures the most liquid items.

Association of Undertakings

Undertaking associations can from time to time direct their members through recommendation decisions in line with their founding purposes. Such recommendations may relate to sales prices and similar matters and may carry purposes and/or effects that restrict competition. It must be determined whether the recommendations of undertaking associations result directly in binding decisions of the association. Indeed, binding undertaking association decisions restrict competition

Guaranteed Sales Base

The core sales of undertakings refer to the sales they can make without feeling the competitive pressure of their rivals, as a result of factors such as brand image, product/service quality, accessibility and after-sales service quality. An undertaking having core sales means that a certain (inelastic) part of total demand in the market depends on that undertaking. Undertakings with market power are generally accepted to have core sales

Waste Disposal

It is the regular and continuous storage of solid, liquid and gaseous waste, its elimination by incineration and its conversion into products and energy for recovery. An Avan Project is a draft project prepared before the implementation project, giving an idea about the main project.

Advance Advance Payment

It is a lending transaction against commercial paper and documents taken as collateral and government bonds and bonds registered on the exchange.

Example: The customer paid an advance of 10.000 before the equipment was delivered.(The customer paid a 10.000 advance before delivery of the equipment.)

English definition: a payment made by a customer ahead of delivery of the goods or services, which creates an obligation on the seller.

Average Total Cost

They are costs obtained by dividing total costs, including variable and fixed costs, by the total production volume.

In-kind income income in kind

They are non-monetary benefits provided to households by the state or employers in the form of goods and services.

In-kind Contribution

A contribution made in a project by the project owner and/or its partners in the form of land, goods, materials, labor, machinery and equipment to meet the project's specific needs. B2B: an abbreviation of Business to Business, this term refers to any commercial activity that businesses carry out directly with one another. B2C: an abbreviation of Business to Consumer, this term refers to any commercial activity that businesses carry out directly with consumers

Goods competing for the same resource goods competing for the same resource

They are products obtained from a common source that can be used a limited number of times.

Decomposition STRIP - Separate Trading of Registered Interest and Principal of Securities

Refers to the stripping of a coupon-bearing security so that its principal and coupons can be traded separately. The aim is, in particular, to broaden the scope of use of long-term coupon-bearing issues from the investor's perspective and to increase demand for such issues. For this purpose, the Undersecretariat of Treasury can strip the Government Domestic Debt Securities it has auctioned and the Lease Certificates it has sold directly into their principals and coupons.

Bear Market Bear Market

It refers to markets in which prices are falling.

Descending price auction descending-price auction

Or the Dutch auction, a type of auction in which participants submit open bids and the price is progressively reduced from a high starting level until a buyer emerges.

Law of diminishing returns law of diminishing returns

It is the basic economics concept stating that when at least one factor of production is fixed, the contribution of an increase in another variable factor to production will gradually decline.

Decreasing-cost industry cost-reducing industry

It is an industry in which firms' average total cost decreases as the market grows.

Law of diminishing marginal utility the law of diminishing marginal utility

It is the basic economics concept stating that the additional benefit obtained from a product diminishes as more of it is consumed within a given period.

Open economy open economy

It is an economy that trades with the rest of the world to a significant extent and establishes relations.

Open inflation targeting explicit inflation targeting

It is the monetary policy regime in which the central bank determines according to explicit rules how it will use monetary policy instruments to achieve the inflation it targets.

Open expenditures explicit cost

They are costs that are directly incurred during economic activity and involve a definite payment.

Open letter Open Letter

A written text in which, pursuant to Article 42 of the CBRT Law, if inflation targets are not achieved or the possibility of not achieving them arises, the reasons for this situation and the measures to be taken are notified in writing to the Government and simultaneously announced to the public. The open letter is written, under the principles of transparency and accountability, when inflation occurs at a point outside the symmetric uncertainty band defined around the point target.

Open market operation open market operation

Or abbreviated as API (OMO), the central bank's purchase or sale of securities from financial institutions for monetary policy purposes.

Open Market Operations - OMO Open Market Operations – OMO

Within the framework of the targets of monetary policy, the CBRT's transactions — in exchange for Turkish lira — of outright purchase and sale of securities, sale with a repurchase commitment (repo), purchase with a resale commitment (reverse repo), and the taking and giving of Turkish lira deposits, with the aim of effectively regulating the money supply and the liquidity of the economy. Through open market operations, central banks ensure that short-term interest rates form around the policy rate(s) and regulate liquidity in the financial markets.

Open position Short Position

It is the portion of assets held through an instrument such as foreign exchange, gold or securities that is not offset by liabilities of the same kind. For example, if 5 million US dollars of assets are held against a liability of 10 million US dollars, the 5 million US dollars in between represent the open US dollar position.

Short-sale transaction Short Selling

It is the transaction of selling an asset that is not held in the investor's portfolio for various reasons, starting with the expectation of a price decline, and then buying that asset from the market and delivering it when the time for delivery arrives.

Network effect network effect

It is the fact that the value of a product increases as more people use it.

Extreme wealth excessive wealth

It is having wealth a thousand times or more than the average in a given society, although it has no precise definition.

Overconfidence Overconfidence bias

It is the tendency of the investor to overestimate their forecasting ability and the quality of their information; it shows itself in excessive trading, insufficient diversification and high risk.

B

Bank panic bank run

Or bank run, the situation in which a large number of depositors wish to withdraw their deposits from a bank at the same time.

Bank money bank money

These are payment instruments kept under an account record and monitored by the banking system.

Banking system banking system

It is a network of institutions made up of banks operating in a country and having a regular structure.

Interbank interest rate interbank offered rate

Or abbreviated as IBOR, the interest rate at which banks lend to one another for their unsecured overnight borrowings in the money market.

Interbank Card Center - BKM Interbank Card Centre – ICC

A payment system with legal personality, established in 1990 through the partnership of 13 public and private Turkish banks to provide the clearing and settlement of debit and credit cards. Through BKM, banks carry out the clearing and settlement of the credit card and debit card payments they make on one another's behalf. BKM operates on a netting basis, and the settlement of the debts arising from netting takes place through the accounts held at the Istanbul Branch of the CBRT.

Interbank Money Market Interbank Money Market

It is the market in which banks, operating under the TCMB, may carry out TL deposit buying and selling transactions with one another and with the TCMB, and may access the Late Liquidity Window Facility and intraday limit transactions solely in their capacity as the TCMB.

Interbank TL Transfer System Interbank Payment System

As a component of the EFT System owned and operated by the CBRT, the system that provides the real-time electronic settlement of the payments banks make among themselves and of the banking payments related to the correspondent services they provide to financial institutions abroad.

To run to a bank bank run

Or bank panic, the situation in which a large number of depositors wish to withdraw their deposits from a bank at the same time.

Bank-specific Countercyclical Capital Buffer Countercyclical Capital Buffer

It refers to the additional core capital amount that banks are expected to hold in order to prevent equity from falling below the regulatory capital adequacy requirements if credit expansion in the financial sector reaches levels that could raise the overall level of risk.

Banknote Banknote

They are non-interest-bearing instruments whose payment of the amount stated on them is guaranteed by the issuing institution. They are legal tender. The TCMB is the only institution authorised to issue Turkish lira banknotes.

Exchange rate crawling in the band crawling peg with a strap

It is a foreign exchange rate regime in which exchange rates are allowed to move within a certain band around the central parity and in which the central parity is also changed from time to time.

Barriers to Expansion

Barriers to expansion are the obstacles in front of the firms already on the market in increasing their market shares.

Basel Committee on Banking Supervision Basel Committee on Banking Supervision – BCBS

An international organization operating under the umbrella of the Bank for International Settlements (BIS) based in Basel, Switzerland, which prepares prudential regulatory frameworks for the banking sector on a global scale. The Committee also provides cooperation on the supervision of banks. With the aim of consolidating global financial stability, the Committee issues regulatory frameworks, particularly in the fields of capital and liquidity, and monitors the implementation of these regulations in countries. The Committee submits its fundamental decisions and work programme to the Group of Central Bank Governors and Heads of Supervision (GHOS) for approval.

Simple interest rate Simple Interest Rate

It is the interest rate at which an investment earns only on its principal over the investment period.

Simple money multiplier simple money multiplier

It is the money multiplier in a situation where banks hold no excess reserves and the non-bank sector holds no cash.

Dominant strategy dominant strategy

In game theory, it is the strategy that yields the highest payoff to a player whatever the strategies of the other players are.

Too Big to Fail Financial Institutions Too Big to Fail

Financial institutions that carry the potential of creating systemic risk and can impose large costs on the economy should they become unable to continue their operations, owing to their size and complexity, their connections with other institutions in the financial system, and the difficulty for other financial institutions to provide their significant activities at short notice.

Base Effect Base Effect

Refers to the effect carried over to the period in which the change is calculated, when the reference period for the change contains a realization significantly lower or higher than normal. For example, in calculating the annual inflation rate for November 2014, the percentage change of the November 2014 consumer index relative to the November 2013 consumer index is taken. If the consumer index rose in November 2013 at a rate far below the normal average, a base effect raising the annual inflation in November 2014 will be observed.

Basis Point Basis Point

It is a unit of measurement expressing a change in interest rates. It corresponds to the fourth decimal place (0,0001). For example, if the interest rate rises from 5.25% to 6.75%, that is a rise of 150 basis points. In other words, a change of 100 basis points corresponds to a change of 1%.

Example: The interest rate increased by 25 basis points.( The interest rate rose by 25 basis points.

English definition: One hundredth of a percentage point.

Break-even point break-even point

It is called the situation in which total income equals total expenses so that economic profit is zero.

Free-rider problem free-rider problem

It is the situation in which some economic units use more of a good or service than their share or do not pay their share of the cost.

Expected value Expected Value

Or abbreviated as EV, the operation of taking the probability-weighted average.

Example: The expected value of the consideration was calculated from possible outcomes.(The expected value of the provision was calculated from possible outcomes.)

English definition: A probability-weighted average of possible outcomes.

Expected shortfall Expected Shortfall / CVaR

It is the average loss that arises when the VaR threshold is exceeded; it measures tail risk better than VaR.

Expected benefit Expected Utility

Or abbreviated as EU, the operation of taking the probability-weighted average of the benefit.

Expectation Management Managing Expectations

Within the monetary policy framework, expectation management is the set of strategies the Central Bank of the Republic of Türkiye (TCMB) applies to ensure that the medium- and long-term inflation expectations of economic units take shape in line with the inflation targets.

Uncertainty Uncertainty

It is the state of not knowing exactly how a future event will turn out.

Benchmarking

It is the examination by a company of the business techniques of other companies with high competitive strength in order to raise its own competitive strength, the comparison of those techniques with its own, and the application in its own business of the knowledge obtained from that comparison.

Beta coefficient Beta Coefficient

It is the sensitivity of the asset's return to the market return; β = 1 means moving with the market, while β > 1 means volatility above the market.

Human capital Human capital

; It is the accumulation related to humans such as education, skills, experience, and entrepreneurship used in production.

Bid-rigging, Collusive Bidding

( (Tendering) Cartels formed by enterprises to raise prices, share quantities or regions, or reduce the quality of goods or services for buyers who want to obtain products or services through a tender process. Cartel members operate the cartel through methods such as determining the order of winning, sharing tenders among themselves, and giving shares to losers by the artificially winning bidder.

Big Data

Big data refers to large-volume data sets that cannot be analyzed by traditional information methods and require new solutions to be processed, that can generally be produced in large quantities within a short time, that are collected from different sources, and that can contain structured or unstructured data. With the valuable insights it provides, big data gives an undertaking the opportunity to better analyze consumer preferences and behaviors, thereby enabling innovative and high-quality

Compound interest Compound Interest

It is the interest applied on the total amount obtained after the interest earned on the principal held at the bank by an investor has been added to the principal.

Effective interest rate Compound Interest Rate

It is the interest showing the return obtained when the interest earned by an investment over the investment period is also reinvested. In other words, it is interest earning interest.

One-Week Repo Interest Rate One-Week Repo Rate

In May 2010, the CBRT introduced the one-week repo rate as a new liquidity instrument.

One-week repo auction interest rate one-week repo auction rate

It is the interest rate applied to the transaction in which banks sell the securities they hold to the TCMB for one week and buy them back at maturity.

Individual supply Individual Supply

Or abbreviated as s, the quantity of the product that a single seller in the market wants to sell at different prices in a given period.

Individual demand Individual Demand

Or abbreviated as d, the quantity of the product that a single buyer in the market wants to buy at different prices in a given period.

Accrued interest Accrued Interest

The interest accumulated on an investment within the investment period, up to the payment date. For example, to purchase a government bond issued at 100 TL with a periodic 4% coupon payment every 6 months (182 days), the price to be paid 3 months later (91 days) is 102 TL, under the assumption that the market interest rate has not changed.

Unit elastic relationship Unit Elastic Relationship

It is a one-percent response of one variable resulting from a one-percent increase in another variable.

Unit labour cost Unit Labour Cost

Refers to the average labor expenditures required to produce one unit of output. It is calculated by dividing total wage payments by production. It can also be expressed as the ratio of hourly wages to labor productivity. An increase in hourly wages raises unit labor cost, while an increase in productivity lowers it. From the firm's perspective, an increase in unit labor cost is a factor that reduces profitability and weakens competitiveness.

First-degree price discrimination first-degree price discrimination

Or perfect price discrimination, the practice of selling a given product to each buyer at the highest price that buyer is willing to pay for it.

First-price sealed-bid auction First-Price Sealed-Bid Auction

It is a type of auction in which participants submit sealed bids and the highest bidder wins and pays the price they bid.

Primary Income Account Primary Income Account

It shows the income earned and the amounts paid in return for providing labour, a financial or a natural resource, and includes the wages of the employed as well as investment income and expenses on direct investments, portfolio investments and other investments.

Primary Market Primary Market

The market where first-time issuances take place. The first launch (sale) of an investment instrument, for example a security, is a primary market transaction. Likewise, the auction-based sale of government domestic debt securities by the Ministry of Treasury and Finance of the Republic of Türkiye is also a primary market transaction. Subsequent purchases and sales of these securities among investors take place in markets known as the secondary market.

Merger merger

It is the merger of two companies of their own accord into a single new company.

Goods offered together goods supplied jointly

It is each of the multiple products obtained as output at the end of the same production process.

Biomass

It is a renewable energy source defined as the mass of plant or animal organisms that they possess at a given time.

Block Exemption

Used to describe the regulations granting exemption to certain types of agreements as a group. In Turkish competition law, these regulations are made through block exemption communiqués. Agreements within the scope of a block exemption are exempt from the prohibitions on restrictive agreements, provided they satisfy the conditions relating to the block exemption. Such agreements are automatically valid and enforceable.

Paradox of plenty the paradox of abundance

Or the resource curse; the phenomenon whereby natural resources such as oil bring conflict, war, poverty and deprivation in some countries instead of economic growth.

Bond Bill

Securities with a maturity of less than one year, for which the issuing institution undertakes to pay a certain amount at the maturity it foresees. Issues with a maturity of less than one year made by the Ministry of Treasury and Finance of the Republic of Türkiye trade under the name Treasury Bills (Hazine Bonosu). The English equivalent 'Bond' refers to instruments with maturities longer than 10 years (maximum 30 years) for US Treasury issues. Similarly, 'Note' is used for issues of 1-10 years, and 'Bill' for issues of one year or shorter.

Debt-to-equity ratio Debt-to-equity ratio

It is the ratio of total liabilities to equity; it indicates the level of financial leverage. The higher the ratio, the greater the financial risk.

Example: The entity's debt-to-equity ratio remained within the covenant limits agreed with its lender.(The entity's debt-to-equity ratio remained within the covenant limits agreed with its lender.)

English definition: a financial ratio comparing total liabilities with shareholders' equity, showing financial leverage and risk.

Boycott

Agreements not to do business with certain individuals or undertakings are called boycotts, and such behavior can be prohibited, particularly when the boycotting undertakings hold market power. Group boycotts of this kind may, for example, be used in pursuit of a price-fixing agreement. Accordingly, some rivals may decide not to do business with the others unless certain conditions are met, and this will bring about an increase in price levels. Not doing business with others

Bull Market Bull Market

It refers to a market in which prices are rising.

Broker Broker

It is the name given to intermediaries who mediate between purchase and sale transactions for a certain commission, carrying out transactions on their own account but on behalf of the person or institution they represent.

Brownfield Investment

Brownfield investments are a type of foreign direct investment taking the form of buying or leasing a facility or firm that is already active. Budget: an estimation table showing the revenues to be obtained for a certain period and the expenditures planned to be made. C2C: a commerce (e-commerce) model carried out over the internet from consumer to consumer, whose English is 'Consumer to Consumer'.

Gross profit margin Gross profit margin

It is gross profit (revenue − cost of goods sold) as a percentage of revenue; it measures cost discipline in production.

Crisis or depression buhran

It is a severe and prolonged recession that, although there is no precise definition, affects more than one country and in which total output falls by more than 10 percent.
