Game theory game theory

It is the set of principles that explains how economic agents acting in their own interest behave in competition or cooperation with each other.

Gamma Gamma

It is the rate at which delta changes with respect to the underlying price (Γ = N′(d1)/(S·σ·√T)); it shows how frequently the hedge needs to be renewed.

GDP at current prices GDP at current prices

Or nominal GDP, the national income method that measures the value of goods and services produced in a given period at the prices of the same period.

GDP deflator GDP deflator

It is a price index based on the basket of goods covering all goods and services included in GDP.

GDP in fixed prices GDP at constant prices

Or real GDP, the national income method that measures the value of goods and services produced in a given period at the prices of a fixed base year.

General Budget General Budget

; It is the budget of public institutions such as the Turkish Grand National Assembly, Presidency, Prime Ministry, Constitutional Court, Court of Cassation, Council of State, Court of Accounts, and various ministries that constitute the legal personality of the state.

General Government Balance General Government Balance

It consists of the balances of the central government budget, local administrations, social security institutions, general health insurance, funds, revolving funds and the unemployment insurance fund. This definition is used in international comparisons.

General Incentive Applications

Natural Person: the term legally defining the period of a person from the womb until death, on the conditions of being born alive and remaining alive.

General price level General price level

Or abbreviated as P, the conceptual quantity expressing the prices of all goods and services in the economy in aggregate.

Gentlemen's Agreements

Such arrangements are defined as tacit collusion shaped by the level of commitment the participating parties feel, and are examined under the "agreement, concerted action and/or decisions" framework in Article 4 of the Competition Law.

Geographical Indication

These are signs indicating a product that is identified with a region, area, locality or country of origin by its distinctive qualities, reputation or other characteristics.

Giffen good Giffen good

It is a necessity good for which the quantity demanded increases, rather than decreases, as its price rises.

Gini coefficient Gini coefficient

goods competing for the same resource goods competing for the same resource

goods in joint supply goods supplied jointly

government consumption expenditures government consumption expenditure

Great Depression The Great Depression

Green GDP Green GDP

green-field investment greenfield investment

gross domestic happiness Gross Domestic Happiness

Gross domestic product Gross Domestic Product

Or abbreviated as GDP (GSYH), the market value of all final goods and services produced using the resources of a country in a given period.

Gross Domestic Product - GDP Gross Domestic Product – GDP

gross fixed capital formation Gross Fixed Capital Formation

Or abbreviated as GFCF (GSSSO), the total of all fixed capital expenditure made by resident economic units domestically.

gross investment expenditures gross investment expenditure

Or abbreviated as I, the main expenditure heading covering the fixed capital and stock purchases of domestic economic units.

Gross national product Gross National Product

Or abbreviated as GNP (GSMH), the market value of all final goods and services produced using resources belonging to the nationals of a country in a given period.

Gross profit margin Gross profit margin

It is gross profit (revenue − cost of goods sold) as a percentage of revenue; it measures cost discipline in production.

gross value added Gross Value Added

Or abbreviated as GVA (GSKD), the total value added created in the economy or in a given sector of the economy in a period.

Guarantee Cooperative

They are cooperatives obliged to guarantee to the bank against loans so that small businesses, tradespeople and artisans can obtain credit from a financial institution. A guarantor is the person who undertakes to pay the debt on behalf of another when that person does not pay it.

Guaranteed Sales Base

The core sales of undertakings refer to the sales they can make without feeling the competitive pressure of their rivals, as a result of factors such as brand image, product/service quality, accessibility and after-sales service quality. An undertaking having core sales means that a certain (inelastic) part of total demand in the market depends on that undertaking. Undertakings with market power are generally accepted to have core sales

Guided Project Support

It is the type of support granted to special-purpose model projects whose subject and conditions are determined under the guidance and direction of the relevant Development Agency, in line with the priorities foreseen in the regional plan, with a view to providing direct support without applying the call-for-proposals method.

Gun Jumping

It is a concentration transaction carried out without notification or permission, including agreements, information exchange and unlawful coordination that restrict competition between the parties beforehand.

With the aim of reducing the risks that may be encountered to a certain value, the investor can, by taking a long position with the help of futures contracts, purchase a futures contract with the expectation that prices will rise in the future, or, by taking a short position, sell a futures contract with the expectation that prices will fall in the future.

Herfindahl-Hirschman Index Herfindahl-Hirschman Index

Returns to scale can be defined as the effect on efficiency of the change occurring in the scale of production. Increasing returns to scale is the situation in which the undertaking's average cost falls as the quantity produced rises. There are two sources of this fall. The first is internal, stemming from the increase in the undertaking's level of production. The second is external; accordingly, the increase in the quantity of production in the industry affects the prices in the market in a way that will cause the undertaking to change its production decision

Inflation Compensation Inflation Compensation

It is equal to the sum of the inflation expectation and the inflation risk premium demanded by investors in order not to be exposed to real losses.

Inflation Expectations Inflation Expectations

It refers to economic agents' expectations about inflation that has not yet materialised but will occur in the future. To measure inflation expectations, indices derived from various market instruments or surveys such as the Expectations Survey published by the CBRT may be used.

Inflation Forecast Inflation Forecast

These are medium-term inflation forecasts updated every three months by the TCMB within the framework of existing data, models and analyses. While the inflation target announced for a given period is fixed, the inflation forecast is regularly reviewed in the light of new information.

Inflation Indexed Securities Inflation-Indexed Securities

Bonds whose principal and interest payments are linked to a certain price index and which therefore do not suffer a real loss of value. Because the real return of these bonds does not fluctuate despite changes in inflation, offering investors a predictable net return, these bonds are preferred by risk-averse investors.

Inflation Risk Premium Inflation Risk Premium

Refers to the risk premium added to the real interest rate to compensate for the loss that would occur if inflation materialized above the expected level. In other words, the nominal interest rate contains the real interest rate, inflation expectations and the inflation risk premium. This risk premium arises in countries where inflation fluctuations and uncertainties are present.

Inflation Target Inflation Target

It is the annual CPI growth rate set by the TCMB and the Government for three-year periods. The inflation target is defined as the annual inflation in December of each year.

Inflation Targeting Inflation Targeting

Central banks' targeting, generally together with governments, of a specific inflation figure as the aim of monetary policy. In such a practice, it is envisaged that the targeted inflation, to the extent it can affect expectations, will serve as the nominal anchor. Its difference from other policies is the clear announcement of the inflation target to the public and the taking of direct responsibility in this matter. In practice, as the implementing institution, central banks acquire instrument independence directed toward the aim; in other words, central banks can use exchange rate and interest rate policies as they wish with the aim of bringing inflation under control. Such practices can also emerge as a necessity as a result of all the magnitudes in an economy losing their nominal anchor property. For the success of such a policy, elements such as a strong and healthy fiscal structure, an observable and stable relationship between inflation and monetary policy instruments, credibility, independence, accountability and transparency are counted as prerequisites.

Inflation Tax Inflation Tax

When money is considered a liability for the institution issuing it into circulation and an asset for the institutions and individuals demanding and holding it, inflation reduces the issuing institution's liability and the holding institution's asset in real terms. In this sense, inflation carries the property of a tax for the earner, and hence, like a tax, causes a transfer of purchasing power.
