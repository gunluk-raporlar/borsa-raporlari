Terms & Definitions

N

NAIRU or the non-accelerating inflation rate of unemployment non-accelerating inflation rate of unemployment

It is the unemployment rate that keeps the labour market in the country at long-run equilibrium and therefore does not lead to an increase in inflation.

Freight Equalization System (Freight

Equalization System): This method basically resembles a multi-base-point system; the only difference is that all production centres are accepted as base points. Thus, sellers calculate the delivery price as if a sale were made to the closest competitor of the buyer.

Nash equilibriumNash equilibrium

In game theory, it is the state reached as a result of each player choosing their best strategy according to their expectations about the strategies of the other players.

Master Plan

The term for the master plan reports showing all the particulars of lands with determined cadastral status on plans at a scale of 1/2000 or 1/5000, prepared in a manner consistent with the environment or regional order plans. Master plans are created to show the uses of lands, the zone types, the population structure and the transportation systems. All these points are stated in a detailed report. The master plans show what form a settlement area will take in the future.

Negative externality negative externality

It is the negative impact that an economic activity has on people who are not party to that activity.

Negative output gap negative output gap

Or recessionary gap, the situation in which real GDP in the short term is below potential GDP and the economy is therefore considered to be in a recession.

Net present value Net Present Value

It is the value reached by discounting to present date the return an investment provides over the investment period at the market interest rate or its own interest rate.

Net factor income net factor income

It is the difference between the income that domestic factors of production earn abroad and the income that foreign factors of production earn domestically.

Net Errors and OmissionsNet Errors and Omissions

Because the balance of payments is an accounting system in which every transaction is recorded with two separate signs (a credit and a debit entry), it is essential that, while each transaction of the balance of payments is recorded under the item relating to its content, its counterpart entry takes place in another item. In other words, every transaction being recorded with equal-value credit and debit entries requires that the total of the 'Current Accounts' and the 'Capital Account' always equal the 'Financial Account' item. However, the obtaining of the data from different sources creates differences in valuation, measurement and recording time; the differences formed as a result are reflected as a 'residual' in the Net Errors and Omissions item. This item is obtained by subtracting the current account and the capital account from the financial account.

Net exportsnet exports

Or abbreviated as NX, the main expenditure heading showing the difference between the goods that domestic economic units export to the rest of the world and the goods they import from the rest of the world.

Net working capital Net Working Capital

It is the excess of short-term assets over short-term liabilities (Current Assets − Current Liabilities); it shows the liquidity buffer.

Net Stable Funding Ratio Net Stable Funding Ratio – NSFR

The ratio of the current steady funding amount to the required steady funding amount. The current steady funding amount fundamentally consists of capital, liabilities with an effective maturity of more than one year, and savings and SME deposits. Some other liabilities are included in steady funding after being multiplied by certain coefficients. The required steady funding amount is obtained by classifying assets, according to their liquidity, from the most liquid to the least liquid and multiplying them by certain coefficients.

Net profit margin Net profit margin

It is the ratio of net profit to sales (Net profit / Sales) and the first component of DuPont; it shows how much net profit is left from every 1 lira of sales.

Net national product net national product

Or abbreviated as NNP (NMH), the difference between all final goods and services produced by the nationals of a country in a given period and the capital consumed in the course of that production.

Net Sales Revenue

Refers to the amount found as a result of deducting sales discounts and returns and other rebates from an enterprise's gross sales. NUTS: The Classification of Territorial Units for Statistics (İBBS), developed since the 1970s by the Statistical Office of the European Communities (Eurostat) in order to ensure that regional statistics in the European Union are produced according to a single spatial classification

Net taxesnet taxes

It is the difference between the taxes paid by households and firms to the state and the transfer payments made by the state to households and firms.

Netting Netting

It is the offsetting of the receivables and payables arising from the transfer orders sent and received by a participant of a payment or securities settlement system against each other and their conversion into a single receivable or payable.

Network Effects

Network externality is the external benefit that each consumer joining a network provides for the other customers within the network. As the number of users increases, a certain product or service becomes more valuable in the user's eyes. This situation is also called a network externality.

Quantitative easing Quantitative Easing

An unconventional monetary policy approach, meaning central banks' increasing the money supply by purchasing financial assets from commercial banks or other private institutions. After the global financial crisis, the central banks of the United States in particular, and of England, Europe and Japan, applied quantitative expansion policies of different magnitudes in order to support the financial sector, increase private consumption expenditures and prevent inflation from falling below the target.

Nihai Kredi Mercii Lender of Last Resort

A concept expressing that, because of their capacity as monetary authority and their obligations to ensure financial stability, central banks are the final institution of application for participants, in the event that the funding shortages arising in the system cannot be met from the market, the participants applying to the central bank and benefiting from the facilities arranged under this name.

Nihai mal final good

These are goods purchased by the end user.

Final domestic demand Final Domestic Demand

It is the total of the consumption and investment expenditures of the private and public sectors.

Point elasticity point elasticity

It is an elasticity calculation method used to find the elasticity at a single point on a curve or line.

Nominal value Par Value, Face Value

It is the value printed on an asset. For example, the nominal value of each domestic government bond (DIPS) issued by the Turkish Treasury and Finance Ministry is the 100 TL actually printed on it. Likewise, the nominal value of a 200 TL banknote is the 200 printed on it.

Nominal exchange rate nominal exchange rate

Or abbreviated as E, the relative value of one currency in terms of another currency.

Nominal effective exchange rate Nominal Effective Exchange Rate

It refers to the average obtained using an appropriate weighting method of the foreign exchange rates selected for a specific purpose.

Nominal faiz Nominal interest rate

It is the interest rate stated in the contract that is not adjusted for inflation; it does not show an increase in real purchasing power.

Nominal interest rate nominal interest rate

Or abbreviated as i, the percentage that borrowers pay to lenders without making an adjustment for the price changes arising from inflation.

Nominal GSYH nominal GDP

Or GDP in current prices, the national income method that measures the value of goods and services produced in a given period at the prices of the same period.

Nominal rigidity nominal rigidity

; The phenomenon of sticky fees and prices observed in the short term due to various contracts, regulations, or other costs.

Normal mal standard goods

They are products whose demand in the market rises when the average income level rises.

Normative analysis normative analysis

It is an analytical resolution that examines the behaviour and systems of the employed from a subjective standpoint, focusing on their consequences and incorporating value judgements.

Notification

It is the submission of the necessary information to the Competition Authority in applications such as notification, merger or acquisition clearance, a negative clearance certificate or exemption.

O

Okun's law Okun’s Law

It is a resolution stating that for every one percent increase in the unemployment rate, real GDP falls by about two percent.

Probability probability

Or abbreviated as Pr, the relative frequency with which the outcome of a random event is observed.

Example: A provision is recognized when an outflow of resources is probable.( A provision is recognised when an outflow is probable.)

English definition: More likely than not to occur.

Oligopol oligopoly

It is a market structure in which firms with large market shares act interdependently in their price-output choices.

Confirmation bias Confirmation bias

It is seeking out information that supports one's own view and ignoring contradicting evidence; it leads to one-sided evidence gathering in analysis.

One-Stop Shopping

It refers to the ability to meet the needs of customers in different areas from a single point of service (for example hypermarkets).

Operational target operating target

It is the financial indicator that the central bank closely monitors in order to keep its intermediate target under control.

Option Option

Contracts granting the right to buy or sell a certain good, asset or financial indicator at a predetermined maturity and price. While the holder of the contract has the right to buy (call option) or sell (put option) the instrument underlying the contract, it has no obligation whatsoever arising from the contract. The party selling (writing) the contract, on the other hand, is obliged to fulfill the terms of the agreement if the party holding the contract wants, at maturity, to buy or sell the instrument underlying the contract within the contract's conditions. The parties selling (writing) the options obtain premium income in return for this transaction. In markets where uncertainty (volatility) is high, the premium demands for the contract can reach very high dimensions.

The holder of the call option may purchase the shares at the agreed exercise price.)(The holder of the call option may purchase the shares at the agreed exercise price.)

English definition: a contract granting its holder the right, though not the obligation, to purchase or dispose of an asset at a set price.

Optimum Scale

It is the scale that enables production and sales at the lowest possible cost.

Ordinal utility ordinal utility

; the idea that the utility consumers derive from goods and services can be measured not numerically but through comparison and ranking.

Organic Farming

A production system directed at re-establishing the balance in nature lost as a result of mistaken practices, containing production systems friendly to humans and the environment by controlling diseases and pests with biological control ensuring continuity in the soil's fertility, prohibiting the use of synthetic chemical fertilizers and pesticides, recommending organic and green fertilization, crop rotation and soil conservation, every stage of which, from production to consumption, is under control, and the product obtained is documented with a certificate

Organized Industrial Zone

( TDİOSB) A region for the production of goods and services that includes plant and animal production forming agricultural-based industrial inputs and industrial facilities for the processing of these, aimed at integrating the agricultural and industrial sectors.

Organized derivatives markets Derivatives Markets

The markets operating within the exchange in which standardized derivative transaction contracts are bought and sold forward, and the settlements carried out are guaranteed by an institution. The derivative market transactions — futures, forwards, options and swaps — can be used by market players with the aim of hedging against risk or making profit.

Midpoint formula midpoint formula

It is a calculation method that divides the net change by the average change in order to find the average percentage change in the economy.

Common value auction common value auction

It is the auction environment in which each participant values the object up for auction in dependence on the others and this value is decisive for everyone.

Common good shared resource

These are goods that are not excludable in consumption but create rivalry.

Tragedy of the commons tragedy of the commons

It is an economic concept describing the disappearance of or damage to shared resources used by everyone in society as a result of individual interest and greed.

Average variable cost average variable cost

Or abbreviated as AVC, the variable cost incurred per unit of product.

Average revenue average revenue

Or abbreviated as AR, the revenue obtained per unit of product.

Average expenditure average expenditure

Or abbreviated as AE, the expenditure per unit of product.

Average fixed cost average fixed cost

Or abbreviated as AFC, the fixed cost incurred per unit of product.

Average total cost average total cost

Or abbreviated as ATC, the total cost incurred per unit of product.

Self-Financing Bail-in

It is the practice under which losses arising during resolution are borne jointly by the equity holders of the financial institutions as well as by those who extend credit to these institutions.

Automatic Stabilizers Automatic Stabilisers

They are fiscal elements that operate automatically, quickly and effectively in establishing and maintaining economic equilibrium. Examples include progressive taxes and unemployment insurance payments.

Automatic stabilizer automatic stabiliser

They are elements of the public budget that come into operation automatically along with business cycles and have a dampening effect on fluctuations in the economy.

Autonomous variable independent variable

They are exogenous effects that do not depend on other variables in a mathematical model.

Autonomous money demand autonomous money demand

; It is the fixed part of money demand that is not dependent on interest rate, income or general price level.

Autonomous consumption autonomous consumption

It is the fixed part of private consumption expenditure that is not income-dependent.

Autonomous investment autonomous investment

It is the fixed portion of planned investments that is independent of the return or interest rate.

Session Permission

It is another name for the residence permit that foreigners who have decided to settle in Turkey for various reasons are required to obtain.

Volatility Volatility

It expresses the variability of the price of an asset. It is usually measured by standard deviation. High volatility is an indicator of increasing uncertainty.

Game theory game theory

It is the set of principles that explains how economic agents acting in their own interest behave in competition or cooperation with each other.

Player player

In game theory, they are the economic units whose behaviour is to be explained.

Money money

It is any object of value generally accepted as payment in exchange for goods and services.

Money supply curve money supply curve

A graphical tool showing the relationship between the nominal interest rate and the quantity of money in the economy, other things being equal.

Money supply money supply

Or abbreviated as MS, the amount of money created jointly by the central bank, commercial banks and the non-banking sector in a given period.

Near money quasi-money

They are securities that are a close substitute for money held for trading purposes.

Currency Board currency board

It is the exchange rate regime that allows money to be printed only up to the amount of foreign exchange held.

Money market Money Market

It is the market in which highly liquid financial instruments with a short-term maturity (90 days or less for international markets) are traded.

Money market equilibrium money market equilibrium

It is the situation in a given period in which the aggregate demand for money in the market equals the aggregate supply of money.

Money market fund money market fund

They are investment funds consisting of short-term and low-risk securities.

Monetary policy Monetary Policy

Refers to the decisions taken with the aim of influencing the availability and cost of money in order to reach aims such as economic growth, increasing employment and price stability. The institutions responsible for its implementation are central banks. In the CBRT Law, it is stated that the fundamental aim of the CBRT is to ensure price stability.

Monetary Policy Committee - PPK Monetary Policy Committee – MPC

The board formed within the CBRT, charged and authorized with determining the monetary policy principles and strategies with the aim of ensuring price stability, and, within the framework of these strategies, determining the inflation target together with the government, taking the measures necessary to preserve the internal and external value of the Turkish lira, and determining, again together with the government, the exchange rate regime directed at establishing its parity against foreign currencies and gold. The Monetary Policy Committee, under the chairmanship of the Governor, consists of the Deputy Governors, a member to be elected by the Bank Council from among its members, and a member to be appointed by joint decision upon the Governor's proposal. The Undersecretary of Treasury or the Deputy Undersecretary he designates may attend the meetings without voting rights.

Money demand demand for money

Or abbreviated as MD, the amount of money that all units in the economy want to hold depending on the interest rate in a given period.

Money demand curve money demand curve

A graphical tool showing the relationship between the nominal interest rate and the amount of money that economic agents wish to hold, other things being equal.

Currency carry trade currency carry trade

Or abbreviated as carry trade, the transaction of borrowing in a low-interest-rate currency and investing in a high-interest-rate currency in order to make a profit.

Money and Banking Statistics Money and Banking Statistics

It is a stock data showing the money, deposit and other monetary liabilities of the central bank and the banks, the loans extended by these institutions and their positions with respect to abroad. Under the residency principle, transactions of the banks' foreign organisations are not included.

Money multiplier money multiplier

Or abbreviated as θ (read as theta), the coefficient showing how much money is created from one unit of reserves in the banking system.

Velocity of Money Velocity of Money

It shows how many units of goods or services one unit of money can change hands for. It is expressed as GDP / Money Supply.

Quantity theory of money quantity theory of money

It is the set of theories arguing that there is a direct and proportional relationship between the money supply and the general price level.

Neutrality of money the neutrality of money

It is the doctrine holding that the money supply determines only nominal magnitudes in the economy and will not affect real values.

Monetary transmission mechanism Monetary Transmission Mechanism

Shows through which channels and to what extent monetary variables affect aggregate demand, the output gap and inflation. The monetary transmission mechanism is generally explained in three stages. The first stage determines the transmission of the changes in monetary policy implementations to variables such as interest rates, asset prices, expectations and the exchange rate. In the second stage, the said variables affect the demand for domestic and imported goods; in the third stage, aggregate demand and domestic pricing behaviors are determined, and domestic prices and import prices shape inflation. In addition, interest rates have direct effects on inflation, and exchange rates on the demand for imported goods and on import prices in local currency.

Monetary gold Monetary Gold

It refers to gold owned by the central bank and held as reserves.

Monetary aggregates Monetary Aggregates

A monetary magnitude is calculated by subtracting the monetary sector's liabilities to one another from the total of the money in circulation and the various liabilities of the monetary sector (the central bank, banks and money market funds) that are monetary or convertible into money. There are various monetary magnitude definitions according to the degree of liquidity of the assets they contain: M1: consists of the total of the money in circulation and demand deposits. M2: consists of the total of M1 and time deposits. M3: M3, the most broadly defined money supply, consists of the total of M2, the funds provided from repos, money market funds and issued securities (with maturities of up to two years).

Monetary growth rate rule monetary growth rate rule

It is the monetary policy principle that recommends the central bank increase the money supply in proportion to the growth rate of real GDP.

Monetary income money income

It is income consisting only of payments in the form of money.

Monetary targeting monetary targeting

It is the monetary policy regime in which the central bank targets a certain growth rate for monetary aggregates such as the money supply and bank reserves.

Monetary sector Monetary Sector

It can also be defined as the money-creating sector. It comprises the central bank, deposit banks, development and investment banks, participation banks and money market funds.

Monetarist economic theory monetarist economic theory

It is the school of thought that argues changes in real income in the short run stem from demand shocks linked to money supply and advocates government intervention in the economy through rules-based monetary policy.

Pareto efficiency Pareto efficiency

It is a resource allocation that improves the position of some individuals without worsening the position of any other individual.

Parity Parity

It is the value of another country's currency calculated against the currency of the country taken as the basis.

Passive job search passive job search

It is the state of awaiting a response to applications previously submitted through active job search channels.

Perfect Competition

A hypothetical market model in which, owing to the presence of a great many buyers and sellers, it is not possible for individuals to influence prices, so prices are taken as given; entries into and exits from the market are entirely free; there is the opportunity of full information about market conditions; goods are homogeneous; the rational behavior pattern directed toward profit maximization is dominant; and production and exchange can be carried out free of any limitation. It is known that such a market cannot exist in reality

Performance Indicator

They are scales that produce quantitative information about projects and enable the project objectives to be defined in a clear and measurable way.

Persons

Phillips curve Phillips curve

A graphical tool showing the relationship between unemployment and inflation rates, other things being equal.

Pigou effect Pigou effect

It is the resolution put forward by the British economist Arthur Cecil Pigou, stating that total demand will increase when the general price level falls because of the appreciation of wealth.

Pigou tax Pigouvian tax

It is a tax imposed on economic activities that create negative externalities.

Market imperfection market failure

It is the situation in which social welfare decreases as a result of a decline in the market's efficiency in production or distribution.

Market supply market supply

Or abbreviated as S, the quantity of the product that all sellers in the market want to sell at different prices in a given period.

Market equilibrium market equilibrium

It is the situation in a given period in which the amount of goods demanded in the market equals the amount of goods supplied.

Market imbalance market disequilibrium

It is a short- or long-lasting situation in which the market fails to reach its equilibrium point due to internal or external reasons.

Market value market value

It is the price of goods and services determined jointly by supply and demand in a free market.

Market power market power

It is a measure of how much a buyer or seller can influence the market price of a given product.

Market demand market demand

Market or marketplace market

It is the physical or virtual environment where buyers and sellers come together and product prices are determined in this process.

A system in which, with the aim of increasing effectiveness in primary market transactions and facilitating the functioning of secondary markets, the treasury or central banks accept as counterparty only the banks and certain other institutions it selects according to certain criteria, in transactions such as debt security issuance (auction) and foreign exchange intervention. The institutions selected under the name of market maker have obligations such as transacting uninterruptedly in the secondary markets, giving quotations, and purchasing a certain amount of an issue.

Market structure market structure

It is the concept that describes the characteristics of the economic interaction arising from the distribution of the number and size of buyers and sellers in the market.

Market concentration market concentration

It is a conceptual measure that indicates how competitive a market is by looking at the distribution of firms' market shares.

Market product basket market basket of goods

Or basket of goods, the list of goods determined in order to represent the economy as a whole or a particular sub-sector.

Market entry barrier barrier to market entry

Planned total spending planned total expenditure

Or abbreviated as AE, the market value of all final goods and services produced using the resources of a country in a given period and intended to be purchased.

Platform Economies

Platform economies are the formations enabling two (or more) different but interlinked user groups operating in a two (or multi)-sided market to interact in a way that creates value for at least one of the sides. Their most frequently observed examples are online demand-coordinating platforms (online marketplaces, general or specialized search sites) and hardware/software platforms.

Policy ineffectiveness proposition the proposition of policy ineffectiveness

It is the view that, under rational expectations, the state cannot create a lasting effect on production and employment by using monetary policy.

Policy interest rate policy interest rate

It is the interest rate that the central bank determines in line with its policy objectives and manages effectively.

Policy mix policy mix

It is the state's use of fiscal and monetary policies together in order to influence the macroeconomy.

Portfolio portfolio

Portfolio Management Company

Position

Potansiyel GSYH potential GDP

Potential labor force potential labour force

They are the people among those of working age who are not employed and not unemployed, who are seeking work but are not in a position to start work within a short period, as well as those who are not seeking work but want to work and are in a position to start work within a short period.

Potential Competitor

Where there is a small and persistent rise in prices, an undertaking that has the likelihood (and the intention) of entering the market by incurring the necessary conversion costs or by making mandatory investments is regarded as a potential competitor.

Refers to the net surplus position held in foreign currency, securities or any financial instrument. Positive externality positive externality

It is the positive impact that an economic activity has on people who are not party to that activity.

Positive analysis positive analysis

It is an analytical resolution that examines the behaviour and systems of the employed from an objective standpoint and seeks only to explain them without passing judgement.

Positive output gap positive output gap

Or expansionary gap, the situation in which real GDP in the short term is above potential GDP and the economy is therefore considered to be in an expansion phase.

A supplier's recommendation of the price at which a good will be resold is assessed under competition law depending on whether this recommendation turns into a fixed or minimum price determination. Recommended pricing is problematic under competition law from two perspectives.

Price Rigidity

Price rigidity refers to the price of a good, service or production factor remaining constant despite perceptible changes in supply and demand conditions, or changing in only one direction. Price rigidity, also called price stickiness, is a matter closely concerning competition policies. Primary Market

Special terms are used to refer to the markets where the sale of the main product and the side products that need to be purchased along with it or later than it is realized. The first product the customer buys is called the primary product, and the product needed later for that product to be usable the secondary product. The markets where the trade of the primary and secondary products takes place are, respectively, the primary/fore market and the secondary/aftermarket

Producer Surplus

The difference between the price the producer actually sells at and the price it is willing to sell at is called the producer surplus. The concept of producer surplus is dealt with within the scope of competition economics/policy because, when there is a price increase in the market, while the consumer surplus decreases, this decrease is not transferred as an equal amount of producer surplus (and the deadweight loss thereby formed) results in a fall in total social welfare.

Production joint ventures can be fully functional, or can also emerge as non-fully functional (partially functional) joint ventures undertaking only a limited part of the parent undertakings' fields of activity. The reason for partial functionality is generally that, because these ventures make use of the parent undertakings' raw material sources and technical knowledge, they cannot behave independently in the markets, and the sale of the produced products is left to the parent undertakings

Profit

In economic theory and in the accounting discipline it is possible to speak of two separate profit concepts that differ from one another. The profit concept used in accounting expresses the revenue surplus remaining in the firm's hands after it has covered the costs of all the inputs it used in production. Profit in the economic sense, on the other hand, expresses the revenue surplus formed after the opportunity cost of producing the good is also added to the total costs. In this case, a firm earning zero profit in the economic sense still earns normal

Profit Maximisation

One of the fundamental assumptions on which microeconomic theory rests is that firms realize their production quantities at the level that maximizes their profits. At this production quantity, the price level forming in the market is named 'the price at the level where profit is maximized'. In microeconomic theory this point is the price formed according to the demand function at the production level where marginal revenue equals marginal cost.
