Terms & Definitions

Ekonomi, finans ve borsa terminolojisi sözlüğünün T–Z grubu. Tanımlar TCMB, Rekabet Kurumu ve iktisat sözlükleri gibi resmî kaynaklardan derlenmiştir. Diğer harf grupları:

A–B C–D E–F G–I J–M N–P Q–S T–Z

Tacit collusion tacit collusion

It is firms or states secretly agreeing and acting in a coordinated manner so as to benefit each other.

Takasbank (company) Istanbul Settlement and Custody Bank

The central clearing institution authorized to conclude the cash and securities clearing of the transactions relating to the shares, debt instruments, foreign capital market instruments, derivatives and precious metals realized in the existing markets within Borsa Istanbul. The securities delivery and receipt commitments of the Borsa Istanbul members arising from their purchase and sale transactions, and the payment commitments against the securities subject to purchase and sale, are concluded through Takasbank on an account basis. The Takasbank Electronic Transfer System (TETS) offers intermediary institutions access to the national payment system (EFT-EMKT). As the 'National Numbering Institution', Takasbank assigns ISINs (International Securities Identification Numbers), identification numbers to international standards, to the securities issued in Türkiye.

Target Group Target Group

It is the group or community targeted for the message intended to be delivered in communication activities.

tariff tariff

They are taxes applied by the state to imported goods and services.

Tax tax

They are the payments the state legally and mandatorily collects from individuals and institutions in order to cover public expenditure.

Example: Current tax is measured at the amount expected to be paid to the tax authorities.(Current tax is measured at the amount expected to be paid to the tax authorities.)

İngilizce tanım: A compulsory charge levied by a government on income, profits or transactions.

Tax Base

The value or quantity taken as the basis in the calculation of the tax. The tax debt is calculated by applying a tax schedule to the base. Tax bases are of two kinds, value-based and quantity-based. An ad valorem base (value-based) is calculated taking the values of the economic elements as the basis, and a certain tax rate is set for this base. A specific base (quantity-based) is a base applied to tax subjects with low monetary value.

Tax Exemption

It is the transaction by which a person or group of persons who is liable to pay tax under tax laws is excluded from tax under the same or different tax laws.

Tax Multiplier tax multiplier

It is the coefficient showing how much a unit change in autonomous taxes will affect equilibrium income.

Tax pass-through tax incidence

It is the concept explaining how the tax is shared between buyers and sellers in the market.

Tax Relief Support

An investor making an investment within the scope of an investment incentive certificate containing the tax reduction support element can pay income or corporate tax at reduced rates, varying according to the region where the investment is being made, and can continue benefiting from this reduction until reaching the investment contribution amount, at rates again varying by region.

taxes on production taxes on production

They are taxes paid to the state regardless of the quantity or value of the goods and services produced.

TCMB's Weighted Average Interest Rate CBRT's Weighted Average Funding Rate

It is the amount-weighted average interest rate of the TCMB's funding rates at different maturities.

Technical Analysis Technical Analysis

The making of price forecasts for the future by analyzing past-period market movements on the basis of indicators such as price and volume. For this purpose, use is made of various chart formations and statistical methods. It rests on the assumption that the formations arising on the charts because of past price movements constitute an indicator for the future, and hence that such formations can be used as an indicator for purchases and sales. Such analyses predict, for example, that the lowest prices realized in the past — in other words, the points at which prices began to rise — are 'support points', and hence these points should be awaited (be the criterion) for purchases, and again that the highest prices realized in the past are 'resistance' points and should be taken into account for sales.

Technical Analysis of the Investment

It is a method of forecasting possible future price movements by looking at past price movements in any investment instrument traded.

Technical Support Program

It is the support programme that development agencies provide, with their own staff or through service procurement in cases where it is compulsory, to non-profit institutions and organisations carrying out institutional and capacity-building activities for work that can contribute to local and regional development.

technological knowledge technological knowledge

It is the body of knowledge about how resources are to be used in the production process.

Technopark

The organized research and business center in which universities, research institutions and industrial organizations pursue their research, development and innovation activities in the same environment, produce value-added products, carry out knowledge and technology transfer among one another, and the academic, economic and social structure is integrated.

X-Inefficiency

Y

Yield Curve Yield Curve

The graphical expression of the relationship, in any time interval, between the various maturities of an investment instrument and the returns at those maturities. The vertical axis holds return/yield, the horizontal axis the maturities. They are classified as positively sloped (normal) yield curves, negatively sloped (inverted) yield curves, flat yield curves and yield curves expressing high returns at medium maturities (bell shape). The movements of the curve over time — its steepening ('steeping': if a positively sloped curve is in question, the distant part of the curve rising and the near part falling, the slope increasing), its flattening ('flattening': in positively sloped yield curves, the distant part falling, the near part rising, the slope decreasing) and the curve moving up or down without changing its shape ('parallel shift') — are so named. An increase in the curve's slope reflects the expectation that interest rates (inflation) will rise in the long term. A decrease in the slope — in other words, the flattening of a positively sloped yield curve (the near side rising, the distant side falling) — expresses investors, with the expectation that interest rates will fall in the future, turning toward the long term in order to benefit from the current interest rates for longer, and hence, with the increasing demand, the return at that maturity falling; and those demanding funds, with the expectation that the opportunity of borrowing at lower interest rates will arise in the future, preferring the short term, and hence, with the excess supply, the return rising.
