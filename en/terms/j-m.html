Terms & Definitions

Ekonomi, finans ve borsa terminolojisi sözlüğünün J–M grubu. Tanımlar TCMB, Rekabet Kurumu ve iktisat sözlükleri gibi resmî kaynaklardan derlenmiştir. Diğer harf grupları:

A–B C–D E–F G–I J–M N–P Q–S T–Z

J

J-curve effect J-curve effect

It is the phenomenon whereby, as the foreign exchange rate rises, net exports first decline and then begin to increase.

Jensen's alpha Jensen’s alpha

It is the difference between the portfolio's return and the return predicted by CAPM: α = Rp − [Rf + β(Rm − Rf)]; a positive alpha is considered manager value added.

Joint Profit Maximisation

They must make their pricing and production decisions so as to maximise the profit of the whole group in formations such as cartels, duopolies and oligopolies.

K

Kaldor-Hicks efficiency Kaldor–Hicks efficiency

It is a resource allocation in which the losses of individuals whose position worsens are compensated as far as possible while improving the position of some individuals.

Keynesian economic theory Keynesian economic theory

It is the school of thought that argues changes in real income in the short run stem from demand shocks linked to spending and advocates government intervention in the economy through fiscal policy.

Kinked demand curve model kinked demand curve model

Or the Sweezy model, an oligopoly model in which demand for a firm's product depends on its response to rival firms' price changes.

Kolektif Hakimiyet

Joint dominance is a legal concept without a direct economic counterpart. In general, it is thought that joint dominance occurs in markets where there are few large undertakings that can coordinate their behavior with tacit cooperation in order to keep prices above competitive levels. For this reason, explicit coordination is not necessary. Successful cooperation requires not only alignment in prices and product levels, but also

Konsorsiyum

It is a group of independent undertakings that are employed together to carry out a certain project. It arises in situations involving large projects such as the construction market, where close cooperation between many undertakings is required.

L

Labor labour

Or abbreviated as L, the basic production factor describing the physical or mental work and labour performed by people.

labor demand labour demand

Or abbreviated as DL, the quantity of labour that producers want to employ depending on different wage rates in a given period.

labor market labour market

Or the labour market, the physical or virtual environment where those who supply labour and those who demand labour come together and the wage rate of labour is determined in the process.

labor market equilibrium labour market equilibrium

It is the situation in a given period in which the amount of labour demanded in the market equals the amount of labour supplied.

labor market or employment market labour market

It is the physical or virtual environment where those who supply labour and those who demand labour come together and where the wage rate of labour is determined in this process.

labor productivity labour productivity

It is the average labour production per worker in a given period.

labor shortage labour shortage

Or excess labor demand, the situation in which the quantity of labour demanded in the labour market exceeds the quantity supplied.

Labor supply labour supply

Or abbreviated as SL, the quantity of labour that households offer to the market depending on different wage rates in a given period.

Labor supply curve labour supply curve

A graphical tool showing the relationship between the wage level and the quantity of labour supplied, other things being equal.

Labour Force Labour Force

Those who show a desire to work from the working-age population and participate in the labour market are accepted into the labour force. Those who are looking for work but have not yet found a job, in other words the unemployed, together with the employed, constitute the total labour force; the share of the unemployed in it is the unemployment rate.

Labour Force Participation Rate Labour Force Participation Rate

It is the ratio of the labour force to the working-age population.

Land arazi

Or natural resource, abbreviated as N, a basic production factor describing land that can be cultivated or built on as well as every kind of resource that comes from the land and from nature.

land market land market

It is the physical or virtual environment where land and natural resources are bought and sold and the land rent rate is determined in this process.

land market equilibrium land market equilibrium

It is the situation in a given period in which the amount of land demanded in the market equals the amount of land supplied.

Late Liquidity Window Facility Late Liquidity Window Facility

The Late Liquidity Window (GLP) refers to the overnight TL borrowing facility that the CBRT, in its capacity as lender of last resort, provides without limit to banks at the end of the day (between 16:00 and 17:00), against collateral, in order to prevent problems that may arise in the payment systems and to meet their temporary liquidity needs, and the overnight TL lending facility it provides under the same conditions so that liquidity surpluses can be evaluated.

law of demand law of demand

A fundamental concept in economics stating that, other things being equal, as the price of a good rises, the quantity demanded of that good decreases or remains the same.

law of diminishing marginal utility the law of diminishing marginal utility

It is the basic economics concept stating that the additional benefit obtained from a product diminishes as more of it is consumed within a given period.

law of diminishing returns law of diminishing returns

It is the basic economics concept stating that when at least one factor of production is fixed, the contribution of an increase in another variable factor to production will gradually decline.

law of large numbers law of large numbers

It is the basic statistics concept stating that when a random event is repeated many times, the average result approaches the expected value.

law of one price law of one price

It is the idea that product prices will be the same in every country provided there are no transportation costs and no trade barriers.

Leading Indicator leading indicator

They are measurable factors that develop before a change is seen in basic macroeconomic aggregates such as national income and inflation.

Lease Certificate Lease Certificate

The security arranged by asset leasing companies, in their own name and for the account and benefit of the certificate holders, with the aim of financing the assets taken over by purchasing or leasing, enabling their holders to be entitled, in proportion to their shares, to the incomes obtained from these assets.

Leasing Leasing

It is the financing technique used to meet the medium-term funding needs of undertakings. It is a contract signed between the lessee and the lessor in which the lessee selects the asset and the lessor purchases it, the ownership remaining with the lessor while the use remains with the lessee.

The equipment was recognised as a finance lease because ownership transferred at the end of the term.(The equipment was recognised as a finance lease because ownership transferred at the end of the term.)

İngilizce tanım: A lease that transfers substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership to the lessee.

Lender of Last Resort Lender of Last Resort

A concept expressing that, because of their capacity as monetary authority and their obligations to ensure financial stability, central banks are the final institution of application for participants, in the event that the funding shortages arising in the system cannot be met from the market, the participants applying to the central bank and benefiting from the facilities arranged under this name.

Lender of last resort Lender of last resort

It is an institution that is ready to lend even when nobody is borrowing.

Leniency Programmes

They are programmes that regulate the procedures for not penalising those who cooperate with the competition authority in order to uncover cartels, or for granting reductions on the penalties to be imposed. In Turkish competition law, the relevant regulation has been made with the Leniency Regulation.

Lerner index Lerner index

Or abbreviated as L, the measure of market power calculated by dividing the difference between the price of a product and its marginal cost by the price.

Letter

It is the supporting document issued by the relevant distribution company for investments whose evaluation result in the production and connection applications of renewable energy investments is positive and that is also requested in incentive certificate applications.

Letter of Credit Letter of Credit

A type of guarantee concerning the payment for exported goods, under which payment will be made once certain conditions are fulfilled. Banks or other financial institutions intermediate letter-of-credit transactions. Accordingly, a letter of credit is a kind of letter of guarantee sent by a financial institution to a correspondent bank in a foreign country, committing it to extend credit on its own account for a certain period, in a certain amount and in favor of a third party.

Level level of employment

It is the development level determined by taking into account the economic, social, cultural etc. criteria of a country, province or district. Start-up It is the name given to innovative and risky ventures with rapid growth potential

Leverage Leverage

It is increasing a position using leverage or derivatives; it magnifies returns and losses by the same proportion.

Example: High leverage increases the entity's financial risk.( High leverage increases the entity’s financial risk.)

İngilizce tanım: The use of debt to increase the potential return on equity.

Leverage Ratio Leverage Ratio

The ratio of debt to equity or to total capital. Leverage ratios show a business's funding structure and make it possible to see what resources finance its assets and in what proportion. The leverage ratio developed under Basel III is calculated by dividing core capital by the total risk amount.

LIBOR London Interbank Offered Rate

It is the interest rate applied by highly rated banks when lending to one another over the key currencies (US dollar, euro, pound sterling, Japanese yen and Swiss franc) in the London interbank market.

License

The document given by the municipalities, provincial special administrations, organized industrial zones or the Ministry of Industry and Technology, as a result of the conditions sought in the workplaces specified in the regulation according to the activity being met. Redemption: the transaction of eliminating the debt by the debtor through payments made at once or in certain periods. Import Substitution: the domestic production of products previously imported.

Licensed Warehousing

The facility providing the services of preserving agricultural products in healthy conditions and storing them for commercial purposes, within the scope of the Licensed Warehousing of Agricultural Products Law No. 5300. Lobbying: the activity of influencing the decisions given by governments or international organizations. Mineral Exploration Investment: the investment directed at extracting a mineral. Mineral Processing Investment: the investment directed at processing a mineral. Return to Origin: the returning of a good to the place it came from (the country of exit/where the trade was conducted)

liquidity liquidity

It is a conceptual measure of how easily and quickly a financial asset can be converted into cash of an equivalent amount.

Liquidity Liquidity

It is the criterion for the ability of any asset or value traded on the market to be bought and sold without affecting its market price. It is also expected that the trading volumes of assets or values with high liquidity will be high.

Example: The company maintained sufficient liquidity to meet its maturing obligations.(The company maintained sufficient liquidity to meet its obligations as they fell due.)

İngilizce tanım: The availability of cash and the ability of an entity to meet its short-term obligations.

Liquidity Bills Liquidity Bills

A monetary policy instrument with the property of short-term bills, used with the aim of increasing the effectiveness of monetary policy and regulating the liquidity in the market (withdrawing the excess liquidity). According to Article 52 of the CBRT Law, the CBRT can issue liquidity bills with maturities not exceeding 91 days, in its own name and for its own account.

Liquidity Risk Liquidity Risk

Refers to funding and market liquidity risk. Funding risk is the risk of an additional funding need arising from a mismatch between short-term cash inflows and outflows, while market liquidity risk refers to the risk involving the time and value at which financial assets can be converted into cash.

Example: The entity monitors its liquidity risk through maturity analysis.( The entity monitors liquidity risk through maturity analysis.)

İngilizce tanım: The risk that an entity will struggle to meet its financial obligations.

Liquidity Support Credit Facility Liquidity Support Credit Facility

The Turkish lira credit support provided by the CBRT, in the event that uncertainty and insecurity form in the banking system and fund withdrawals accelerate, to the banks regarding which uncertainty and insecurity have formed but that have the ability to pay, in the form of advances with one-month maturities and for a maximum of one year, against collateral in an amount to cover the fund withdrawals, limited to twice their equity.

Liquidity Trap liquidity trap

It is the situation in which increasing the money supply cannot affect aggregate demand because the nominal interest rate has fallen to the lowest level it can reach.

LM curve LM curve

A graphical tool showing the relationship between aggregate production and the interest rate when the money market is in equilibrium.

Local Government Budget Local Government Budget

; It is the budget of organizations such as municipalities and special provincial administrations within the local government scope.

London Interbank Offered Rate London Interbank Offered Rate

Or abbreviated as LIBOR, the interest rate at which banks lend to one another for their unsecured overnight borrowings in the London money market.

Long run in the long run

In the microeconomic context, the conceptual time interval in which entry into or exit from the market is possible and all the production factors are variable. In the macroeconomic context, it is the conceptual time interval in which prices and wages are variable and the production factors can show full mobility among regions and sectors.

long run aggregate supply curve long-run aggregate supply curve

Or abbreviated as LRAS, the diagrammatic tool showing the long-run relationship between the general price level and the quantity of aggregate supply, with everything else held constant.

long run average cost long-run average cost

Or abbreviated as LRAC, the total cost per unit of product according to the scale chosen in the long run.

long run market supply curve long-run market supply curve

A graphical tool showing the long-run relationship between the price of a good and the quantity supplied in the market, other things being equal.

long run Phillips curve long-run Phillips curve

Or abbreviated as LRPC, the diagrammatic tool showing the relationship between the unemployment and inflation rates when expected and actual inflation are equal.

Lorenz curve Lorenz curve

It is a graphical tool showing the relationship between the percentage share of households in a country by population and the percentage share of income these households receive.

Loss aversion Loss aversion

It is the finding that according to Kahneman-Tversky's Prospect Theory losses feel about twice as heavy as equivalent gains; one of its consequences is that investors sell too late when in a losing position.

Low good inferior good

They are products whose demand in the market falls when the average income level rises.

Low unemployment low unemployment

It is a low share in the labour force of a country of those unemployed who are defined as people seeking work but not working in a job.

Lump-sum tax specific tax

It is a tax levied as a fixed amount per unit of the object taxed.

luxury good luxury goods

They are products whose demand in the market rises more rapidly than that of other products when the average income level rises.

M

Macaulay duration Macaulay duration

It is the maturity-weighted average present value of the bond's cash flows: for each t, t × PV is summed and divided by the price; yield.

Macroeconomic policy macroeconomic policy

They are the rules and practices the state uses to manage various macroeconomic aggregates, primarily national income and inflation.

Macroeconomic populism macroeconomic populism

It refers to politicians following unsustainable expansionary fiscal policies in the short term in order to look good to the public at the expense of harming the economy in the long term.

Macroeconomics macroeconomics

It is the branch of economics that treats economic decision-makers as a whole and studies their aggregate behaviour.

managed floating exchange rate managed floating exchange rate

It is the foreign exchange rate regime in which nominal exchange rates are determined by the market but the monetary authority intervenes in the rate when it deems necessary.

Managed Floating Exchange Rate Regime Managed Floating Exchange Rate Regime

It is the exchange rate regime in which exchange rates are freely determined in the market without an official rate target, but in which the authorities may intervene in the rates by selling foreign exchange to the market or buying foreign exchange from the market.

Management)

It is a systematic management approach meaning "Project Cycle Management" in Turkish, in which all stages of the project are operated in an interrelated manner.

Margin call Margin call

It is the additional collateral call of the intermediary when the collateral in a leveraged position falls below the required level; if it is not met, the position is liquidated.

Marginal cost marginal cost

Or abbreviated as MC, the additional cost incurred to increase production by one unit.

Marginal expenditure marginal expenditure

Or abbreviated as ME, the additional expenditure made in order to buy one unit more of a product.

Marginal private benefit marginal private benefit

Or abbreviated as MPB, the additional benefit that one unit more of a product provides to the consumer.

Marginal private cost marginal private cost

Or abbreviated as MPC, the additional cost the producer incurs to increase production by one unit.

Marginal product marginal product

Or abbreviated as MP, the additional product resulting from using one unit more of a given production factor.

Marginal product of capital Marginal Product of Capital

Or abbreviated as MPK, the change in the quantity of total product when the quantity of capital is increased by one unit.

marginal product per Lira marginal product per dollar

It is the additional output brought by spending one unit more money on a given factor of production.

Marginal propensity to consume marginal propensity to consume

Or abbreviated as MPC, the change in private consumption spending when total income in an economy rises by one unit.

Marginal propensity to import marginal propensity to import

Or abbreviated as MPM, the change in the volume of imports when total income in an economy rises by one unit.

Marginal propensity to save marginal propensity to save

Or abbreviated as MPS, the change in the volume of private saving when total income in an economy rises by one unit.

Marginal rate of substitution marginal rate of substitution

Or abbreviated as MRS, the amount of one good that must be given up in order to consume more of another good without changing total benefit.

Marginal rate of technical substitution marginal rate of technical substitution

Or abbreviated as MRTS, the amount of one input that must be given up in order to use more of another input without changing total product.

Marginal rate of transformation marginal rate of transformation

Or abbreviated as MRT, the opportunity cost of producing one unit more of a given good in terms of another good.

Marginal Revenue

It is the extra income earned when sales increase by one unit.

Marginal revenue marginal revenue

Or abbreviated as MR, the additional revenue resulting from selling one unit more.

Marginal revenue product marginal revenue product

Or abbreviated as MRP, the additional revenue resulting from using one unit more of a given production factor.

Marginal revenue product of capital marginal product of capital

Or abbreviated as MRPK, the change in total revenue when the quantity of capital is increased by one unit.

marginal revenue product of labor marginal product of labour

Or abbreviated as MRPL, the change in total revenue when the quantity of labour is increased by one unit.

marginal revenue product of land marginal revenue product of land

Or abbreviated as MRPN, the change in total revenue when the quantity of land or natural resources is increased by one unit.

Marginal social benefit social benefit

Or abbreviated as MSB, the additional benefit that one unit more of a product provides to the consumer together with the whole of society.

Marginal social cost marginal social cost

Or abbreviated as MSC, the additional production cost that one unit more entails for the producer together with the whole of society.

Marginal utility marginal utility

Or abbreviated as MU, the additional benefit obtained from consuming one unit more of a product in a given period.

marginal utility per lira marginal utility per dollar

It is the additional benefit brought by spending one unit more money on a given good or service.

Marginal value marginal value

Or abbreviated as MV, the additional benefit that the purchase of one unit more of a product brings to a monopsonist buyer.

marginal value product of labor marginal product of labour

Or abbreviated as MVPL, the revenue obtained from selling the additional product resulting from increasing the quantity of labour by one unit.

Mark of origin

They are names identifying products made within the boundaries of a geographically determined area, region or country that has a distinctive quality, reputation or other characteristic and in which at least one of production, processing or other operations takes place.

Market market

It is the physical or virtual environment where buyers and sellers come together and product prices are determined in this process.

Market basket of goods market basket of goods

Or basket of goods, the list of goods determined in order to represent the economy as a whole or a particular sub-sector.

Market concentration market concentration

It is a conceptual measure that indicates how competitive a market is by looking at the distribution of firms' market shares.

Market demand market demand

Or abbreviated as D, the quantity of the product that all buyers in the market want to buy at different prices in a given period.

market disequilibrium market disequilibrium

It is a short- or long-lasting situation in which the market fails to reach its equilibrium point due to internal or external reasons.

market equilibrium market equilibrium

It is the situation in a given period in which the amount of goods demanded in the market equals the amount of goods supplied.

Market failure market failure

It is the situation in which social welfare decreases as a result of a decline in the market's efficiency in production or distribution.

Market Interest Rate Market Interest Rate

It is the interest rate in the money market that is determined by the interaction of money supply and money demand and that is paid for deposits and other investments.

Example: The loan was granted at the market interest rate. ( The loan was granted at the market rate of interest.)

English definition: The prevailing interest rate in the market.

Market Maker System Authorised Dealership

A system in which, with the aim of increasing effectiveness in primary market transactions and facilitating the functioning of secondary markets, the treasury or central banks accept as counterparty only the banks and certain other institutions it selects according to certain criteria, in transactions such as debt security issuance (auction) and foreign exchange intervention. The institutions selected under the name of market maker have obligations such as transacting uninterruptedly in the secondary markets, giving quotations, and purchasing a certain amount of an issue.

Market or marketplace market

It is the physical or virtual environment where buyers and sellers come together and product prices are determined in this process.

market power market power

It is a measure of how much a buyer or seller can influence the market price of a given product.

Market structure market structure

It is the concept that describes the characteristics of the economic interaction arising from the distribution of the number and size of buyers and sellers in the market.

Market supply market supply

Or abbreviated as S, the quantity of the product that all sellers in the market want to sell at different prices in a given period.

Market value market value

It is the price of goods and services determined jointly by supply and demand in a free market.

Example: The market value of the land increased sharply. ( The market value of the land rose sharply.)

İngilizce tanım: The price at which an asset could be traded in the market.

market-clearing price market-clearing price

Or clearing price, the price at which transactions take place when the market is in equilibrium.

Master Plan

The term for the master plan reports showing all the particulars of lands with determined cadastral status on plans at a scale of 1/2000 or 1/5000, prepared in a manner consistent with the environment or regional order plans. Master plans are created to show the uses of lands, the zone types, the population structure and the transportation systems. All these points are stated in a detailed report. The master plans show what form a settlement area will take in the future.

Maturity maturity

The Payback period, meaning the maturity or date for the repayment of a loan taken.

Example: The bond's maturity is ten years from issue. ( (The bond matures ten years after issue.)

İngilizce tanım: The date an instrument becomes due for settlement.

Maturity-Based Required Reserves Maturity-Based Reserve Requirement

The required reserve amount that banks and other financial institutions must hold at the CBRT against their liabilities varies according to the maturity of those liabilities. Reserve requirement ratios are higher for short-term liabilities and lower for long-term ones. Thereby, by encouraging longer-term liabilities, the aim is to extend the average maturity of banks' liabilities.

medium of exchange medium of exchange

It is the function and property of being used in the purchase of goods and services by money.

Mental accounting Mental accounting

It is the division of money into mental pockets according to its source and intended use; it contradicts the principle of the convertibility of money into one another.

Menu Cost menu price

They are the costs that firms incur in order to change the nominal prices of products.

Mercantilism merkantilizm

It is the school of thought that argues that wealth is determined by the amount of valuables owned and that commercial relations are fundamentally a zero-sum game.

Merger merger

It is the merger of two companies of their own accord into a single new company.

Micro Enterprise

They are enterprises employing fewer than ten people on a yearly basis and whose annual net sales revenue or balance sheet does not exceed 3 million Turkish Lira.

Microcredit microcredit

They are credits granted to low-income persons and especially to women so that they can engage in a production activity that will earn their living.

Microeconomics microeconomics

It is the branch of economics that treats individual economic decision-makers and studies their behaviour.

Microfinance microfinance

They are small-scale banking and insurance services provided to people who have difficulty accessing the traditional financial system.

Midpoint formula midpoint formula

It is a calculation method that divides the net change by the average change in order to find the average percentage change in the economy.

Minimum Efficient Scale

The minimum efficient scale is the smallest scale at which average costs are minimized. This concept carries importance for understanding market structure. For example, in the case where the minimum efficient scale is smaller compared to the general size of the market, a greater number of firms is expected to be found in the market, and hence the market is expected to have a competitive structure.

minimum living allowance minimum living allowance

It is the exemption of a certain part of the wage from tax.

minimum wage minimum wage

It is the minimum wage determined by the state as the counterpart of labour for a certain period of time and which cannot be reduced by mutual agreement.

mixed economy mixed economy

It is the economic system in which what is produced, how and for whom is decided by the interaction of buyers and sellers in the markets, but in which the state also has an influence.

mixed good mixed goods

Or club goods, goods that are excludable in consumption but do not create rivalry.

mixed income mixed-income

They are the incomes arising from the combination of different factors of production that cannot be separated.

Model model

It is the whole of mathematical definitions and relationships that serve to describe and predict an economic fact or behaviour by simplifying it to a certain extent.

Model Factory

The training applications specially designed — and the physical environments where these applications will be offered — to develop the application-oriented skills of enterprises, through learning by doing in experimental settings, on matters such as lean production, process improvement, product development and problem solving, within the scope of the efficiency increase and digitalization required for global competition in enterprises, and to transform firms by quickly putting these new skills into practice.

modern quantity theory modern quantity theory

It is the set of theories arguing that, under the assumption that the velocity of money circulation is stable, there is a direct and proportional relationship between the money supply and the general price level.

Modernization Investment

Refers to the investments including the addition of parts suited to the machinery and equipment that have completed their technical and/or economic life in the production lines of existing facilities, or the replacement of existing machinery and equipment with new ones, the completion of the sections left incomplete in the facility, the direct raising of the quality of the final product or the changing of its model.

Modified duration Modified duration

It is a sensitivity measure giving the percentage effect on price of a one-unit change in yield, found by dividing the Macaulay duration by (1+y): ΔP ≈ −D*·Δy.

monetarist economic theory monetarist economic theory

It is the school of thought that argues changes in real income in the short run stem from demand shocks linked to money supply and advocates government intervention in the economy through rules-based monetary policy.

Monetary Aggregates Monetary Aggregates

A monetary magnitude is calculated by subtracting the monetary sector's liabilities to one another from the total of the money in circulation and the various liabilities of the monetary sector (the central bank, banks and money market funds) that are monetary or convertible into money. There are various monetary magnitude definitions according to the degree of liquidity of the assets they contain: M1: consists of the total of the money in circulation and demand deposits. M2: consists of the total of M1 and time deposits. M3: M3, the most broadly defined money supply, consists of the total of M2, the funds provided from repos, money market funds and issued securities (with maturities of up to two years).

Monetary Gold Monetary Gold

It refers to gold owned by the central bank and held as reserves.

monetary growth rate rule monetary growth rate rule

It is the monetary policy principle that recommends the central bank increase the money supply in proportion to the growth rate of real GDP.

Monetary Policy Monetary Policy

Refers to the decisions taken with the aim of influencing the availability and cost of money in order to reach aims such as economic growth, increasing employment and price stability. The institutions responsible for its implementation are central banks. In the CBRT Law, it is stated that the fundamental aim of the CBRT is to ensure price stability.

Monetary Policy Committee - MPC Monetary Policy Committee – MPC

The board formed within the CBRT, charged and authorized with determining the monetary policy principles and strategies with the aim of ensuring price stability, and, within the framework of these strategies, determining the inflation target together with the government, taking the measures necessary to preserve the internal and external value of the Turkish lira, and determining, again together with the government, the exchange rate regime directed at establishing its parity against foreign currencies and gold. The Monetary Policy Committee, under the chairmanship of the Governor, consists of the Deputy Governors, a member to be elected by the Bank Council from among its members, and a member to be appointed by joint decision upon the Governor's proposal. The Undersecretary of Treasury or the Deputy Undersecretary he designates may attend the meetings without voting rights.

monetary targeting monetary targeting

It is the monetary policy regime in which the central bank targets a certain growth rate for monetary aggregates such as the money supply and bank reserves.

Monetary Transmission Mechanism Monetary Transmission Mechanism

Shows through which channels and to what extent monetary variables affect aggregate demand, the output gap and inflation. The monetary transmission mechanism is generally explained in three stages. The first stage determines the transmission of the changes in monetary policy implementations to variables such as interest rates, asset prices, expectations and the exchange rate. In the second stage, the said variables affect the demand for domestic and imported goods; in the third stage, aggregate demand and domestic pricing behaviors are determined, and domestic prices and import prices shape inflation. In addition, interest rates have direct effects on inflation, and exchange rates on the demand for imported goods and on import prices in local currency.

Monopoly monopoly

A monopoly is the situation in which there is a single seller in the market. The demand curve facing the monopolist is negatively sloped. The comparison of the outcomes of the monopoly and fully competitive markets shows that the monopolist sets a higher price, produces less, and earns more than economic profit (monopoly rent). In this case, consumers face a high price in a way that causes a loss of welfare, and income flows from consumers to the monopolist undertaking

Mortgage Loans Mortgage

Refers to a type of real estate financing system envisaging the extension of long-term housing credits against the mortgage to be realized over real estates such as houses and plots, by a bank or a similar financial institution.
