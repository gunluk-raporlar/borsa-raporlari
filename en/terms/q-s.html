Terms & Definitions

Ekonomi, finans ve borsa terminolojisi sözlüğünün Q–S grubu. Tanımlar TCMB, Rekabet Kurumu ve iktisat sözlükleri gibi resmî kaynaklardan derlenmiştir. Diğer harf grupları:

A–B C–D E–F G–I J–M N–P Q–S T–Z

Q

Quantitative Easing Quantitative Easing

An unconventional monetary policy approach, meaning central banks' increasing the money supply by purchasing financial assets from commercial banks or other private institutions. After the global financial crisis, the central banks of the United States in particular, and of England, Europe and Japan, applied quantitative expansion policies of different magnitudes in order to support the financial sector, increase private consumption expenditures and prevent inflation from falling below the target.

Quantity restriction quantity restriction

It is the market intervention applied by the state by determining the quantity supplied for a product.

quantity theory of money quantity theory of money

It is the set of theories arguing that there is a direct and proportional relationship between the money supply and the general price level.

Quota quota

They are restrictions in the form of quantity or monetary value applied by the state to imported goods and services.

R

Ramsey Pricing

Ramsey pricing envisages that prices be set inversely proportional to demand elasticity, so that welfare losses can be brought down to the lowest level. In the case where there are demands of different elasticity (sensitivity) for the same product, on the other hand, Ramsey pricing envisages that this product be offered to different consumer groups at different prices according to consumers' sensitivity to price. While a low price is applied to the consumer group whose demand is elastic (price-sensitive), to the consumer group whose demand is inelastic

Random Event Random Event

It is a set of outcomes, each with a certain probability of occurring.

rational expectations hypothesis rational expectations hypothesis

It is the view that economic agents, when making forecasts about the future, take all information about the past and the future into account.

Re-export

It refers to the sale of goods that have been purchased and are of foreign or Turkish origin and have been exported, whether transferred for processing or not, to another country in transit or directly, without being subject to the import and export regime provisions.

reaction function reaction function

It is a mathematical relationship explaining how the behaviour of one economic unit changes depending on other economic units.

Real business cycle theory Real business cycle theory

It is the school of thought that argues changes in real income in the short run stem from supply shocks and advocates government intervention in the economy with supply-oriented policies.

Real Effective Exchange Rate Real Effective Exchange Rate

It is the real effective foreign exchange rate calculated taking into account relative price changes between countries.

Real Exchange Rate Real Exchange Rate

Or abbreviated as RER, the foreign exchange rate calculated taking into account the difference in the general price level between two countries.

Real Exports Real Exports

It is expressed by the export volume index. The export volume index measures the change in export volume provided that prices are constant. The export volume indices are calculated by TÜİK.

Real GDP Real GDP

Or GDP in fixed prices, the national income method that measures the value of goods and services produced in a given period at the prices of a fixed base year.

Real Imports Real Imports

It is expressed by the import volume index. The import volume index measures the change in import volume provided that prices are constant. The import volume indices are calculated by TÜİK.

Real Interest Real Interest Rate

It is interest rate adjusted for inflation; with the Fisher equation (1+real) = (1+nominal)/(1+inflation), approximately real ≈ nominal − inflation.

Real Interest Rate Real Interest Rate

It is the nominal interest adjusted for inflation. Technically, it is obtained by subtracting the inflation rate expected from the nominal interest rate.

Real Sector Confidence Index - RSCI Real Sector Confidence Index – RSCI

It is an indicator that summarises the answers given to the different questions of the Survey of Economic Expectations in order to bring out the general impressions of sector representatives regarding the economic outlook. In order to reach the Real Sector Confidence Index

Real Time Gross Settlement Real-Time Gross Settlement – RTGS

The settlement of fund and securities transfers individually and instantaneously (continuously), in an order, without netting. Although classifiable in various respects, payment systems are classified, according to the settlement type, into real-time gross settlement systems and settlement systems based on netting. Today the great majority of payment systems of systemic importance operate with real-time gross settlement methods.

Rebate Schemes/Systems

Rebate systems can emerge in very different forms under the dynamic conditions of commercial life, and vary in their structures, workings and effects. If the earning of the rebate in any rebate system is made conditional on the purchase of a single product, such rebates are accepted as 'single-product rebates'. However, if the purchase condition in the rebate system covers multiple products or markets, the said rebates are 'bundle rebates'

Recession Recession

It is a period in which economic growth is negative or slow. It may also be defined as the presence of spare capacity in the economy, or as growth at a rate below the economy's long-term growth rate.

Reciprocity Agreement

A type of bilateral (or multilateral) agreement made among undertakings in order to provide one another favorable conditions, or to buy from and sell to one another mutually. In reciprocity agreements, an undertaking can be obliged to purchase from the undertaking that purchases from it. Thanks to this agreement, the undertaking can obtain a price reduction by making its purchases from its own customers, or can activate its idle capacity. These competitive effects that reciprocity agreements can create

recognition lag recognition delay

It is the time between the occurrence of an adverse situation in the economy and the recognition of this situation by economists.

Recovery

The recovery of unusable recyclable waste materials into manufacturing processes again as raw material through various recycling methods. GES: the abbreviation of Solar Power Plant, systems converting the energy coming from the sun into electrical energy. Input: the in-kind and financial resources necessary for the realization of project activities and used in project activities.

Recycling

It is the inclusion in the production process again of waste that can be re-evaluated, after being put through various physical and/or chemical processes, by being turned into a secondary raw material.

Example: For some items, recycling of gains on disposal is permitted.( Recycling of gains on disposal is permitted for some items.)

İngilizce tanım: Transferring amounts in other comprehensive income to profit or loss.

Redemption Redemption

It is the repayment at the maturity date set at the time of issue of the debt securities, together with accrued interest and principal.

Example: The amortization of the patent is recorded as a monthly expense. ( Amortisation of the patent is charged monthly.

İngilizce tanım: Systematic allocation of the cost of an intangible asset over its useful life.

Rediscount Rediscount

Refers to the rediscounting — the exchanging for a consideration again (rediscount) — of the instruments that have already been discounted, in other words exchanged for a consideration (discount). It is an instrument used by central banks in monetary policy implementations, under the name rediscount window, by subjecting various bills to discounting (re-discount), on the condition that they determine their own conditions.

Rediscount credit Discount Credit

It is the credit that the central bank provides to commercial banks against securities.

Rediscount rate Discount Rate

It is the interest rate applied by the central bank for rediscount loans.

redistribution of income income redistribution

These are practices aimed at transferring wealth directly or indirectly from high-income groups to low-income groups.

Regional Development Administration

Corporate structures with legal personality that operate for the development of the region they are responsible for, increasing its competitiveness and reducing development gaps. In our country there are 4 Regional Development Administrations: the Eastern Anatolia Project (DAP) Regional Development Administration, the Eastern Black Sea Project (DOKAP) Regional Development Administration, the Konya Plain Project (KOP) Regional Development Administration, and the Southeastern Anatolia Project (GAP) Regional Development Administration.

Rejection of the Transactions

The non-implementation by the relevant financial institutions of the transactions sought to be carried out at the financial institutions. These are the transactions in which the financial institutions could not carry out the identification and verification of the identity of the customer or of the person finally benefiting from the transaction, could not determine to whom the ownership and control belong in legal persons and partnerships, and could not have information about the type and purpose of the transaction being carried out

Rekabet Ekonomisi

The economic analyses used in the enforcement of competition rules. In the early periods, when only legal assessments were made in competition rules, the necessity of the economic analysis of market conditions and of consumer and firm behavior came to be accepted over time. Within this framework, economic and econometric studies are used in the interpretation and enforcement of competition rules.

Rekabet Hukuku

The rules concerning the preservation of competition. These rules contain the regulations necessary to establish and preserve an effective competitive environment in systems where the market economy, and hence free competition, is embraced. The prevention of agreements among undertakings, decisions of undertaking associations and practices that obstruct, distort or restrict competition, and the prevention of the abuse of dominance by undertakings dominant in the market; the review of merger or acquisition transactions for the establishment of competition in the markets

Rekabet Testi (SLC Testi)

Another of the competition-based tests used in the control of concentrations, besides the dominance test, is the 'Substantial Lessening of Competition Test' (SLC Test). The difference between the two tests stems from the difference of approach to the causes of the anticompetitive effects of mergers, rather than the effects themselves. In the SLC test, a substantial lessening of competition occurs through the creation of market power or the strengthening of existing market power, while in the dominance test, the creation of a dominant position

relative poverty relative poverty

It is the state of not having enough income to be able to afford the average living conditions of a country.

relative poverty rate relative poverty rate

It is the ratio of the population with an income below the average income in a country to the total population.

Reliance on Intermediaries and

Third Parties: The leaving, under certain conditions, by the financial institutions of the fulfillment of the measures relating to the identification and verification of the customer's and the beneficiary's identity, the determination of to whom the ownership and control belong in legal persons and partnerships, and the obtaining of information about the type and purpose of the transaction sought to be carried out, in the customer due diligence process, to intermediary institutions or other third parties. In this case, all the responsibility relating to the identification and verification of the customer's identity falls on the third party

Renewable Energy

It is energy obtained from the existing energy flow in continuous natural processes. Renewable energy sources can be listed as solar energy, wind energy, geothermal energy, hydraulic energy, biomass energy and hydrogen energy.

rent Rent

Or rent, the pre-determined price paid for using land or natural resources belonging to someone else.

Rent or Lease Rent

It is a fixed amount paid for the use of land or natural resources belonging to someone else.

rent seeking rent-seeking

It is the attempt by economic agents to influence the state so as to secure benefits for themselves at the expense of others.

Rental rate of capital rate of return on capital

Or abbreviated as PK, the price paid for using one unit of capital in production.

rental rate of land land rental rate

Or abbreviated as PN, the price paid for using one unit of land or a natural resource in production.

Repo Repurchase Agreement

It is generally used in cases where the liquidity tightness in the market is temporary, with the aim of increasing the banking system's liquidity for a temporary period. The central bank buys instruments with a commitment to resell them at a future date (at the price determined during the transaction) from the institutions authorized to conduct open market operations. The resale price is determined on the date the purchase transaction is made. The institution party to the transaction also undertakes to buy the instrument subject to the repo transaction at the transaction's maturity. From the central bank's perspective, the repo transaction expresses the temporary provision of liquidity to the market within the framework of open market operations, for the duration of the transaction's maturity, in return for the instruments subject to the repo.

Example: Securities sold under a repurchase agreement have been recorded as collateralized borrowing.(The securities sold under the repurchase agreement were recorded as a collateralised borrowing.)

İngilizce tanım: An agreement to sell securities with a commitment to buy them back at a later date.

representative money representative money

They are general means of payment that have no significant intrinsic use value but can, on request, be converted into a certain amount of precious metal.

required reserve required reserve

It is the money a bank is required to hold at the central bank account in proportion to the deposits it has gathered.

Reserve Reserve

It is the total of the cash in banks' vaults and their accounts at the central bank.

Example: The revaluation reserve is presented within equity.( The revaluation reserve is presented within equity.

Reserve assets Reserve Assets

It is the main heading in the balance of payments that shows the central bank's official foreign exchange reserves.

Reserve Currency Hard Currency

Refers to the assets in foreign currency and gold held in the portfolios of central banks and international financial institutions. Accordingly, for a payment instrument to carry the property of reserve currency, its value against other currencies must be stable, it must belong to a country with a large share in world trade, and it must be readily bought and sold in the foreign exchange markets.

Reserve Currency Reserve Currency

It is the currency unit held as an official reserve by central banks because it is widely used in international transactions.

Reserve Option Coefficient - ROK Reserve Option Coefficient – ROC

They are the coefficients that determine the foreign exchange or gold equivalent that may be established per one unit of Turkish lira of required reserves under the Reserve Option Mechanism.

Reserve Option Mechanism - ROM Reserve Option Mechanism – ROM

It is a practice that allows banks and other financial institutions to maintain a certain percentage of their Turkish lira reserve requirements in foreign currency and gold.

Reserve Option Ratio Reserve Option Ratio

It is the ratio showing what percentage of banks' Turkish lira required reserves can be established in foreign exchange or gold under the Reserve Option Mechanism.

Reserve Requirement Ratio Reserve Requirement Ratio

The statutory ratio showing the amount of reserves that banks and other financial institutions are required to hold at the central bank against the deposits, loans and similar liabilities on their balance sheets. The reserve requirement ratio may differ according to the type, maturity and currency of the liabilities. Central banks can use this ratio as a monetary policy instrument. If banks do not hold idle reserves, an increase in the reserve requirement ratio leads banks to call back the loans they have extended, which causes the money supply to contract. When the ratio is lowered, part of the required reserves turns into usable reserves, which increases the banks' lending base; the expansion of the banks' lending base in turn causes the money supply to grow.

Reserves

According to the principle contributed to the industrial economics literature by E.A.G. Robinson for the first time in 1958, as the scale of a production unit increases, savings can be achieved in the proportion of the reserve capacity set aside to meet future demand. For example, the number of beds a large hospital leaves empty for sudden needs will be less than the number of beds left empty by two small hospitals half its size.

Residential investment residential investment

They are the expenditures made by resident economic units for the purchase of houses and buildings.

Resolution Resolution

The subjection of a financial institution, as a result of the relevant authorities' establishing that it no longer possesses the capital necessary for it to be able to continue its activities, to a special intervention, with the aim of its liquidation, or of its being restored to the capital structure it needs in order to be able to continue its activities.

resource

It refers to the source of capital taken from banks/financial institutions or from third parties with a different maturity when the internal funding of undertakings is insufficient.

Resource curse resource curse

Or the paradox of plenty; the phenomenon whereby natural resources such as oil bring conflict, war, poverty and deprivation in some countries instead of economic growth.

Restriction on Technology

The designing by the undertaking of the new technology, although it is possible to design it in another way, in a way that other undertakings cannot use, in order to give itself an advantage — in other words, creating a barrier to market entry. This situation can be unlawful if carried out by the undertaking holding a dominant position and the other conditions are also present.

Restrictions on Resale Price

In a vertical relationship, the setting by the seller of the buyer's fixed or minimum resale price is prohibited under Article 4 of the Competition Act. However, on the condition that it does not turn into fixed or minimum resale prices, it is possible for the supplier to determine the buyer's maximum resale price or to recommend a resale price to the buyer. So that the maximum or recommended resale prices notified to the buyer do not turn into minimum or fixed prices, it is necessary that the said prices be published as maximum or recommended in the published price lists

Restructuring Restructuring

All the activities of a financial market infrastructure institution — including the replenishment of the financial resources previously established but exhausted, and various liquidity arrangements — with the aim of resolving any unmet loss, liquidity shortage or capital insufficiency problem that has arisen because of participant default or other reasons (business, operational or other structural weaknesses), and that has remained unresolved despite the use of the normal mechanisms, in conformity with the relevant legislation it is subject to, its own internal rules and procedures, and its contractual obligations.

Example: The restructuring closed two production plants. ( The restructuring led to the closure of two production plants.)

İngilizce tanım: Programme that materially changes the scope or manner of operations.

Result

The measurable, concrete situations expected to form within 1-3 years following the completion of the project, obtained in line with the specific aims determined at the outset. Social Enterprise: the type of venture that aims to make profit but uses the profit it obtains to produce benefit for society. Social Innovation: the term for the innovative works used in addressing the problems and needs arising in societies.

Example: The result for the year was reported after tax.( The result for the year was reported after tax.)

Retail Payment System Retail Payment System

As a component of the EFT System owned and operated by the CBRT, the system providing the real-time electronic settlement of the payments banks carry out on behalf of their customers, such as account-based and name-based customer payments, public payments, housing, workplace and other rent payments, salary and credit card payments.

Return on Assets - ROA Return on Assets – ROA

It is the ratio of net profit to total assets (Net profit / Total assets); it shows how profitably the asset base generates profit.

Return on equity Return on Equity – ROE

It is the ratio of net profit to equity (Net profit / Equity); it is the basic profitability ratio measuring return per shareholder.

Revaluation Revaluation

It expresses the increase in the value of the national currency against foreign currencies. For example, the rise of the Turkish lira against the US dollar from 1.3 TL to 1.2 TL (a depreciation of the US dollar) means that the TL gains 0.1 TL of value.

Example: The revaluation of the property increased its carrying amount and recognised the surplus in other comprehensive income.(A revaluation of the property increased its carrying amount and recognised the surplus in other comprehensive income.)

İngilizce tanım: The remeasurement of a non-current asset to its fair value, with the increase recognised in other comprehensive income.

revenue equivalence theorem Revenue Equivalence Theorem

It is a mathematical resolution showing that, under the assumption that participants are risk neutral and the value given to a product is person-specific, the same income is obtained on average from the four basic types of auction.

Revenue Indexed Bonds Revenue-Indexed Bonds

With the aim of increasing domestic savings, diversifying Government Domestic Debt Securities and broadening the investor base, the first Revenue-Indexed Securities (GES) issue was carried out by the Ministry of Treasury and Finance of the Republic of Türkiye on 28 January 2009. In GES issues, returns are indexed to the revenue shares transferred to the budget from the Türkiye Petrolleri Anonim Ortaklığı (TPAO), which has the status of a State Economic Enterprise, the State Supply Office (DMO), the State Airports Authority (DHMİ) and the General Directorate of Coastal Safety (KIYEM). With Revenue-Indexed Securities, an investment instrument has been created whose return is linked to state revenues. Thanks to the minimum coupon payment guarantee to be applied to the said securities, the investor is protected from being adversely affected by the change in the revenue share. Within this framework, in calculating the payments relating to the returns of the said securities, the total of the quarterly and semi-annual revenue shares transferred to the budget by TPAO, DMO, DHMİ and KIYEM is taken as the basis. If the realized revenue collection exceeds the maximum amounts announced in the budget, payment is made to investors based on the counterparts of the said maximum amounts.

reverse price discrimination reverse price discrimination

Or fourth-degree price discrimination, the practice of selling a given product to each buyer at the same price even if its cost differs between buyers.

Reverse Repo Reverse Repurchase Agreement

Reverse repo transactions are generally carried out, in cases of a temporary liquidity surplus in the market, with the aim of withdrawing the excess liquidity. The central bank sells the instruments in its portfolio to the institutions authorized to conduct open market operations, with a commitment to buy them back at a future date (at the price determined during the transaction). The repurchase price is likewise determined on the date the sale transaction is made. The institution party to the transaction also undertakes to sell the instrument subject to the reverse repo transaction to the central bank at the transaction's maturity. From the central bank's perspective, the reverse repo transaction expresses the temporary withdrawal of liquidity from the market, within the framework of open market operations, for the duration of the transaction's maturity, in return for the instruments given.

Rights of the Complainant

They are rights such as the right to lodge a complaint within the regulatory framework, the right to go to court against the decisions of the Board, the right to receive a reasoned decision, the right to be informed, the right to request that the name be kept confidential and the right to attend an oral defence hearing.

Risk Risk

It is the extent to which the actual outcome of a future event may differ from the expected outcome.

Example: The entity discloses the nature and extent of the risks arising from financial instruments.(The entity discloses the nature and extent of the risks arising from financial instruments.)

İngilizce tanım: The possibility that actual outcomes will differ from expected outcomes, including financial risks such as credit, liquidity and market risk.

Risk Appetite Risk Appetite

It is the desire to buy, which increases as positive expectations rise in financial markets and decreases as negative expectations grow.

risk averse risk-averse

It is a person who prefers the less risky of two options with the same expected value.

Risk Averse, Risk Lover, Risk Neutral Risk-Neutral

The concepts used to express an investor's preferences in the face of risk. While a risk-averse investor settles for a low but predictable return instead of an uncertain high return in the future, a risk-loving investor tends to take high risk even though the expected returns are equal. In contrast, a risk-neutral investor is the investor who makes no choice between risk and return.

Risk lover risk-taker

It is a person who prefers the more risky of two options with the same expected value.

risk neutral risk-neutral

It is a person who is indifferent between two options with the same expected value.

Risk pool Risk pool

It is a method of risk distribution in which more than one insurance company joins together and shares a certain risk.

Risk Premium Risk Premium

It indicates the extra return that a risky asset or holding provides to the investor compared with a completely risk-free asset or holding. The risk premium of an asset or holding can also be regarded as an indicator of the compensation obtained compared with a completely risk-free investment instrument.

Risk-Return Risk-Return

Refers to the relationship between the return expected to be obtained from an investment made in any asset or instrument and the risk assumed with the investment made in the said asset or instrument. In principle, there is a positive relationship between the risk assumed and the expected return. Hence, particularly in the financial markets, as the risk assumed increases, the return is expected to increase, and as the risk assumed decreases, the return is expected to decrease. Risk is generally expressed by the standard deviation of the investment's past returns (historical volatility). The expected return from the investment, on the other hand, is the average of the past returns. The risk-return curve formed by the maximum returns that can be obtained in return for a certain risk is called the 'efficient frontier', and its use is particularly for comparison and benchmark purposes.

rivalry in consumption competition in consumption

It is the inability of others to consume a good or service at the same time as one person is using it.

rule of seventy the rule of seventy

It is a rule of thumb that approximately finds the number of periods needed for a variable to double as 70 / growth rate.

"Strategic Investment"

They are investments of strategic importance with the potential to increase our international competitiveness, directed at the production of goods that are highly import-dependent in order to reduce the current account deficit.

"Strategic Product"

"Strategy" Strategy

It refers to the choices that economic agents make in order to achieve their objectives.

"Structure - Conduct-Performance Paradigm"

According to this approach, developed in the 1940s and playing a determining role in the American industrial economics literature between 1940 and 1970, an industry's performance depends, depending on the structure of the market, on the behaviors of the buyers and sellers in the market; the market structure in turn depends on fundamental conditions such as technology and demand. Hence structure is used as the predictor of conduct and performance. The market's structure (such as full competition, monopoly, monopolistic competition, oligopoly) is generally defined with concentration rates

"Subprime Markets" Subprime Markets

The markets where persons with a poor credit history can obtain credit at a higher interest. If this credit has been taken for the purpose of buying a home, it is called 'subprime mortgage'. The institutions extending the credit can re-release these credits to the market by dividing them into risk groups and can seek investors who will purchase them.

"Subsidy" Subsidy

It is an unconditional payment or tax reduction made by the state in the public interest.

"Sustainable growth" Sustainable growth

It is long-term economic growth that can realistically be achieved within the framework of an action plan that takes limited natural resources and their environmental impacts into account.

"Sweezy model" Sweezy model

Or the kinked demand curve model, an oligopoly model in which demand for a firm's product depends on its response to rival firms' price changes.

"SWOT Analysis"

An analysis method, carried out concerning the current situation, based on the determination of the strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats. It is formed from the initial letters of the English words strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats. Completion Visa: the approval given that an investment made by obtaining an Investment Incentive Certificate has been completed.

Sale Ask, Offer

Sample Banknote Specimen

The banknote whose possibility of circulation has been removed by the writing 'Örnektir Geçmez' (Sample — Not Valid) or 'Specimen' being printed or stapled on it, with the aim of introducing the banknotes to be put into circulation.

Samurai bonds

It refers to security issues made by foreigners in Japan and denominated in Japanese yen. They are mostly issues with a maturity of 5 years and above. The first issue was made in 1970 by the Asian Development Bank.

Sanitary Institution

It is a type of workplace that, by its nature, does not harm the environment through effects such as noise, odour and hazardous waste. Therefore it does not need to be located outside the settlement areas.

saving function save function

It is a mathematical relationship showing how private savings change depending on fundamental variables such as the income level in an economy.

Savings Savings

It is the portion of income that is not spent on consumption as a result of postponing consumption to a future date. The desire to earn more in the future or the instinct to take precautions against unexpected situations that may arise later are among the reasons that increase individuals' savings propensity.

Savings Gap Savings Gap

It is the amount of external financing required to cover investments in a period in which domestic total savings cannot cover domestic total investments.

Scarcity shortage

Or excess demand, the situation in which the quantity demanded in the market exceeds the quantity supplied.

Search Warrant Authorization

( A permit issued by a judicial authority (judge) for law enforcement to conduct a search. SECI (Southeast European Cooperation Initiative) is the Southeast European Cooperation Initiative established pursuant to the "Agreement on Cooperation and Combat Transborder Crime" signed in Bucharest on May 26, 1999. Second Best
