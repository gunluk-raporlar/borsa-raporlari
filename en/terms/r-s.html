Ramsey Pricing

Ramsey pricing envisages that prices be set inversely proportional to demand elasticity, so that welfare losses can be brought down to the lowest level. In the case where there are demands of different elasticity (sensitivity) for the same product, on the other hand, Ramsey pricing envisages that this product be offered to different consumer groups at different prices according to consumers' sensitivity to price. While a low price is applied to the consumer group whose demand is elastic (price-sensitive), to the consumer group whose demand is inelastic

Rent-seekingrent-seeking

It is the attempt by economic agents to influence the state so as to secure benefits for themselves at the expense of others.

Rent or rentRent

It is a fixed amount paid for the use of land or natural resources belonging to someone else.

Random eventRandom Event

It is a set of outcomes, each with a certain probability of occurring.

Current Value

It is the value of real assets that changes from year to year depending on current market conditions and supply-demand balances, and is also called market value.

Re-export

It refers to the sale of goods that have been purchased and are of foreign or Turkish origin and have been exported, whether transferred for processing or not, to another country in transit or directly, without being subject to the import and export regime provisions.

Rebate Schemes/Systems

Rebate systems can emerge in very different forms under the dynamic conditions of commercial life, and vary in their structures, workings and effects. If the earning of the rebate in any rebate system is made conditional on the purchase of a single product, such rebates are accepted as 'single-product rebates'. However, if the purchase condition in the rebate system covers multiple products or markets, the said rebates are 'bundle rebates'

Reciprocity Agreement

A type of bilateral (or multilateral) agreement made among undertakings in order to provide one another favorable conditions, or to buy from and sell to one another mutually. In reciprocity agreements, an undertaking can be obliged to purchase from the undertaking that purchases from it. Thanks to this agreement, the undertaking can obtain a price reduction by making its purchases from its own customers, or can activate its idle capacity. These competitive effects that reciprocity agreements can create

Real exchange rate Real Exchange Rate

Or abbreviated as RER, the foreign exchange rate calculated taking into account the difference in the general price level between two countries.

Real effective exchange rate Real Effective Exchange Rate

It is the real effective foreign exchange rate calculated taking into account relative price changes between countries.

Real interest rateReal Interest Rate

It is interest rate adjusted for inflation; with the Fisher equation (1+real) = (1+nominal)/(1+inflation), approximately real ≈ nominal − inflation.

Real interest rate Real Interest Rate

It is the nominal interest adjusted for inflation. Technically, it is obtained by subtracting the inflation rate expected from the nominal interest rate.

Real GDPReal GDP

Or GDP in fixed prices, the national income method that measures the value of goods and services produced in a given period at the prices of a fixed base year.

Real business cycle theory Real business cycle theory

It is the school of thought that argues changes in real income in the short run stem from supply shocks and advocates government intervention in the economy with supply-oriented policies.

Real exports Real Exports

It is expressed by the export volume index. The export volume index measures the change in export volume provided that prices are constant. The export volume indices are calculated by TÜİK.

Real imports Real Imports

It is expressed by the import volume index. The import volume index measures the change in import volume provided that prices are constant. The import volume indices are calculated by TÜİK.

Real Sector Confidence Index - RKGE Real Sector Confidence Index – RSCI

It is an indicator that summarises the answers given to the different questions of the Survey of Economic Expectations in order to bring out the general impressions of sector representatives regarding the economic outlook. In order to reach the Real Sector Confidence Index

RediscountRediscount

Refers to the rediscounting — the exchanging for a consideration again (rediscount) — of the instruments that have already been discounted, in other words exchanged for a consideration (discount). It is an instrument used by central banks in monetary policy implementations, under the name rediscount window, by subjecting various bills to discounting (re-discount), on the condition that they determine their own conditions.

Rediscount creditDiscount Credit

It is the credit that the central bank provides to commercial banks against securities.

Rediscount rate Discount Rate

It is the interest rate applied by the central bank for rediscount loans.

Rejection of the Transactions

The non-implementation by the relevant financial institutions of the transactions sought to be carried out at the financial institutions. These are the transactions in which the financial institutions could not carry out the identification and verification of the identity of the customer or of the person finally benefiting from the transaction, could not determine to whom the ownership and control belong in legal persons and partnerships, and could not have information about the type and purpose of the transaction being carried out

Reliance on Intermediaries and

Third Parties: The leaving, under certain conditions, by the financial institutions of the fulfillment of the measures relating to the identification and verification of the customer's and the beneficiary's identity, the determination of to whom the ownership and control belong in legal persons and partnerships, and the obtaining of information about the type and purpose of the transaction sought to be carried out, in the customer due diligence process, to intermediary institutions or other third parties. In this case, all the responsibility relating to the identification and verification of the customer's identity falls on the third party

Repo Repurchase Agreement

It is generally used in cases where the liquidity tightness in the market is temporary, with the aim of increasing the banking system's liquidity for a temporary period. The central bank buys instruments with a commitment to resell them at a future date (at the price determined during the transaction) from the institutions authorized to conduct open market operations. The resale price is determined on the date the purchase transaction is made. The institution party to the transaction also undertakes to buy the instrument subject to the repo transaction at the transaction's maturity. From the central bank's perspective, the repo transaction expresses the temporary provision of liquidity to the market within the framework of open market operations, for the duration of the transaction's maturity, in return for the instruments subject to the repo.

Example: Securities sold under a repurchase agreement have been recorded as collateralized borrowing.(The securities sold under the repurchase agreement were recorded as a collateralised borrowing.)

English definition: an agreement to sell securities with an undertaking to repurchase them at a later date.

Reserves

According to the principle contributed to the industrial economics literature by E.A.G. Robinson for the first time in 1958, as the scale of a production unit increases, savings can be achieved in the proportion of the reserve capacity set aside to meet future demand. For example, the number of beds a large hospital leaves empty for sudden needs will be less than the number of beds left empty by two small hospitals half its size.

RecessionRecession

It is a period in which economic growth is negative or slow. It may also be defined as the presence of spare capacity in the economy, or as growth at a rate below the economy's long-term growth rate.

Restriction on Technology

The designing by the undertaking of the new technology, although it is possible to design it in another way, in a way that other undertakings cannot use, in order to give itself an advantage — in other words, creating a barrier to market entry. This situation can be unlawful if carried out by the undertaking holding a dominant position and the other conditions are also present.

Restrictions on Resale Price

In a vertical relationship, the setting by the seller of the buyer's fixed or minimum resale price is prohibited under Article 4 of the Competition Act. However, on the condition that it does not turn into fixed or minimum resale prices, it is possible for the supplier to determine the buyer's maximum resale price or to recommend a resale price to the buyer. So that the maximum or recommended resale prices notified to the buyer do not turn into minimum or fixed prices, it is necessary that the said prices be published as maximum or recommended in the published price lists

Revaluation Revaluation

It expresses the increase in the value of the national currency against foreign currencies. For example, the rise of the Turkish lira against the US dollar from 1.3 TL to 1.2 TL (a depreciation of the US dollar) means that the TL gains 0.1 TL of value.

Example: The revaluation of the property increased its carrying amount and recognised the surplus in other comprehensive income.(A revaluation of the property increased its carrying amount and recognised the surplus in other comprehensive income.)

English definition: the remeasurement of a non-current asset up to its fair value, with the increase booked in other comprehensive income.

ReserveReserve

It is the total of the cash in banks' vaults and their accounts at the central bank.

Example: The revaluation reserve is presented within equity.( The revaluation reserve is presented within equity.

English definition: an equity component that is set aside for a purpose.

Reserve Option Coefficient - ROK Reserve Option Coefficient – ROC

They are the coefficients that determine the foreign exchange or gold equivalent that may be established per one unit of Turkish lira of required reserves under the Reserve Option Mechanism.

Reserve Option Mechanism - ROM Reserve Option Mechanism – ROM

It is a practice that allows banks and other financial institutions to maintain a certain percentage of their Turkish lira reserve requirements in foreign currency and gold.

Reserve option ratio Reserve Option Ratio

It is the ratio showing what percentage of banks' Turkish lira required reserves can be established in foreign exchange or gold under the Reserve Option Mechanism.

Reserve CurrencyHard Currency

Refers to the assets in foreign currency and gold held in the portfolios of central banks and international financial institutions. Accordingly, for a payment instrument to carry the property of reserve currency, its value against other currencies must be stable, it must belong to a country with a large share in world trade, and it must be readily bought and sold in the foreign exchange markets.

Reserve currencyReserve Currency

It is the currency unit held as an official reserve by central banks because it is widely used in international transactions.

Reserve assets Reserve Assets

It is the main heading in the balance of payments that shows the central bank's official foreign exchange reserves.

Rights of the Complainant

They are rights such as the right to lodge a complaint within the regulatory framework, the right to go to court against the decisions of the Board, the right to receive a reasoned decision, the right to be informed, the right to request that the name be kept confidential and the right to attend an oral defence hearing.

Risk Risk

It is the extent to which the actual outcome of a future event may differ from the expected outcome.

Example: The entity discloses the nature and extent of the risks arising from financial instruments.(The entity discloses the nature and extent of the risks arising from financial instruments.)

English definition: the possibility that actual outcomes will deviate from the expected ones, including financial risks like credit, liquidity and market risk.

Risk poolRisk pool

It is a method of risk distribution in which more than one insurance company joins together and shares a certain risk.

Risk appetite Risk Appetite

It is the desire to buy, which increases as positive expectations rise in financial markets and decreases as negative expectations grow.

Risk PremiumRisk Premium

It indicates the extra return that a risky asset or holding provides to the investor compared with a completely risk-free asset or holding. The risk premium of an asset or holding can also be regarded as an indicator of the compensation obtained compared with a completely risk-free investment instrument.

Risk loverrisk-taker

It is a person who prefers the more risky of two options with the same expected value.

Risk-Return Risk-Return

Refers to the relationship between the return expected to be obtained from an investment made in any asset or instrument and the risk assumed with the investment made in the said asset or instrument. In principle, there is a positive relationship between the risk assumed and the expected return. Hence, particularly in the financial markets, as the risk assumed increases, the return is expected to increase, and as the risk assumed decreases, the return is expected to decrease. Risk is generally expressed by the standard deviation of the investment's past returns (historical volatility). The expected return from the investment, on the other hand, is the average of the past returns. The risk-return curve formed by the maximum returns that can be obtained in return for a certain risk is called the 'efficient frontier', and its use is particularly for comparison and benchmark purposes.

Value at Risk Value at Risk – VaR

The statistical method used to measure the riskiness of the financial assets in a company or an investment portfolio. The method points to the highest loss level that can occur in the value of a portfolio, within a certain confidence interval, over a predetermined period. Risk managers aim, with the VaR method, to measure and control the risk assumed.

Example: Value at risk estimates potential losses over a specific period.( Value-at-risk estimates potential losses over a given period.)

English definition: A statistical measure of potential loss.

Risk neutral risk-neutral

It is a person who is indifferent between two options with the same expected value.

Value at risk Value at Risk – VaR

It shows the maximum loss that the portfolio value could suffer at a given confidence level and horizon; VaR = z·σ·Portfolio value. For multi-day horizons the scaling factor is the square root (10 days = √10 times, NOT 10 times).

Risk-averse risk-averse

It is a person who prefers the less risky of two options with the same expected value.

Risk-averse (Risk Averse), Risk Taker/Lover (Risk Lover), Risk Neutral Risk-Neutral

The concepts used to express an investor's preferences in the face of risk. While a risk-averse investor settles for a low but predictable return instead of an uncertain high return in the future, a risk-loving investor tends to take high risk even though the expected returns are equal. In contrast, a risk-neutral investor is the investor who makes no choice between risk and return.

Hedging Hedging

The securing of the investor's investment against possible adverse price movements, by using the instruments in the financial markets effectively and correctly, with the aim of protection against investment risk. With the aim of reducing the risks that may be encountered to a certain value, the investor can, by taking a long position with the help of futures contracts, purchase a futures contract with the expectation that prices will rise in the future, or, by taking a short position, sell a futures contract with the expectation that prices will fall in the future.

Example: The company applied hedge accounting to its interest rate swaps.(The company applied hedge accounting to its interest rate swaps.)

English definition: designating a derivative to offset changes affecting fair value or cash flows.

License

The document given by the municipalities, provincial special administrations, organized industrial zones or the Ministry of Industry and Technology, as a result of the conditions sought in the workplaces specified in the regulation according to the activity being met. Redemption: the transaction of eliminating the debt by the debtor through payments made at once or in certain periods. Import Substitution: the domestic production of products previously imported.

Fixed capital consumption consumption of fixed capital

Pure oligopoly pure oligopoly

Ownership effect Endowment effect

Counterfeit Banknote Counterfeit banknote

Custody Service Custodian service

They are services relating to capital market instruments that have been deposited or delivered in book entry or physically for the purpose of carrying out capital market activities, or by virtue of being a custodian, or for administration, or as collateral, or under any other name whatsoever.

Samurai bonds

It refers to security issues made by foreigners in Japan and denominated in Japanese yen. They are mostly issues with a maturity of 5 years and above. The first issue was made in 1970 by the Asian Development Bank.

Industrial cluster Industrial clustering

It is the more efficient operation of related firms that use similar infrastructure and production factors by clustering in a certain geographic region.

Chamber of Industry

They are professional organisations established by law that have legal personality, aimed at ensuring solidarity among industrialists, working to solve their common problems and protecting their common interests.

Chamber of Industry

A document given to the industrial enterprises evaluated within the scope of Article 1 of the Industrial Registry Law No. 6948, and to the enterprises making continuous and serial repairs and the large construction sites such as power plants producing electricity or other energy and shipbuilding, which must be obtained within 2 (two) months from the date on which the enterprise began its production activity. The said document is drawn up by the Provincial Directorates of Industry and Technology.

Industrial Production Index - SÜE Industry Production Index – IPI

An index calculated monthly in order to measure the short-term positive or negative effects of the developments occurring in the economy and of the economic policies implemented. According to the NACE Rev.2 classification, it covers the sectors of mining and quarrying, manufacturing, and the production and distribution of electricity, gas, steam and air conditioning.

Call option Put option

It expresses the right to sell. It gives the party holding the option contract the right to sell the instrument underlying the option under the conditions stated in the option, but brings no obligation. Depending on market conditions, the party holding the option may not exercise the option. The exercise of the option is expressed as being 'exercised'. The party selling the option, on the other hand, is obliged to fulfill the terms of the contract if the buying party wants to exercise the option.

Example: The non-controlling interest held a put option that could require the group to buy its shares.(The non-controlling interest held a put option that could require the group to buy its shares.)

English definition: a contract that grants its holder the right — with no compulsion — to sell an asset at a previously agreed price.

Purchasing power parity Purchasing power parity

The exchange rate that equalizes the prices of similar goods and services subject to trade in a certain basket across different countries. Since, in calculating purchasing power parity, only the goods and services subject to trade are taken into consideration, such a calculation can fall short of measuring the true value of the rate of exchange between the two countries' currencies.

Purchasing Managers Index Purchasing Managers’ Index – PMI

It is a diffusion index obtained from the answers given by the purchasing managers of industrial companies to questions about production, orders, employment, suppliers' delivery times and the development of input stocks.

Sale Ask, Offer

It is the price at which market participants are willing to sell the values traded in the market (such as foreign exchange and securities).

Example: Revenue from sales is accounted for when control is transferred.( Revenue from the sale is recognised when control transfers.)

English definition: Transferring an asset in exchange for consideration.

Search Warrant Authorization

( A permit issued by a judicial authority (judge) for law enforcement to conduct a search. SECI (Southeast European Cooperation Initiative) is the Southeast European Cooperation Initiative established pursuant to the "Agreement on Cooperation and Combat Transborder Crime" signed in Bucharest on May 26, 1999. The purpose of the initiative is to assist each other in the prevention, detection, investigation and prosecution of transborder crimes among the participating countries. Its center is in Bucharest.

Second Best

The level closest to the conditions of full competition. One of the most important justifications of competition policies is the thought that the fully competitive market leads societies to the highest welfare level, the 'first best'. However, it is known that full competition is a utopian model and that, in practice, it will not be possible to satisfy the conditions of full competition. For this reason, the aim of competition policies is to minimize the departures from the rules of full competition so that social welfare rises to the highest possible level — that is, the 'second best'

Sectoral Foreign Trade Company

They are companies established to enable enterprises in the same production area to come together under one organisation for export purposes and gain access to foreign markets.

Selective Pricing

It is the selective undercutting of prices among customers or products, independent of costs, in order to make it harder for dominant firms to compete.

Seigniorage - Sovereign Right Seigniorage

The real revenue the institution holding the authority to print money obtains by printing money by virtue of this authority. It is expressed as the ratio of the change in the money stock to the general price level. In the case where inflation stems only from the increase in the quantity of money, seigniorage and the inflation tax equal one another.

Freely Floating Exchange Rate Regime Free-floating exchange rate regime

It refers to the system in which the currency is determined according to supply and demand conditions in the market. Central banks never intervene in the market or, very rarely, only intervene under exceptional conditions.

Free cash flow Free Cash Flow – FCF

It is the part of the cash obtained from operating activities that remains after capital expenditures are deducted; it is the input of DCF valuation.

Free market economy free-market economy

It is the economic system in which what is produced, how and for whom is decided by the interaction of buyers and sellers in the markets.

Free reserve Excess Reserve

They are the reserves a bank holds in addition to the required reserve amount.

Free investment fund Hedge Fund

Refers to a type of investment fund whose certain restrictions have been minimized and in which as flexible an investment strategy as possible can be followed. The fundamental difference of hedge funds from ordinary investment funds is the possibilities of short selling and of borrowing (being able to invest with borrowed resources). Furthermore, thanks to hedge funds, the investor can take short and long positions, apply arbitrage strategies, at the same time both buy and sell a security priced below its value, invest in options or bonds, and invest in any kind of market carrying the possibility of obtaining high returns under low risk.

Capital supply Supply of Capital

Or abbreviated as SK, the total amount of capital that all units in the economy want to lease out depending on the interest rate in a given period.

Capital account Capital Account

It contains the flows arising through the acquisition and disposal of non-produced, non-financial assets (intangible assets such as a piece of land, and intangible assets such as franchises, trademarks and leases — transferable contracts) and capital transfers (such as debt forgiveness and other capital transfers).

Capital flight Capital Flight

They are international capital flows occurring outside the knowledge and control of the state.

Capital Conservation Buffer Capital Conservation Buffer

It refers to the additional core capital amount that banks are expected to hold in order to prevent equity from falling below the regulatory capital adequacy requirements in the event of losses arising from deterioration in economic and financial indicators.

Capital market Capital Market

Capital market equilibrium Capital Market Equilibrium

Capital demand Demand for Capital

Rental rate of capital rate of return on capital

Or abbreviated as PK, the price paid for using one unit of capital in production.

Marginal revenue product of capital marginal product of capital

Or abbreviated as MRPK, the change in total revenue when the quantity of capital is increased by one unit.

Marginal product of capital Marginal Product of Capital

Or abbreviated as MPK, the change in the quantity of total product when the quantity of capital is increased by one unit.

Average product of capital Average Product of Capital

Or abbreviated as APK, the quantity of product produced per unit of capital in a given period.

Sharing Economy

The sharing economy is an economic system based on the foundation of a good or service being shared by its owner, for a fee or free of charge, over an online platform. The sharing economy refers to the renting or sharing of goods rather than their purchase. In the sharing economy, the owner of a good, such as a house, a car or a bicycle, rents out a good it is not using to another person for a temporary period. The online platforms on which the sharing transaction is carried out depend on people being able to trust one another

Sharpe ratio Sharpe Ratio

It is the ratio of the portfolio surplus return to its standard deviation: (Rp − Rf) / σp. It measures premium per unit of total risk; beta is not used.

Insurance Insurance

It is a service that provides financial protection against various risks.

Insurance market Insurance Market

It is the physical or virtual environment where insurance policies are bought and sold and in the course of which insurance premium rates are determined.

Insurance policy Insurance Policy

It is a contract that provides a certain payment guarantee when predetermined losses and damages occur.

Insurance premium insurance premium

It is the amount paid in return for an insurance policy.

Discounts on Single Units

If the rebate systems applied by undertakings are made conditional on the buyer's fulfilling the purchase condition relating to a single product/market, such rebates are called 'single-product rebates'. For example, a tire shop's earning a premium in return for making a certain amount of sales annually is a kind of single-product rebate.

Systemic risk systemic risk

It is the risk of payment disruptions arising across the financial system due to adverse economic developments.

Systematic risk Systematic risk

It is a risk that affects the whole market and cannot be eliminated by diversification (interest rate, inflation, currency shocks); beta measures this risk.

Systemic Risk Systemic Risk

The interruption of financial services, in a part of or the whole financial system, in a way that can also lead to serious adverse consequences on economic activity, is called systemic risk. Whether any financial institution's financial insufficiency is a source of systemic risk depends on the effect stemming from this insufficiency being able to spread to the whole of the financial system and hence to the economy in general.

Last-resort debt provider Lender of last resort

It is an institution that is ready to lend even when nobody is borrowing.

Result

The measurable, concrete situations expected to form within 1-3 years following the completion of the project, obtained in line with the specific aims determined at the outset. Social Enterprise: the type of venture that aims to make profit but uses the profit it obtains to produce benefit for society. Social Innovation: the term for the innovative works used in addressing the problems and needs arising in societies.

Example: The result for the year was reported after tax.( The result for the year was reported after tax.)

Social security contributions Social security contributions

It is the portion of labour income paid to social security institutions.

Social Security Institutions Budget Social Security Institutions Budget

It is the budget of public administrations such as the Social Security Institution and the Turkish Employment Agency that provide social security services.

Social Capital

It is the concept expressing the bonds between individuals in social life such as unity, togetherness, solidarity, mutual aid, love, respect, trust and morality.

Special and Exclusive Rights

Special rights are the rights given by the state, through any legal, regulatory or administrative way, to a limited number of undertakings, to be within a certain geographical area. An exclusive right, on the other hand, is the right arising from the state's establishing a monopoly, through any legal instrument, in favor of a single undertaking (public or private) for the conduct of a certain economic activity in a certain geographical area for a certain period.

Speculative motive Speculative motive

It is the demand for money arising from the expectation of profiting from future price movements in financial or non-financial assets.

Spot Market Spot Market

It is the market in which the Bid or sale of a product is carried out at a price determined on the trade date at the latest two business days later.

Spot value date Spot Date

The realization, within two business days following the day on which the transaction is made, of the payments connected with a transaction carried out in the financial markets. If a transaction made with spot value date, for example, took place on Monday, the payments are made on Wednesday. In other words, the spot value date expresses the date on which the money will be ready for use. The difference between the transaction date and the date of use stems from operations such as the preparation of messages, the making of controls and the provision of funds requiring a certain time.

Stackelberg model Stackelberg model

It is a duopoly model in which one of two identical firms producing homogeneous products makes its output choice aware of the mutual dependence, while the other is not aware of it.

StagflationStagflation

It refers to the situation occurring when inflation rises in an environment in which output has fallen or at least has not increased.

Standard deviationStandard deviation
